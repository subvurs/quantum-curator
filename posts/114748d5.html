IBM, Mitsubishi Chemical, and Keio Train Generative Quantum Eigensolvers on 70-Qubit Hardware

Source: Quantum Computing Report November 12, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article shows that a hybrid quantum‑classical workflow—leveraging an autoregressive transformer to train generative quantum eigensolvers directly on IBM’s 70‑qubit processor—can dramatically cut gate counts and circuit depth when modeling strongly correlated organotin photoresist molecules. By demonstrating chemistry‑relevant simulations with far fewer resources, the work builds on recent advances in variational algorithms and quantum‑machine‑learning pipelines, moving them from theory toward practical, near‑term hardware use. The collaboration between IBM, Mitsubishi Chemical, Keio University and NVIDIA also signals growing industry interest in applying quantum methods to complex materials design, though scaling beyond 70 qubits will still require further error mitigation and hardware improvements.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

IBM Quantum, Mitsubishi Chemical, Keio University, and NVIDIA have successfully demonstrated a quantum hardware training framework for generative quantum eigensolvers on a 70-qubit processor. This framework, using an autoregressive transformer, was used to simulate complex organotin photoresist molecules with significantly reduced gate counts and circuit depths compared to conventional methods. The research highlights a new approach to overcoming the challenges of simulating strongly correlated electrons in organometallic clusters using quantum computing.
