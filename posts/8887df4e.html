Dissipation as a Resource for Entanglement via High-Spin Mediator Purification

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics November 13, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article shows that engineered dissipation can actively purify a thermally mixed high‑spin impurity and turn it into a powerful entangling bus, achieving a negativity of 0.64 at 12 K—far above the temperatures where most solid‑state spin qubits retain coherence. By demonstrating a non‑monotonic optimum pump rate, the work links recent advances in optical pumping of magnetic atoms to practical hardware designs that exploit rather than suppress decoherence channels. The result suggests a new route to robust two‑qubit gates in impurity‑based platforms, though scaling will require careful management of the same dissipative coupling that delivers the entanglement.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

We study entanglement generation between two spin qubits mediated by a single, thermally mixed, high-spin ($S=5/2$) magnetic impurity, using the Lindblad master equation to compare coherent and dissipative control of the mediator. We show that no coherent drive, however strong or resonant, can purify the mediator beyond its thermal value --- a direct consequence of unitary evolution exactly preserving state purity --- while a dissipative channel purifies it up to the limit set by the optical pumping cycle itself, $\mathrm{Tr}[ρ_M^2]\approx0.8$. Modeling this channel with rates fixed by the measured optical pumping of a single Mn spin, we find that the entanglement it enables is not monotonic in the pump strength: it peaks at a finite rate and then decreases, since the same channel leaks decoherence to the qubits through the coupling that mediates their entanglement. At the experimental pumping rate, a single $80$ns pulse purifies the mediator, the resulting polarization persists for a timescale three orders of magnitude longer than the entangling dynamics, and the first-recurrence negativity rises from $0.18$ to $0.64$ at $12$K, remaining above $0.6$ up to $30$K. These results establish dissipation, not as noise to be suppressed, but as the very resource that makes robust entanglement generation possible.
