Quantum-Assisted Generative Anomaly Detection: A Dimension-Controlled Study of Latent Refinement

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics November 10, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article demonstrates one of the first systematic comparisons between a classical Gaussian latent space and a truly quantum‑generated distribution from a photonic processor in a generative malware detector, showing that the quantum prior yields a higher effective rank and richer internal geometry. By integrating the ORCA PT‑2 output into SAGEGAN and then refining the frozen representation with greedy and reinforcement‑learning techniques, the authors reveal that quantum‑derived latents respond more strongly to post‑training refinement—a hint that quantum hardware could provide more adaptable feature spaces for security‑focused generative models. Although the final detection accuracy matches refined Gaussian baselines, the work highlights a concrete pathway for leveraging near‑term photonic QPUs to shape latent structures beyond what classical sampling easily offers, underscoring both promise and the need for larger‑scale studies.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

Anomaly-based malware detectors learn what benign files look like and flag deviations. Generative approaches to this problem can make use of a "latent" or "prior" data representation, which is often chosen to be a Gaussian distribution. We ask whether the choice of prior distribution matters, and whether one produced by quantum hardware behaves differently from a classical one. Building on SAGEGAN, a style-based detector trained only on benign data, we compare three latent distributions: the original 512-dimensional Gaussian, a 48-dimensional Gaussian, and a 48-dimensional distribution sampled from an ORCA Computing PT-2 photonic quantum processor. We then refine each trained model's frozen representation using greedy and reinforcement-learning methods. Across ten random seeds, the quantum-derived model shows richer internal structure with better effective rank and latent similarity. It also benefits most from refinement. Though final detection accuracy is similar to that of the refined Gaussian latent, these findings show differences in latent geometry and refinement behavior between the trained configurations.
