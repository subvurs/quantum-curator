Thermal state preparation with shallow quantum circuits

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics November 24, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article shows that low‑temperature thermal states can be prepared with circuits an order of magnitude shallower than those produced by conventional tensor‑network compilation, a breakthrough for noisy intermediate‑scale quantum hardware. By using the PP‑VarQITE method to compute all gate angles classically and then encoding temperature directly into a fixed shallow architecture, the authors demonstrate accurate energy and correlation results on up to 100 qubits—including a 48‑site frustrated chain and a 7×7 Ising lattice on Quantinuum’s Helios processor. The work opens a practical pathway for studying finite‑temperature physics and phase transitions on near‑term devices that were previously limited by circuit depth constraints.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

Preparing thermal states on noisy quantum hardware requires circuits that remain shallow at low temperatures. Even when accurate classical state representations are available, compiling them into suitable circuits can be costly. We introduce PP-VarQITE, combining Pauli propagation with variational quantum imaginary-time evolution to determine circuit parameters entirely classically, with target temperatures encoded in the angles of a fixed circuit architecture. In numerical studies of Ising and frustrated Heisenberg models, we recover low-temperature energies and correlations using up to 100 qubits. Across the frustrated chain and two-dimensional lattice, our circuits are approximately two to ten times shallower than the lowest-depth circuits from sequential tensor-network compilation. These shallow circuits enable us to prepare a 48-site frustrated chain and a $7\times7$ Ising lattice using 96 and 98 qubits on the Quantinuum Helios trapped-ion quantum processor. We reproduce the development of dimer order and lattice-wide correlations from high to low temperatures, with the lattice circuits requiring at most 42 two-qubit layers. Through classical circuit design, we demonstrate low-temperature state preparation at circuit sizes beyond the practical reach of statevector simulation.
