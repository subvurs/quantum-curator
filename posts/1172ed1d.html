CSIC Inaugurates Spain’s Quantum Nanofabrication Area at IMB-CNM in Barcelona

Source: Quantum Computing Report September 16, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article matters because Spain’s new €6 million quantum nanofabrication area gives Europe a dedicated hub for turning quantum designs into physical chips, filling a gap that has limited rapid prototyping of QPUs and QKD components on the continent. It builds on the momentum of the EU Quantum Flagship and recent national chip‑program investments, positioning CSIC’s QuARC network alongside other emerging foundries in the US and Asia to accelerate hardware convergence across computing, sensing and cryptography. While the facility promises faster iteration cycles for research groups and industry partners, scaling those prototypes into manufacturable volumes will still require broader ecosystem support and supply‑chain coordination.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

Spain's CSIC has opened a Quantum Nanofabrication Area at IMB-CNM in Barcelona, a €6 million investment funded by Spain's PERTE Chip program and NextGenerationEU. This facility, equipped with advanced lithography and microscopy systems, is designed for prototyping quantum hardware like QPUs and QKD components. It's part of CSIC's QuARC-CSIC initiative, consolidating 36 research groups to advance quantum technologies across various pillars, from computing to sensing.
