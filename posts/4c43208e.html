Quantum-Classical Framework for the Joint Optimization of Bidirectional EV Charging and Trip Assignment

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics October 26, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article shows how a hybrid quantum‑classical Benders decomposition can tackle the notoriously hard joint scheduling of bidirectional EV charging and trip assignment, introducing logic‑based conflict cuts that sidestep the qubit overhead normally required for feasibility constraints. By sparsifying the state‑of‑charge formulation and demonstrating MILP‑optimal results on benchmark problems—and even running a tiny instance on IBM’s quantum processor—the work bridges recent advances in quantum optimization (e.g., QAOA and annealing) with real‑world energy‑mobility challenges. While still at proof‑of‑concept scale, the approach hints that future quantum accelerators could become practical tools for managing distributed energy assets in smart‑grid fleets.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

The integration of bidirectional (V2G) charging allows electric vehicle (EV) fleets to operate as distributed energy assets. However, the joint optimization of charging schedules and trip assignments is NP-hard. Moreover, bidirectional power flows create non-monotonic state-of-charge (SoC) dynamics that weaken standard dual-based Benders cuts. We propose a hybrid quantum-classical Benders decomposition framework. To overcome the qubit overhead associated with encoding feasibility cuts, we introduce logic-based conflict cuts, which require near-zero auxiliary qubits. Furthermore, we develop a sparse recursive SoC formulation that reduces the constraint-matrix density by a factor of T/3 in the horizon length T. We demonstrate that our framework recovers MILP-optimal solutions on benchmark instances with a low auxiliary-qubit budget, and, as a minimal proof of concept, we execute a small instance end-to-end on IBM quantum hardware.
