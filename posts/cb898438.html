Observing Enhanced Entanglement in an Ising Quantum Register Beyond Weak Measurement

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics November 13, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article shows that by tailoring a periodic‑measurement pattern one can dramatically boost the entanglement that survives after a projective readout of an Ising‑coupled register—something previously thought to be exponentially unlikely in noisy devices. The protocol bridges recent work on measurement‑induced phase transitions and matrix‑product‑state simulations, offering a concrete recipe for experimentally accessing highly entangled post‑measurement states without excessive overhead. If the scaling ansatz holds for larger processors, it could become a practical tool for enhancing quantum sensing and error‑mitigation schemes that rely on strong multipartite correlations.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

Measurements often yield a disentangled quantum state which is the most probable measurement outcome while the measurement outcomes with enhanced entanglement could be exponentially less probable or absent. Higher probability in measurement outcomes with enhanced entanglement is virtue in experimental observation of enhance entanglement in post-selected or Born-rule measurements with less resources in noisy quantum devices. We develop a periodic measurement sampling(PMS) protocol in the framework of uniform matrix product states. Using PMS, we identify a peak enhancement of entanglement in a uniform post-measurement state whose probabilistic outcome can be maximized by optimizing a measurement super-cell. A measurement super-cell is the smallest repeating building block of qubits defined by the measurement density, external field strength and exchange interactions between quantum register and ancilla qubits. The above prediction based on a classical description of the many-body quantum state is demonstrated in a quantum computing experiment in a loop of quantum register qubits with Ising interactions in the presence of transverse magnetic field. Further, we show the scalability of predictions of the periodic measurement sampling protocol in the thermodynamic limit to finite size systems. Especially, we present a rescaling ansatz which would enable predicting the possibility of observing enhanced entanglement in finite size systems.
