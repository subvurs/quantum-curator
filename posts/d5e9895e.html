Improved Error Correction with Leakage Reduction Units Built into Qubit Measurement in a Superconducting Quantum Processor

Source: PRX Quantum September 21, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article demonstrates a hardware‑level solution to one of the most stubborn error sources in superconducting qubits—leakage out of the computational subspace—by embedding leakage‑reduction units directly into the measurement step with simultaneous microwave drives on the transmon and its readout resonator. The approach not only pulls leaked population back while delivering a high‑fidelity readout, but it also dovetails with recent efforts to streamline surface‑code error correction and IBM’s roadmap toward logical qubits by reducing the overhead needed for repeated QEC cycles. If the method scales without introducing extra crosstalk, it could bring superconducting processors closer to the fault‑tolerance threshold, though larger‑scale integration remains an open challenge.

— Mark Eatherly

Author(s): Yuejie Xin, Sean L. M. van der Meer, Marc Serra-Peralta, Tim H. F. Vroomans, Matvey Finkel, Hendrik M. Veen, Marc W. Beekman, and Leonardo DiCarlo Application of simultaneous microwave pulses to a transmon and readout resonator can return the transmon to the computational subspace and provide simultaneous readout to improve quantum error correction.
