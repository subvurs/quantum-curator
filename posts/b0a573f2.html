Quantum-Chaos-Assisted Measure Synchronization in a Double-Well Ultracold Bose Gas

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics November 20, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article demonstrates that quantum chaotic dynamics can dramatically accelerate measure synchronization in a double‑well Bose‑Einstein condensate, revealing a direct link between chaos, rapid synchronization and high entanglement entropy. The finding suggests a new route for fast state preparation or stabilization in analog quantum simulators, complementing recent advances in Floquet engineering and dissipative control of many‑body systems. Nonetheless, the results are derived from an idealized two‑mode Josephson model, so extending chaos‑assisted synchronization to larger, noisy platforms will require additional experimental and theoretical work.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

This paper investigates measure synchronization (MS) in a two-component ultracold atomic system confined in a double-well potential, which realizes a quantum many-body bosonic Josephson junction. Through spectral-statistics analysis, we identify the quantum chaotic parameter regime and confirm that the system achieves MS within this regime with weak dependence on the initial energy imbalance. We further show that chaotic dynamics not only significantly shortens the time scale for establishing synchronization but is also accompanied by entanglement entropy approaching the random-state prediction. These results reveal close connections among quantum chaos, MS, and quantum entanglement in conservative quantum many-body systems and may motivate further studies on the underlying mechanisms of chaos-assisted synchronization.
