Prospects and limitations of adaptive variational quantum algorithms for lattice field theories: a case study in 2+1 dimensions

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics November 24, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article shows that the popular ADAPT‑VQE approach, which has already enabled proof‑of‑principle vacuum preparation in one‑dimensional gauge models, runs into two fundamental roadblocks when pushed to realistic 2+1‑D lattice field theories: real‑valued operator pools become blind to gradient information and the algorithm can mistakenly lock onto excited states. By diagnosing these issues with concrete examples—including free Wilson fermions—and demonstrating that adding charge‑conjugation symmetry to the pool (or mixing pools with tailored initial states) restores reliable ground‑state convergence, the work provides the first practical recipe for scaling variational quantum simulations beyond 1+1 dimensions. These insights are crucial for anyone aiming to use near‑term quantum hardware to tackle higher‑dimensional lattice gauge problems, as they clarify both the limits of current methods and concrete steps to overcome them.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

The variational quantum algorithm ADAPT-VQE has previously been used to prepare the vacuum of lattice gauge theories in $1+1D$. In this paper, we study whether it extends to higher dimensions. We identify two general limitations of ADAPT-VQE. First, if the qubit Hamiltonian and the initial state are real, i.e., invariant under qubit-level time-reversal, all real-valued pool operators have vanishing gradients and are never selected. Second, the algorithm can converge to excited eigenstates. We illustrate both limitations, also providing examples from literature, and present a first application of ADAPT-VQE to a lattice field theory in $2+1D$, namely free Wilson fermions, where both obstacles occur. Adding charge conjugation symmetry to the operator pool improves the results, and combining different pools with suitable initial states recovers the ground and first excited states over the full mass range. These findings give practical guidelines for applying ADAPT-VQE to lattice field theories beyond $1+1D$.
