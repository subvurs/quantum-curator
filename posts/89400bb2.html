Algorithmiq and IBM Quantum Execute 40-Qubit Discrete-Time Quantum Walks on Biological Networks

Source: Quantum Computing Report November 28, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article demonstrates the first scalable implementation of a 40‑qubit discrete‑time quantum walk on an irregular, biologically relevant graph, showing that sparse single‑excitation encodings can tame circuit depth even on near‑term hardware. By beating dense‑encoding approaches and delivering a tangible biomedical insight—prioritising an asthma risk gene more accurately than classical heuristics—it bridges algorithmic theory with a real‑world use case. The work builds on recent advances in quantum walk algorithms and IBM’s roadmap for larger, error‑mitigated processors, suggesting that graph‑based quantum analytics could become a practical tool as hardware improves. Readers should note, however, that the result still relies on extensive post‑processing and error mitigation, so broader applicability will depend on continued gains in qubit fidelity and scaling.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

Algorithmiq and IBM Quantum have successfully executed 40-qubit discrete-time quantum walks (DTQWs) on irregular protein-protein interaction networks, overcoming limitations of conventional dense encodings. They used a sparse single-excitation encoding and novel gate operations to significantly reduce circuit depth. This advancement was applied to a biomedical case study, where quantum walks helped prioritize an asthma risk gene more effectively than classical methods.
