Residual Entanglement vs. Residual Coherence: A Wave Packet Approach in Neutrino Oscillation with Matter

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics November 12, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article shows that treating neutrinos as wave packets rather than ideal plane waves fundamentally alters the predicted quantum‑correlation signatures—especially residual l₁‑norm coherence—along DUNE’s long baseline and beyond, highlighting how decoherence and matter effects can wash out oscillatory behavior. By expressing concurrence and three‑π entanglement directly in terms of measurable transition probabilities, the authors bridge high‑energy neutrino physics with quantum‑information tools, offering a new way to assess sensitivity to CP‑violating phases at very long distances. The work therefore provides theorists and experimentalists with realistic benchmarks for how coherence loss may limit future precision measurements, while also suggesting that residual coherence could become a novel observable in next‑generation neutrino experiments.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

In this work, we investigate quantum correlations in three-flavor neutrino oscillations beyond the standard plane-wave (PW) description by employing a wave-packet formalism in the presence of constant matter density. We study three quantum correlation measures, namely the residual $l_1$-norm coherence, Concurrence Fill, and Three-$π$ entanglement, and express them in terms of transition probabilities for an initial muon-flavor state. The analysis is performed for the DUNE setup and extended beyond its baseline to study the interplay between wave-packet decoherence and matter effects over long distances. For the DUNE baseline, finite wave-packet widths suppress and smooth the oscillations relative to the PW case, with smaller widths causing stronger damping, while matter effects modify the amplitudes and positions of the oscillation features. Among the considered measures, residual $l_1$-norm coherence dominates over the entanglement measures. At very long propagation distances, wave-packet decoherence progressively washes out the oscillatory behavior, driving the oscillation probabilities and quantum correlation measures toward averaged values, with smaller wave-packet widths leading to earlier damping. Further, in the $L-δ_{\rm CP}$ plane, residual $l_1$-norm coherence exhibits weak $δ_{\rm CP}$ dependence, while Concurrence Fill and Three-$π$ entanglement show greater sensitivity at the DUNE baseline. Beyond a characteristic baseline in the extended propagation regime, all three measures approach constant values, independent of $L$ and $δ_{\rm CP}$.
