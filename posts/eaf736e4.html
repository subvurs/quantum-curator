Cryogenic Multiplexing Control Chip for Superconducting Quantum Processor

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics October 14, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article tackles one of the biggest engineering roadblocks to scaling superconducting processors: the sheer number of microwave control lines required at millikelvin temperatures. By demonstrating a cryogenic multiplexing control chip that can generate and route XY pulses for many qubits over a single cable while suppressing crosstalk, it builds on recent Cryo‑CMOS and integrated‑control efforts from IBM and Google and could dramatically shrink the wiring harness of future 1,000‑plus‑qubit machines. If the demonstrated frequency‑domain performance translates to low‑error operation in real devices, the approach promises a more compact, power‑efficient control stack essential for practical quantum computers. However, integration challenges such as thermal loading and on‑chip isolation will need careful engineering before large‑scale deployment.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

This paper proposes a scheme and design of cryogenic multiplexing control chip (MCC) which enables one microwave cable to control many superconducting qubits. Designed based on adjustable inductor, MCC is a non-reciprocal device which generates microwave signals between 4 and 8 GHz and constructs XY control circuit to operate qubits. The effects of filters, isolators, power splitters, qubits and load impedance on MCC are analyzed in the frequency-domain simulation. And in time-domain simulation, the response time, Gaussian pulses, single-sideband, amplitude and frequency compensation of MCC are examined. The MCC can be integrated with the superconducting qubits on the same package at 20 mK temperature stage, and output specific microwave pulses used to drive the superconducting qubits without crosstalks. The MCC can greatly reduce the number of microwave control lines and is beneficial to the development of large-scale superconducting quantum processor.
