Superconducting circuit links smaller photon groups into larger entangled states

Source: Phys.org Quantum October 09, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This work demonstrates a superconducting circuit that can fuse multiple small‑photon ensembles into a single, larger entangled state, offering a hardware‑level shortcut to scaling up photonic qubit registers without the overhead of sequential gates. By showing deterministic, high‑fidelity merging of photon groups, it complements recent advances in modular quantum processors and hybrid microwave‑optical interfaces that aim to stitch together disparate quantum modules. If the technique can be integrated into larger architectures, it could accelerate the construction of error‑corrected logical qubits and broaden the practical reach of photonic‑based quantum computing.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

Quantum computers, computer systems that leverage the laws of quantum mechanics, store and process information using qubits (i.e., quantum bits). In many quantum computers, qubits are linked via entanglement, a quantum mechanical effect that connects particles in such a way that their shared state cannot be described as separate, independent states.
