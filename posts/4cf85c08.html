LYNX - A trapped-ion quantum information processor

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics October 23, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article showcases LYNX, a trapped‑ion processor that pushes ion‑based hardware into the same performance tier as leading superconducting systems by delivering sub‑10⁻⁴ single‑qubit errors and two‑qubit errors below 3×10⁻³ across a 24‑qubit register. The all‑to‑all connectivity and a demonstrated quantum volume of 2¹⁵ signal that trapped ions can now support deep, highly entangled circuits at scales relevant for early error‑corrected algorithms. While the QV benchmark stops at 15 qubits, the reported gate fidelities suggest LYNX is poised to accelerate both quantum‑chemistry simulations and the development of fault‑tolerant logical qubits on ion platforms.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

We introduce LYNX, a quantum information processor based on trapped $^{40}$Ca$^+$ ions. It utilizes up to 31 qubits with all-to-all connectivity. We characterize the system performance using rigorous benchmarks, such as randomized benchmarking (RB), GHZ state fidelity estimates, and quantum volume (QV) tests. The LYNX processor shows average 1-qubit gate errors of $8(5)\times 10^{-5}$ tested via standard RB in a 24-qubit register. An average 2-qubit gate error in a 24-qubit register of $2.84(8)\times 10^{-3}$ is demonstrated using interleaved RB. Furthermore, the device passes QV tests using up to 15 qubits which corresponds to a quantum volume of QV$=2^{15}$.
