Localization of an Individual Charge Fluctuator in a Si/SiGe Spin Qubit Device

Source: arXiv Condensed Matter November 25, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article marks the first time a single charge‑noise two‑level fluctuator has been pinpointed inside a Si/SiGe spin‑qubit stack, showing that electrostatic triangulation combined with realistic simulations can locate a defect to within a few nanometres and even infer its dipole strength. By revealing that the culprit resides in the atomic‑layer‑deposited Al₂O₃ rather than the silicon channel, the work gives device engineers a concrete target for material improvements—a crucial step toward reducing charge noise and pushing gate fidelities past the fault‑tolerance threshold. The technique also opens a pathway to systematically map noisy interfaces across different qubit platforms, although its reliance on high‑resolution sensing dots may limit immediate scalability to large‑scale processors.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

Charge noise from two-level fluctuators (TLFs) limits gate fidelities in semiconductor spin qubits, yet locating individual fluctuators within a device remains challenging. Here, we demonstrate a spatial triangulation method that combines the gate dependence of a TLF's detuning with the chemical potential shifts it induces in a sensing dot. Applying this method to previously reported measurements from a gated Si/SiGe quantum dot device, we use electrostatic simulations to jointly constrain the fluctuator's three-dimensional position and the magnitude of its electric dipole moment. We localize the fluctuator to a region spanning 31 nm by 1 nm laterally and 4 nm vertically within the bulk of the atomic layer deposited aluminum oxide, identifying the material hosting this individual charge-noise source. The measurements are consistent with a vertically oriented TLF with a magnitude of 3 to 4 e$\mathring{A}$. This approach provides a route to determining which materials and interfaces host active charge fluctuators in semiconductor spin qubit devices.
