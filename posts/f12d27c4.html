$\mathsf{AC}^0 \not\subseteq \mathsf{BQAC}^0/\mathsf{qpoly}$ via Sublinear-Degree Pauli Approximation

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics November 25, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article establishes the first explicit separation between constant‑depth classical circuits ( AC⁰ ) and their quantum analogue with polynomial‑size advice ( BQAC⁰/qpoly ), showing that even with arbitrary quantum help a shallow QAC⁰ circuit can do no better than inverse‑polynomial guessing on a simple monotone CNF family. The proof hinges on a new sublinear‑degree Pauli approximation technique that tightly bounds the acceptance operator of any such circuit, and it immediately yields stretched‑exponential limits on shallow quantum algorithms for distinguishing state designs or probing NLTS Hamiltonians—key benchmarks in quantum complexity and error‑correction research. By clarifying exactly how much power constant‑depth quantum circuits can (and cannot) wield, the work sharpens our understanding of the frontier between feasible near‑term hardware and provably hard computational tasks.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

We prove $\mathsf{AC}^0 \not\subseteq \mathsf{BQAC}^0/\mathsf{qpoly}$ in the single-copy, single-output model via an explicit $\mathsf{P}$-uniform family of linear-size monotone depth-2 CNFs. Every $\mathsf{QAC}^0$ circuit with arbitrary quantum advice achieves only an inverse-polynomial worst-case advantage over random guessing on this family. To prove this, we approximate the acceptance operator of any such circuit in operator norm by an advice-independent Hermitian operator of sublinear Pauli degree on a designated register alone, with degree exponent independent of width. Product-projection localization and Pauli twirling produce an auxiliary-register spectral cutoff that folds into contractions during backward peeling. The same approximation yields two stretched-exponential bounds against $\mathsf{QAC}^0$ circuits on $m$-qubit states. First, given polynomially many copies and input-independent quantum advice, single-output circuits distinguish exact or exponentially approximate state $2$-designs from Haar-random states with advantage at most $e^{-m^α}$. Second, any state prepared from fewer than $k$ arbitrary input qubits and remaining $|0\rangle$ inputs has spectral weight at most $2e^{-m^α}$ in a constant-energy-density band of an Anshu--Breuckmann--Nirkhe NLTS Hamiltonian encoding $k$ logical qubits. Both bounds hold for every fixed $α\in(0,1)$.
