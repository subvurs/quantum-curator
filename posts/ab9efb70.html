Entangled logical qubits on a superconducting quantum processor

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics October 23, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article marks a concrete step toward fault‑tolerant quantum computing by showing that logical qubits encoded in surface codes can be created, entangled and teleported on a real superconducting processor with error rates below the 1 % threshold for each round of error correction. By combining component selection, fast reset to suppress leakage, and noise‑aware decoding, the IBM Phoenix team pushes distance‑5 surface‑code performance into a regime where logical operations begin to outpace raw physical errors—a milestone that aligns with recent advances in lattice‑surgery protocols and multi‑round stabilizer cycles reported by other groups. The demonstrated logical Bell fidelities above 93 % and teleportation of gates suggest that near‑term applications such as error‑corrected quantum sensing or small‑scale algorithmic primitives could soon be run on hardware that already incorporates the essential ingredients for scalable fault tolerance.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

We demonstrate high-fidelity logical qubit operations using \texttt{ibm\_phoenix}, a square-lattice array of 120 coupled superconducting qubits that are capable of fast reset. Using strategies to exclude underperforming components together with noise informed decoding, we routinely produce surface codes at distance-3 and distance-5 with logical error per round below 1\%. We demonstrate entanglement between two logical qubits, where logical Bell-state fidelities above 93\% are observed for entanglement created via lattice surgery. With three logical qubits and lattice surgery, we perform logical teleportation of a range of states and gates, observing fidelities in the range 78\% - 93\%. Using reset to reduce qubit leakage, we show that repeated rounds of stabilizer measurements continue to exhibit improvements in a stability experiment up to $\sim 2\cdot 10^{-7}$ over 170 measurement rounds.
