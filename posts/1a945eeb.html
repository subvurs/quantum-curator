Quantum Bernstein--Kolmogorov--Arnold approximation

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics October 30, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article shows that Quantum Neural Networks can achieve the same universal approximation power as classical networks by embedding the input into coordinate qubits and using a single outer unitary to encode any continuous multivariate function, effectively closing a long‑standing expressivity gap in QML theory. By translating the Kolmogorov–Arnold representation into a constructive circuit built from coherent‑state inputs and commuting inner observables, it provides a concrete blueprint for hardware‑friendly architectures that could reduce depth and gate count compared with existing variational designs. The result is primarily theoretical—its practical impact will depend on advances in coherent‑state preparation and error‑robust measurement—but it offers a compelling bridge between classical approximation theory and near‑term quantum machine‑learning implementations.

Summary

This paper addresses the expressivity and representation gap in Quantum Neural Networks by establishing a quantum analogue of the classical Kolmogorov--Arnold representation theorem. We propose a constructive approximation framework over fixed input coherent states, where the dependence of the function argument is embedded entirely in coordinate qubits, while the target function dictates a single outer unitary evolution. By applying a Sprecher-type representation to the rotation angle of the state, we demonstrate that the multidimensional operator factorises into \(2d+1\) commuting universal inner observables. We prove that the resulting measurement probability precisely reproduces the multidimensional Bernstein polynomial of the target function. For any continuous multivariate function, the joint quantum state converges uniformly to the target output register, linking classical superposition theory with probabilistic and coherent quantum circuits.
