Cornell Researchers Lower Tantalum Qubit Deposition Temperature to 200°C Using Krypton Gas Sputtering

Source: Quantum Computing Report August 19, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article matters because it demonstrates a practical route to fabricate high‑coherence tantalum qubits at only 200 °C by swapping argon for krypton in the sputtering step, eliminating the need for the >400 °C bake that has limited integration with standard CMOS back‑end processes. The result dovetails with recent advances that have shown tantalum’s superior loss properties, and now adds a scalable, low‑temperature manufacturing pathway that could accelerate heterogeneous QPU‑classical chip stacks. While the krypton process still requires careful control of film stress and uniformity, its compatibility with existing semiconductor lines makes it a significant step toward larger, more manufacturable quantum processors.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

A research team led by Cornell University Assistant Professor Valla Fatemi has developed a low-temperature fabrication process for tantalum-based superconducting qubits, resolving a major manufacturing bottleneck for superconducting quantum processing units (QPUs).
