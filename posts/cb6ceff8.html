Qunova Computing Achieves Chemical Accuracy at 80 Qubits on Iron-Sulfur Cluster Using IBM Quantum System Two and Fugaku

Source: Quantum Computing Report November 13, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article shows that a hybrid quantum‑classical workflow can finally reach chemical accuracy on an 80‑qubit model of an iron–sulfur cluster, a size previously thought out of reach for near‑term devices. By pairing IBM’s System Two hardware with the Fugaku supercomputer and their HI‑VQE algorithm, Qunova cut the classical overhead by roughly 300× while still matching state‑of‑the‑art benchmarks, highlighting how co‑design between quantum processors and high‑performance computers can unlock realistic chemistry simulations. The result points toward more practical studies of metalloenzymes and industrial catalysts, though scaling beyond this proof‑of‑concept will still require further error mitigation and hardware improvements.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

Qunova Computing achieved a breakthrough in quantum chemistry by simulating an 80-qubit iron-sulfur cluster with chemical accuracy using a hybrid approach combining IBM Quantum System Two and the Fugaku supercomputer. Their HI-VQE algorithm reduced classical computing costs by approximately 300x while matching classical benchmarks. This advancement has significant implications for understanding complex biological and industrial processes.
