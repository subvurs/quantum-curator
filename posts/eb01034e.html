Quantitative measurement of microwave power at millikelvin temperatures: Comparison between dispersively and directly coupled superconducting qubits

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics November 16, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article demonstrates that superconducting transmon qubits can serve as calibrated microwave‑power sensors at millikelvin temperatures, directly comparing the performance of qubits coupled straight to a feedline versus those read out dispersively. By spanning a dynamic range from –155 dBm up to –55 dBm, the dual‑coupling device provides a practical way to measure line attenuation and drive strength—key parameters for achieving high‑fidelity gates as quantum processors scale. The approach builds on recent work integrating quantum sensors into hardware stacks, offering a simpler cryogenic metrology tool while highlighting that dispersive readout carries larger uncertainty than direct coupling. Readers should care because accurate power calibration underpins error budgeting and reproducibility across quantum computing experiments.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

The Rabi frequency of a two-level system is directly proportional to the electric field amplitude being incident on this system. Taking advantage of this relationship, it is possible to use two-level systems as quantitative sensors of electric fields. Here, we apply this concept to superconducting qubits and use them for quantitative measurement of microwave power at millikelvin temperatures. Our superconducting circuit design includes three transmon qubits directly coupled to the central microwave feedline and three transmon qubits coupled dispersively via readout resonators to the feedline. We compare both coupling schemes to assess their reliability for microwave power sensing. Further, we describe the modeling behind each power-sensing method, including an uncertainty estimation based upon repeated measurements. By combining dispersively coupled and directly coupled qubits on the same device, we are able to explore different power ranges: from $-155\;$dBm to $-130\;$dBm for the directly coupled qubits and from $-95\;$dBm to $-55\;$dBm for the dispersively coupled qubits. When determining the microwave-line attenuation for a frequency of $6.0\;$GHz from room temperature to the millikelvin stage of the cryostat, we obtain best estimates and uncertainties for the directly coupled transmons of $(74.7\pm 0.2) \;$dB and for the dispersively coupled transmons of $(75.4\pm 6.8) \;$dB. The larger uncertainty of the dispersively coupled qubit measurements indicates that these qubits are less reliable for power sensing than the directly coupled qubits, most likely, due to the additional coupling resonator, whose transfer function is difficult to estimate accurately. Our results constitute the first step towards validation of qubit power sensing in dilution fridges, leading towards traceability for cryogenic microwave measurements.
