Exponential-SWAP gate between bosonic motional modes of trapped ions

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics October 16, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article demonstrates the first experimental realization of a continuously tunable exponential‑SWAP gate between two bosonic motional modes of trapped ions, providing a genuine hybrid interaction that can interpolate smoothly between doing nothing and swapping entire quantum states. By leveraging both spin‑independent and spin‑dependent beam‑splitter couplings, the work extends the toolbox for bosonic encoding schemes—such as cat or GKP qubits—that have so far been dominated by superconducting cavity platforms, opening new routes to error‑corrected logical operations and entanglement generation in ion traps. The ability to program the identity‑SWAP superposition on demand also promises more flexible quantum sensing protocols that exploit bosonic mode interference while retaining compatibility with existing trapped‑ion qubit architectures.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

The exponential-SWAP (eSWAP) gate generates a coherent superposition of identity and SWAP operations, with continuously tunable amplitudes. We experimentally implement this gate between two bosonic motional modes of trapped ions using spin-independent and spin-dependent beam splitter interactions. Measurements of selected slices of the joint characteristic function for distinct input states test the gate and demonstrate continuous control of the identity-SWAP superposition. This implementation provides a tunable interaction for hybrid qubit-bosonic quantum information processing.
