High-efficiency photonic GHZ measurements with linear optics

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics October 30, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article shows that by exploiting many‑body interference in linear‑optical circuits, photonic GHZ measurements can reach a success probability of 9/32 for four photons—far above the ~1/4 limit of conventional Bell‑fusion schemes—and that the method scales exponentially better for larger entangled states. The higher‑efficiency fusions directly translate into faster generation of graph‑state resources and more reliable entanglement distribution across spin‑photon interfaces, addressing a key bottleneck in fusion‑based quantum computing architectures. While still probabilistic and requiring adaptive post‑selection, the approach provides a practical new primitive that could shrink overheads for scalable photonic processors.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

Probabilistic and destructive Bell-measurements, also known as fusions, constitute a fundamental primitive for photonic quantum technologies. These operations rely only on pairwise photonic interference, whereas the computational power of photonic quantum systems originates from many-body interference. Here, we introduce a family of high-efficiency linear-optical GHZ measurements based on four-mode interferometers and show that incorporating many-body interference into this ubiquitous primitive leads to more efficient entangling operations. For the four-photon case, the success probability is $9/32$, and adaptive variants ensure that unsuccessful events project onto useful stabilizer states. We further show that the approach naturally generalizes to larger GHZ measurements, yielding an exponentially higher success probability than previous linear-optical schemes. Finally, we demonstrate practical advantages for graph-state resource generation, entanglement distribution between remote spin-photon interfaces, and fusion-based quantum computing. Our results establish high-efficiency many-body measurements as a new primitive for scalable photonic quantum information processing.
