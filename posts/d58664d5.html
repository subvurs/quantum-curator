Accelerating Quantum Error Correction Simulations under Coherent Noise with Compiled Clifford-Augmented Matrix Product States

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics November 18, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article tackles a long‑standing bottleneck in fault‑tolerant research by showing how tensor‑network simulations of coherent noise can be sped up dramatically without sacrificing accuracy, a crucial step because most error‑correction benchmarks still rely on Pauli‑twirling approximations that miss subtle interference effects. By reusing Clifford operations and compressing the residual state, the compiled CAMPS method delivers order‑of‑magnitude sampling gains at modest bond dimensions, enabling realistic studies of surface‑code and bicycle‑code performance out to distances previously inaccessible to exact simulation. Summary

Reliable assessment of quantum error correction requires understanding how coherent errors affect logical performance. Although tensor networks naturally capture coherent interference, entanglement growth and large qubit counts make repeated sampling costly. Here we develop a compiled Clifford-Augmented Matrix Product State (CAMPS) framework that reuses Clifford transformations across shots, recycles qubits released by measurements, evolves a compressed residual state, and accelerates sampling through rotation fusion and compilation tailored to the MPS layout. Surface-code benchmarks under fixed-sign coherent noise yield accurate logical error estimates at small bond dimensions, while random-sign simulations up to distance $d=9$ and on the $[[72,12,6]]$ bivariate bicycle code match the Pauli-twirling approximation results. Compared with uncompiled CAMPS, compiled CAMPS converges at smaller bond dimensions and achieves order-of-magnitude sampling speedups at equal bond dimension, opening a scalable route to studying coherent error propagation in larger fault-tolerant circuits.
