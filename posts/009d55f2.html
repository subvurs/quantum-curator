Entanglement inference from quantum erasure and path discrimination

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics November 02, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article shows how to rigorously extract the minimum amount of multipartite entanglement that must have been present in an experiment solely from quantum‑eraser and path‑discrimination data, closing a long‑standing loophole where recovered interference fringes could be misleading. By providing exact semidefinite‐programming bounds and explicit state constructions for noisy GHZ preparations, the work links entanglement certification directly to observable measurement records—a capability that will tighten verification protocols for quantum sensors and near‑term processors. It also clarifies how additional parity or marker measurements can boost certification, offering a practical roadmap for experimental groups seeking device‑independent guarantees without assuming ideal sources. The results therefore sharpen both the theory and practice of entanglement witnessing in hardware‑focused quantum technologies.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

Fringes recovered by conditioning on quantum-eraser outcomes do not, by themselves, establish entanglement. We determine the minimum entanglement compatible with recorded eraser data when the preparation may be mixed, distinguishing these data from independently determined optimal which-path distinguishabilities and additional joint measurement outcomes. For noisy Greenberger-Horne-Zeilinger (GHZ) preparations, explicit compatible states establish sharp entanglement-depth thresholds and show how parity information strengthens certification. A matching converse establishes the exact threshold for two complete Pauli-outcome distributions. For an ideal three-qubit eraser, we classify the additional marker measurement directions that certify genuine multipartite entanglement within a fixed eraser configuration and determine the separate minima of compatible concurrence and squared GHZ fidelity for specified tilted axes. For noisy three-qubit data, an exact semidefinite characterization and constructive converse determine biseparable compatibility at arbitrary fixed local measurement axes, with rational certificates bounding one example. These results establish what entanglement is required by the measurement record, rather than inferred from assumptions about the source.
