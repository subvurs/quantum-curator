Magnetization, susceptibility, and entanglement entropy in antiferromagnetic star-shaped homometallic tetranuclear and pentanuclear Cu(II) complexes

Source: arXiv Condensed Matter November 25, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article shows how the magnetic response of tiny antiferromagnetic Cu(II) clusters can be directly linked to their quantum‑entanglement entropy, providing a rare experimental window onto many‑body entanglement in solid‑state systems. By demonstrating that cluster size, geometry and Dzyaloshinskii‑Moriya anisotropy control both susceptibility peaks and magnetization jumps, the work bridges molecular spin chemistry with the design rules emerging for scalable quantum simulators based on engineered spin networks. The results suggest that such star‑shaped molecules could serve as testbeds for benchmarking entanglement‑based error mitigation strategies in near‑term quantum hardware.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

We present a theoretical investigation of the magnetization, magnetic susceptibility, and quantum entanglement entropy in antiferromagnetic star-shaped tetranuclear ($Cu_4$) and pentanuclear ($Cu_5$) Cu(II) clusters, motivated by experimentally realized molecular spin systems. In both cases, the study presents a discussion of the comparison between experimental data and theoretical analytical calculations of magnetic susceptibility as a function of temperature. The correlation between magnetization jumps and magnetic susceptibility peaks, along with the presence of entanglement entropy plateaus as a function of external magnetic fields, is analyzed in the low-temperature regime. Overall, the results demonstrate that the cluster size, geometry, and interaction anisotropy play a crucial role in shaping both the thermodynamic and the entanglement properties. In addition, the study is based on antiferromagnetic Heisenberg spin Hamiltonians, with the inclusion of the Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya interaction.
