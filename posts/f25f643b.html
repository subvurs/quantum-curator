Entanglement, records, and quantum recovery in qubit chains

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics November 19, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article demonstrates for the first time how a simple, exactly solvable qubit chain can separate the spread of entanglement from the emergence of locally readable information, revealing that weak interactions let quantum correlations outrun any measurable record on a neighboring site. By showing that off‑diagonal elements of a reduced density matrix serve as the leading “record” detectable with a single Pauli measurement—and by quantifying optimal recovery fidelities when only a fragment of the environment is accessible—the work bridges fundamental studies of decoherence with practical concerns in quantum error correction and sensor design. The finding that intra‑environment interactions can reshuffle readable records without altering the system’s reduced state suggests new strategies for engineering protective environments that preserve recoverable information while minimizing disturbance to the processor itself.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

We investigate entanglement, readable records and quantum recovery using an exactly solvable chain of sequentially interacting qubits. For weak interactions, entanglement reaches a later environment site before that site carries an equally strong local record. The leading local record is encoded in an off-diagonal element of the reduced density matrix and can be detected by a Pauli measurement, while a population measurement gives a higher-order signal. We further characterize the accessibility of the relative phase through joint transverse correlations and derive the optimal fidelity for recovering an unknown quantum state from the system and an accessible environment fragment. We show that interactions acting only within the environment can redistribute readable records and change the region required for quantum recovery without affecting the reduced state of the system.
