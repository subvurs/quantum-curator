Quantum Advantage has Few Complexity Implications

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics November 03, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article shows that the existence of “proofs of quantumness” or oblivious state‑preparation protocols does not automatically force a separation between classical and quantum complexity classes, even in worlds where P = NP or BQP = QCMA. By constructing explicit classical and unitary oracles, the authors demonstrate that such quantum advantage tests can coexist with surprisingly weak separations, which tempers expectations that they will directly yield new post‑quantum cryptographic primitives via relativizing proofs. The result sharpens our understanding of what recent experimental demonstrations of quantumness can (and cannot) guarantee for future security constructions.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

We consider the complexity implications of proofs of quantumness and (quantum verifier) oblivious state preparation. We show that both of these objects can coexist with the easiness of nondeterministic computation. That is, we give evidence that proofs of quantumness can exist when $\mathsf{P} = \mathsf{NP}$ and that (quantum verifier) oblivious state preparation can exist when $\mathsf{BQP} = \mathsf{QCMA}$. In particular, we give a classical oracle relative to which (1) $\mathsf{P}^\mathcal{O} = \mathsf{NP}^\mathcal{O}$ (2) $\mathsf{BQP}^\mathcal{O} = \mathsf{PSPACE}^\mathcal{O}$ (3) Proofs of quantumness exist. In particular, there exists a language in $\mathsf{IP}^\mathcal{O} \subseteq \mathsf{BQP}^\mathcal{O}$ which is hard on average against $\mathsf{P}^\mathcal{O}/poly$. We give a unitary oracle such that (1) $\mathsf{BQP}^\mathcal{O} = \mathsf{QCMA}^\mathcal{O}$ (2) Quantum verifier oblivious state preparation exists. These oracle separations directly imply a number of related consequences. For example, our first result implies that proofs of quantumness cannot be used to construct classical or quantum cryptography with a relativizing proof. Our second result implies that quantum computation/classical communication oblivious transfer cannot be used to construct post-quantum one-way functions with a relativizing proof.
