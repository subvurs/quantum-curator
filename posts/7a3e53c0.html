Resolving and Suppressing Temporally Correlated Noise in Quantum Error Correction

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics November 16, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article demonstrates the first scalable mitigation of temporally correlated errors on a 105‑qubit superconducting processor, showing that surface‑code logical memory can retain its time‑domain error suppression as the code size grows from 3×3 to 7×7 patches. By identifying and modeling leakage into the third excited state and introducing a “walking” stability circuit, the authors push the logical error floor down to ≤3.2 × 10⁻⁸ over 60 cycles—two orders of magnitude better than conventional approaches—and reveal that remaining failures are dominated by rare ionizing‑radiation bursts. The adaptive termination scheme further trims spacetime overhead, highlighting a practical pathway toward fault‑tolerant quantum computers that must control both spatial and temporal error channels.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

Scaling fault-tolerant quantum error correction (QEC) requires suppressing errors in both space and time, yet standard logical memory experiments primarily probe spatial faults. Here, we use a 105-qubit Willow superconducting quantum processor to realize and scale the surface code stability experiment, which is alternatively sensitive to temporal error chains. Scaling from patch sizes of $3 \times 3$ to $7 \times 7$, we realize a timelike block suppression factor of $μ_T = 1.47 \pm 0.25$, demonstrating the ability to maintain temporal error suppression as the number of qubits increases. By extending the stability experiment for many cycles, we uncover a sum-of-exponentials behavior in error suppression caused by a temporally correlated error: data-qubit heating to the third excited state $|3\rangle$. We fully capture this effect with a parameter-free, device-level leakage model incorporating higher-state transport during syndrome extraction cycles. To suppress this error, we develop and realize a walking stability circuit, reaching a logical error probability floor of $\le 3.20\times 10^{-8}$ at 60 cycles---two orders of magnitude below the standard construction---and limited by ionizing radiation error bursts. Finally, by adaptively terminating syndrome extraction based on decoder confidence in an offline analysis, we reduce spacetime logical error by 38% and time overhead by 34% compared to the optimal fixed-cycle strategy.
