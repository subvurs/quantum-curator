Explicit Absolutely Maximally Entangled states in dimensions twice a prime power

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics November 12, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article delivers the first explicit families of absolutely maximally entangled (AME) states in local dimensions that are twice an odd prime power, closing a long‑standing gap in multipartite entanglement constructions and completing the nine‑party AME classification for all integer dimensions ≥ 3. By coupling qubits with higher‑dimensional q‑level systems, the authors obtain scalable AME(N, 2q) states that can be directly translated into optimal quantum error‑correcting codes and secret‑sharing schemes, a development that dovetails with recent advances in high‑dimensional quantum networking and holographic code design. The work also highlights an exponential growth requirement on q for larger N, underscoring both the power of the new construction and the remaining challenge of finding more resource‑efficient AME states.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

Absolutely maximally entangled (AME) states are pure multipartite states with maximally mixed reductions on every set of at most half the parties. We construct explicit families in local dimension $2q$, twice an odd prime power $q$, using an established coupling of qubits and $q$-level systems. The $q$-level states remove interference between branches, so the qubit states need maximally mixed reductions only on average. This gives AME$(9,2q)$ and AME$(11,2q)$ for every odd prime power $q\geq5$, AME$(13,2q)$ for $q\geq17$, AME$(15,2q)$ for $q\geq9$, and AME$(17,2q)$ for $q\geq67$. For every odd $N\geq3$, the same coupling gives AME$(N,2q)$ under the sufficient condition $q\geq\max\{N,2^{N-2}\}$ on the odd prime power $q$. Within this coupling, the smallest such $q$ must grow exponentially with $N$. Combined with known inputs and products, these families complete the nine-party classification: for integer $d\geq2$, an AME$(9,d)$ state exists if and only if $d\geq3$. Choosing the branch pairing also gives AME$(15,2p)$ states with a cyclic shift of the parties compensated by local unitaries, for every prime $p\equiv1\pmod{15}$.
