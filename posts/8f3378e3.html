Altera and Riverlane Partner on QEC Interfaces for Agilex FPGAs Following Post-Quantum Security Rollout

Source: Quantum Computing Report September 11, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article highlights a rare convergence of quantum‑ready hardware and error‑correction infrastructure by pairing Altera’s Agilex FPGA line with Riverlane’s QEC expertise, offering the first open‑source protocol (QECi) that can shuttle syndrome data to decoders in real time. By coupling that capability with Altera’s newly added post‑quantum cryptography blocks, the partnership creates a unified platform that both hardens classical systems against future quantum attacks and supplies low‑latency control fabric for near‑term quantum processors. It signals a move toward standardized, FPGA‑based QEC stacks—a step that could accelerate experimental demonstrations and reduce custom engineering effort across labs. The approach still depends on the maturity of software decoders and integration with specific qubit technologies, so broader adoption will hinge on those downstream developments.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

Altera and Riverlane have partnered to optimize quantum error correction (QEC) interfaces for Altera's Agilex FPGAs, enabling real-time QEC data movement and low-latency control. This collaboration, which includes an open-source QEC Interface (QECi) protocol, aims to standardize data exchange between quantum control electronics and QEC decoders. This initiative complements Altera's recent rollout of Post-Quantum Cryptography (PQC) support in its Agilex 3 and 5 FPGAs, establishing a dual strategy to secure classical infrastructure against quantum threats and provide control fabric for quantum computing.
