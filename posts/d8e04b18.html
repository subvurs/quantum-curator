Finite-Key Analysis for Multiple-Antenna Quantum Key Distribution with Continuous Variables

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics November 19, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article delivers the first finite‑key security proof for continuous‑variable quantum key distribution that exploits multiple‑antenna (MIMO) techniques, showing how a brief low‑Earth‑orbit satellite overpass can still yield provably secret keys. By quantifying the channel with realistic signal counts, the authors demonstrate up to a 6 dB advantage over single‑mode CV‑QKD and a 3 dB gain versus simple multiplexing—an improvement that could make space‑based QKD links far more efficient. The work builds on recent advances in finite‑key analysis for CV protocols and extends them into the multi‑spatial‑mode regime, suggesting that key rates may scale with the product of input and output ports. While promising, the results remain theoretical and will need experimental validation under actual atmospheric turbulence and hardware constraints.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

Multiple-input multiple-output (MIMO) techniques, known for their use in wireless communications, can also find applications in quantum communications systems. This is, for instance, relevant to satellite-based quantum communications when atmospheric turbulence and the time-varying nature of the channel can make their implementation difficult. Here, we study the application of MIMO techniques to continuous-variable (CV) quantum key distribution (QKD) in the realistic setting where a finite number of signals are exchanged. We show how data collected during the brief overpass time of low-earth-orbit satellites over ground stations can be processed to estimate MIMO channel parameters, extract a secret key, and quantify the key generation rate. For $2\times 2$ MIMO settings with Gaussian encoding and heterodyne detection, our finite-key analysis indicates that MIMO CV-QKD could outperform both multiplexed (with a nominal 3~dB improvement) and single-mode (with a nominal 6~dB nominal improvement) scenarios. Furthermore, we conjecture that the ultimate enhancement of a multiple-antenna CV-QKD system can be characterized by the product of the number of its inputs and outputs.
