Dynamics of Majorana tetron qubits under quasiparticle poisoning

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics August 18, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article tackles one of the toughest obstacles for Majorana‑based hardware—quasiparticle poisoning—by delivering closed‑form decoherence rates that remain valid from the strong‑charging to the transmon‑like regime. By showing how the protective exponential suppression erodes once the qubit’s energy splitting grows, it gives experimentalists a concrete tool for interpreting time‑domain measurements and optimizing device parameters. The work therefore bridges theory and the emerging wave of Majorana tetron prototypes, helping to chart realistic pathways toward topologically protected quantum processors.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

We study the dissipative dynamics of a Majorana tetron qubit in the presence of extrinsic quasiparticle poisoning due to the coupling to external leads. From the Bloch-Redfield equation describing a finite-size topological superconductor hosting four Majorana zero modes and tunnel-coupled to fermionic reservoirs, we recover analytical expressions for the decoherence rate of a Majorana qubit at arbitrary values of the charging energy. The analysis shows that the exponential suppression of the decoherence rate is gradually removed by the energy splitting of the qubit states. These results can be useful to understand time-domain experimental data in Majorana qubit prototypes.
