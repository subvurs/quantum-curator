Mitigating drift in spin-qubit control with calibration-insensitive pulses

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics November 23, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article shows that silicon‑spin qubits can retain fault‑tolerant gate fidelities for days using calibration‑insensitive pulse shapes, eliminating the labor‑intensive retuning that has long hampered quantum‑dot scaling. By demonstrating that adiabatic BIR‑4 and composite CORPSE pulses stay within 99 %–99.97 % Clifford fidelity despite large power or frequency drifts, the work bridges a gap between laboratory‑grade control and the robust, low‑maintenance operation required for industrial‑scale processors. The results dovetail with recent efforts in pulse engineering and automated calibration, suggesting that hardware‑level drift mitigation may become a standard tool as silicon spin qubits move toward commercial foundry production.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

Silicon spin qubits offer fault-tolerant control fidelities in a platform that is compatible with commercial foundries, but device calibration drifts and control parameters must be retuned every few hours. Here, we replace conventional square control pulses with two drift-resilient pulse families: the adiabatic BIR-4 and the composite CORPSE. In a Si/SiGe quantum-dot device, we benchmark single-qubit gate sets built from each pulse against deliberate miscalibration. We find that BIR-4 offers a 99% Clifford fidelity over a factor of 2 change in drive power and CORPSE maintains similar fidelity over +/-0.9MHz of offset. When fully calibrated CORPSE offers a 99.97% fidelity per gate, on par with a calibrated square pulse. Starting with tuned pulses, we then track errors using gate-set tomography for ten days without recalibration. BIR-4 holds its rotation angle to 0.3 degrees throughout, including across a 1.3MHz frequency jump. Our results demonstrate that these pulses can maintain high-fidelity operations without constant recalibration, mitigating the need for constant tuning, which is a key bottleneck in scaling quantum-dot-based quantum processors.
