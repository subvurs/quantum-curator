Physicist develops detector capable of counting photons for quantum computing

Source: Phys.org Quantum November 05, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article highlights a breakthrough in photon‑number resolution that could remove a long‑standing bottleneck for optical quantum processors, where precise counting of simultaneous photons is essential for error‑corrected gates and boson‑sampling experiments. Baalbergen’s detector builds on recent advances in superconducting nanowire and transition‑edge sensors, but adds true multiplexed readout that scales to higher photon fluxes without sacrificing efficiency. If the technology matures, it will enable more reliable photonic qubit encoding and faster interconnects between quantum modules, though integration with existing waveguide platforms still needs engineering work.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

Detecting a single photon is a challenge in itself. But what if you also want to know exactly how many photons arrive at a particular point at the same time? Physicist Erik Baalbergen is working on light detectors that can do just that. They form a key building block for the optical quantum computer of the future.
