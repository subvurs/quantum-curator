An Alternative to Quantum Theory

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics November 09, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article proposes a radical, scale‑invariant ontology that seeks to reproduce all quantum predictions without invoking wavefunctions or dynamical evolution, offering a fresh angle on the long‑standing measurement and entanglement puzzles. By grounding observable phenomena in simple ratios of masses, charges and separations among point particles, it connects to earlier “hidden‑variable” approaches while sidestepping many of their technical hurdles, potentially reshaping how we think about atomistic structure and macroscopic emergence. If the framework can be extended to concrete sensing protocols or experimental tests, it could open a new pathway toward theory‑driven device design, but at present its claims remain highly speculative and will require rigorous validation against established quantum results.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

Despite the remarkable empirical success of quantum theory, the interpretation of measurement and the nature of entanglement correlations remain open questions. We outline an alternative framework that seeks to recover the empirical results of quantum theory from a holistic, scale-invariant ontology. Assuming only point particles in Euclidean space, characterised by ratios of masses (and charges) and by the ratios of their mutual separations, the theory describes shapes through a single scale-invariant function; no dynamical evolution is required. In principle, this framework provides a unified description of finite subsystems embedded in a configuration of infinitely many particles and offers a possible route to deriving the observed structures of atoms, molecules and macroscopic bodies without invoking standard quantum mechanics.
