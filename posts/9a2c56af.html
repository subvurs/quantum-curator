Quantum-centric assessment of saturated ferromagnetism beyond the flat-band limit

Source: arXiv Condensed Matter November 25, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article demonstrates that near‑term quantum processors can tackle genuinely hard many‑body problems by mapping a 64‑qubit sawtooth Hubbard chain onto a configuration‑interaction subspace that is orders of magnitude smaller than the full Hilbert space yet still captures saturated ferromagnetism with >97 % fidelity. By quantifying how the critical on‑site repulsion scales with band dispersion (U≈2W) and establishing a rigorous lower bound for a 4×3 decorated lattice, the work bridges a gap between ideal flat‑band theory and realistic materials where bandwidth cannot be ignored. The results showcase both a concrete benchmark for quantum hardware and a pathway toward using quantum simulations to predict magnetic phases in correlated electron systems.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

Saturated ferromagnetism has been proven for every on-site repulsion $U > 0$ in Hubbard models with a flat lowest band, and the interaction required for it grows with the band width $W$ when the band becomes dispersive. We study the dispersive case at quarter filling by quantum-selected configuration interaction and sample-based quantum diagonalization with IBM Quantum's Heron processors, focusing on the quantized square of the total spin of the computed states. We treat a sawtooth chain of 16 unit cells on 64 qubits, whose full configuration space of $1.1\times 10^{14}$ determinants exceeds that of one of the largest full configuration interaction calculations, and obtain the stability diagram of saturated ferromagnetism on the decorated square lattice in the plane of $U$ and $W$. For the chain, a subspace of only $3\times 10^{-5}$ of the full configuration space gives a state with more than $97\%$ of its weight in the sector of maximal spin. For the decorated square lattice of $4\times 3$ unit cells on 48 qubits, the computed states in subspaces of less than $1\%$ of the full configuration space switch from low-spin states to nearly fully polarized states at $U \simeq 2W$. The calculations also give a rigorous lower bound of $1.9W$ on the critical interaction of the $4\times 3$ lattice.
