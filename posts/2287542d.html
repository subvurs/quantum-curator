Trotterized hybrid qubit-boson simulation of the Jaynes-Cummings-Hubbard model

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics November 24, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article demonstrates a practical route to simulate light‑matter lattice models on near‑term quantum hardware by combining qubit and bosonic registers in a Trotterized circuit, something that has only recently become feasible with tools like Bosonic Qiskit and matrix‑product‑state backends. By benchmarking single‑polariton transport, two‑excitation dynamics under hard‑core constraints, and entanglement growth against exact diagonalization, the authors show that hybrid qubit‑boson simulations can capture nontrivial many‑body physics while remaining tractable on classical simulators. The work therefore paves the way for experimentalists to explore more complex, possibly open, photonic quantum systems on emerging platforms that natively support both qubits and bosonic modes.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

The Jaynes-Cummings-Hubbard (JCH) model describes the interaction of light and matter in coupled optical cavities and provides a framework for studying their quantum dynamics. In this work, we design a Trotterized quantum circuit to investigate the real-time dynamics of the JCH model using Bosonic Qiskit and a Matrix Product State (MPS) backend. We simulate single-polariton transport and benchmark site occupations against exact diagonalization results. We then extend the simulations to the two-excitation sector under a hard-core photon constraint, examining excitation transport and symmetry properties. Finally, we study the dependence of bipartite entanglement entropy on the inter-cavity photon-hopping strength. The results illustrate how hybrid qubit-boson circuits, simulated with matrix product states, can be used to study light-matter lattice dynamics. This work provides a numerical benchmark for extending hybrid simulation approaches to more complex interacting and open light-matter systems.
