Instanton tunnelling as a verifiable benchmark for digital quantum simulation: circuit structure, classical simulability and hardware reproducibility

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics November 18, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article introduces a rare “exact‑by‑construction” benchmark for digital quantum simulators by exploiting instanton tunnelling in a quartic double‑well, allowing every intermediate result to be compared against an analytically known answer. By encoding the lattice with a single‑excitation (unary) mapping, the authors prove that the circuit’s entanglement stays bounded (Schmidt rank ≤ 4), which makes matrix‑product‑state simulations tractable even up to 48 sites and reveals a clear N⁻² scaling of the admissible Trotter step. Demonstrating reproducible tunnelling splittings on two superconducting processors, the work provides a practical yardstick for assessing hardware fidelity and error drift in near‑term devices while highlighting that larger lattices will still demand careful Trotter optimisation.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

We use instanton-mediated tunnelling in the quartic double well as a benchmark for digital quantum simulation that is checkable against an exact answer at every step. The instanton enters through the classical analysis; the hardware tests the dynamics of the lattice model. A unary (single-excitation) encoding maps N lattice sites to N qubits, with the single-excitation dynamics as a reference that remains classically cheap at any N. For a second-order Trotter circuit with adjacent, equal-angle XX/YY pairs on each bond, we prove that the Schmidt rank across any cut is at most four at every gate boundary and at most two after each pair, independently of N. Matrix-product-state simulations of this gate list reproduce the exact splitting up to N = 48. For this bond-wise formula the admissible Trotter step shrinks roughly as N^-2, so one tunnelling period costs about N^3.3 two-qubit gates; a kinetic/potential splitting keeps the step near 0.065 up to N=128, so this scaling belongs to splitting the kinetic term bond by bond. At a coarser, separately bounded step we measure the full transfer at N=8 on two superconducting processors, recovering the lattice splitting to a few percent and its dependence on the barrier height, which at this resolution is set by the lattice rather than by the instanton. Over 9 repetitions, runs taken minutes apart agree to shot noise, while runs hours to weeks apart differ by up to 6.1%, also on a fixed set of qubits; with sessions as the sampling unit, the processors differ in the damping, while a difference in the splitting is not established. Normalising by the measured single-excitation population extends the usable depth about 4-fold but does not improve the splitting. A Gaussian start state, which needs no diagonalisation, reproduces the splitting of the eigenvector-based start to within 1.6 percentage points.
