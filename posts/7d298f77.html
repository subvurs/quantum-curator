Quantum control of an electrically levitated nanoparticle with a trapped ion

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics November 20, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article shows that a single laser‑cooled ion can act as a quantum “refrigerator” and control hub for an electrically levitated nanoparticle, opening a realistic path to ground‑state cooling and non‑Gaussian state engineering of macroscopic objects. By exploiting parametric modulation of the Paul‑trap potential to resonantly enhance Coulomb coupling, the scheme builds on recent hybrid ion–nanoparticle experiments and complements optomechanical approaches that rely on optical tweezers, offering a fully electrical route with potentially lower photon‑scattering heating. If experimentally realized, such ion‑nanoparticle hybrids could become versatile testbeds for macroscopic quantum physics, precision sensing, and even modular quantum networking, although achieving the required trap stability and mitigating charge noise will be critical challenges.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

Quantum control of levitated nanoparticles offers a route towards macroscopic quantum states. Here we propose a novel way to achieve it for a nanoparticle levitated electrically in a linear Paul trap together with a single ion, a system recently realized experimentally. By suitably engineering the trap's AC potential, one can parametrically modulate the ion-nanoparticle distance and thus the Coulomb coupling to enable a resonant interaction. For realistic parameters, we calculate that this enables sympathetic cooling of the nanoparticle to its motional ground state by a single, continuously laser-cooled ion. Additional AC drives enable the preparation of squeezed nanoparticle states and of ion-nanoparticle entanglement. Moreover, non-Gaussian states such as Fock and cat states, prepared in the ion with standard trapped-ion techniques, can be transferred to the nanoparticle with high fidelities. We provide complete protocols for each of these states, including all known decoherence sources. Our results establish electrically trapped ion-nanoparticle systems as a platform for quantum control of levitated matter.
