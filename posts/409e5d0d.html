Perturbative unitarity bounds from qubit entanglement in scalar scattering and QED

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics November 24, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article demonstrates that the entanglement generated between qubit‑encoded particle spins in simple scalar and QED scattering can be used as a quantitative probe of perturbative unitarity, yielding coupling‑constant limits that are often tighter than traditional partial‑wave bounds. By tracing out momentum and analysing the resulting quantum channel, the authors turn a textbook scattering problem into a concrete testbed for quantum information techniques, suggesting new ways to benchmark and validate quantum simulators that aim to emulate relativistic field theories. The approach also sidesteps the notorious forward‑scattering singularities that plague conventional analyses, offering a cleaner theoretical handle that could inform both high‑energy phenomenology and the design of hardware‑efficient algorithms for scattering simulations.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

In the framework of $2\to 2$ relativistic scattering described by the perturbative $S$-matrix, we perform a comprehensive analysis of the properties of quantum channels obtained by tracing out the momentum from the scattered state. We work in two distinct scenarios with qubit degrees of freedom: flavored scalar contact interactions and QED, focusing in particular on initial-state leptons with transverse polarization. We identify the conditions that make the quantum channel unitary, calculate the entanglement of the qubits through the concurrence, and use its maximally allowed value to extract perturbativity upper bounds on the coupling constants. We find that the limits are often more constraining than the corresponding partial-wave expansion bounds, and that they allow us to bypass some of the obstacles associated in the latter with the singular behavior of forward scattering.
