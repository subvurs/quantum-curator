HKMA and HKUST Launch PQC Toolkit to Accelerate Banking Sector Transition by 2030

Source: Quantum Computing Report November 07, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article is notable because it marks one of the first coordinated, regulator‑driven efforts to embed post‑quantum cryptography into a national financial ecosystem, signaling that quantum‑grade threats are being taken seriously beyond academic circles. By providing a concrete toolkit for inventorying legacy algorithms, prioritizing risk, and aligning supply‑chain governance, HKMA and HKUST give banks a clear pathway toward the 2030 target of a perfect Quantum Preparedness Index—a benchmark that could become a model for other jurisdictions. The move dovetails with global NIST standardisation work and recent pilot deployments in Europe, underscoring that practical quantum‑safe migration is now an operational priority rather than a distant research problem. Readers should note, however, that the toolkit’s success will hinge on industry adoption speed and the eventual finalisation of PQC standards.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

The Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) and The Hong Kong University of Science and Technology (HKUST) have jointly launched a Post-Quantum Cryptography (PQC) Toolkit. This toolkit provides a framework for Hong Kong's banking sector to transition to quantum-safe infrastructure by 2030, aiming to raise the Quantum Preparedness Index (QPI) score from 2.3 to 10.0. It addresses cryptographic inventory, risk prioritization, supply chain engagement, and governance to mitigate risks from quantum computing.
