DynaMite: Dynamic Measurement Scheduling To Accelerate Fault-Tolerant Quantum Programs

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics November 03, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article tackles one of the biggest hidden bottlenecks in fault‑tolerant processors: the static measurement latency that inflates logical cycle times and error rates. By introducing DynaMite’s real‑time latency selection, which tailors readout speed to each logical cycle’s error budget, the authors show a clear path to shave tens of percent off both runtime and logical error probability—an advance that dovetails with recent efforts in active reset and low‑latency control stacks from IBM and Google. If hardware can expose fast, programmable measurement windows, the technique could become a practical lever for scaling up surface‑code or other QEC codes without waiting for fundamentally faster detectors.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

Fault-tolerant quantum computers run millions of logical cycles to perform quantum error correction (QEC). As logical operations span entire logical cycles, the logical cycle time (LCT) dictates program runtime. Qubit measurements, critical to extract parity information for QEC, are the slowest operations and account for 43-86% of LCT. Measurement latencies impact fidelity: fast measurements lower our ability to correctly classify quantum states, whereas slow measurements increase the likelihood of decoherence. Hardware providers thus calibrate measurement latencies that maximize readout fidelity per qubit and use them to schedule all measurements during program execution. However, calibrated latencies are sub-optimal for logical performance because logical cycles span multiple QEC rounds, during which data qubits are not measured. This increases LCT and logical error rates (LERs) due to decoherence at the logical level. We propose DynaMite, a lightweight real-time measurement latency selection policy built on two key insights. First, logical cycles must use a measurement latency that is fast enough to reduce both LCT and LER for the QEC code, instead of the calibrated latency that only maximizes readout fidelity. Second, not all logical cycles are equal; many do not fully utilize the error budget of the QEC code, i.e., the number of failed parity checks it can tolerate without compromising LER. These cycles could use even faster measurements but do not, because latencies are chosen statically, whereas error information is only available at runtime. DynaMite exploits the fact that a logical cycle comprises multiple QEC rounds and selects the fastest latency for an upcoming round based on the error budget left and rounds remaining. Our evaluations show that DynaMite reduces LCT by 1.69x on average and up to 2.29x, and reduces LER to 0.46x on average and up to 0.13x of baseline.
