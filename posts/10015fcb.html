Accelerating Computing With Light

Source: The Quantum Insider November 24, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article spotlights the growing momentum behind photonic quantum processors, a platform that leverages the inherent speed and low‑noise properties of light to push computational tasks far beyond what conventional silicon chips can achieve. Recent milestones such as Xanadu’s Borealis and IBM’s integrated‑photonics roadmap make Avik Dutt’s vision of solving classically intractable problems in minutes feel increasingly plausible, especially for optimization and sampling workloads that map naturally onto boson‑sampling architectures. While the promise is exciting, readers should keep in mind that scaling up photon sources, low‑loss waveguides, and high‑efficiency detectors remain critical hurdles before light‑based quantum computers can move from laboratory demos to practical accelerators.

— Mark Eatherly
