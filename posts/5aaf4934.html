RIKEN Upgrades Reimei-Fugaku Hybrid Quantum Platform with 56-Qubit Quantinuum H2 Processor

Source: Quantum Computing Report November 10, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article matters because it marks the first time a leading national supercomputer has been directly coupled to Quantinuum’s newest 56‑qubit trapped‑ion processor, dramatically expanding the quantum‑classical bandwidth available for chemistry and materials simulations. By replacing the modest 20‑qubit H1 with a higher‑fidelity, larger‑capacity H2, RIKEN can explore active spaces that were previously out of reach on NISQ hardware, putting hybrid algorithms into a regime closer to practical relevance. The upgrade also signals a broader shift toward tightly integrated quantum–HPC testbeds, echoing similar efforts at IBM, Google and AWS, and suggests that near‑term scientific breakthroughs will increasingly rely on such co‑design platforms—though the ultimate impact will still depend on continued improvements in error mitigation and scaling of trapped‑ion control.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

RIKEN has upgraded its Reimei-Fugaku hybrid quantum platform with Quantinuum's 56-qubit System Model H2 trapped-ion quantum computer, replacing the 20-qubit H1. This upgrade enhances physical capacity, gate fidelity, and computational active space, allowing for more complex quantum chemistry simulations. The platform links Quantinuum's quantum computer directly to the Fugaku supercomputer, creating a powerful testbed for hybrid quantum-classical computing.
