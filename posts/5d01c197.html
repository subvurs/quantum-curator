Anderon Finalizes $1 Billion CHIPS Act Award to Scale 300mm Pure-Play Quantum Wafer Foundry

Source: Quantum Computing Report September 16, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article marks a watershed moment for quantum hardware by turning the long‑awaited promise of a dedicated, large‑scale quantum wafer fab into reality; a $2 billion investment—half from the CHIPS Act and half from IBM—will lift Anderon’s 300 mm line to volume production of superconducting qubit arrays. By providing an open, pure‑play foundry, the effort directly tackles the current bottleneck in QPU scaling that has forced many startups to rely on limited university or legacy CMOS facilities, and it dovetails with recent government pushes to secure a domestic microelectronics supply chain for quantum technologies. The move also signals that the industry is maturing beyond prototype labs toward an ecosystem where multiple vendors can access standardized, high‑yield quantum chips, although achieving commercial yields at this scale will still require significant process refinement.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

Anderon LLC, an IBM subsidiary, secured $1 billion in CHIPS Act funding, supplemented by another $1 billion from IBM, to expand its 300mm pure-play quantum wafer fabrication facility at Albany NanoTech. This investment aims to scale production of superconducting qubit arrays and other quantum components, addressing QPU scaling bottlenecks and strengthening domestic microelectronics supply chains. The facility will serve as an open commercial foundry for the global quantum hardware ecosystem.
