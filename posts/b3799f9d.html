Efficient calculation of the full Laguerre-Gaussian spectrum of entangled biphotons

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics November 19, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article delivers a compact analytical tool that replaces the brute‑force multidimensional integrals normally required to predict the full Laguerre‑Gaussian spectrum of SPDC biphotons, making it practical to explore very high radial and OAM orders without numerical instability. By expressing the source geometry in just a few dimensionless parameters, researchers can now rapidly optimize pump and crystal settings to approach maximal high‑dimensional entanglement—a key step for scaling orbital‑angular‑momentum based quantum communication and photonic processors. The work builds on recent efforts to harness OAM for large Hilbert spaces but overcomes the longstanding bottleneck of computational cost, although experimental validation will still be needed to confirm how closely real devices follow the idealized phase‑matching model.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

The spatial biphoton state generated by spontaneous parametric down-conversion (SPDC) enables applications in high-dimensional quantum information processing and is often described in Laguerre-Gaussian (LG) modes. However, calculating the full LG spectrum for arbitrary radial and orbital angular momentum (OAM) indices requires computationally demanding multidimensional integrations. We present a generating-function approach that analytically performs the four-dimensional transverse spatial integral without approximating the phase-matching function. The source geometry can be completely described by a few dimensionless parameters, which reduce to two parameters for wave-vector-degenerate photons with equal waists. Our derived expressions for the full LG spectrum allow for computationally efficient and numerically stable evaluation at high mode orders, addressing a crucial obstacle in previous works. To illustrate our approach, we compare OAM spectra obtained by projection onto the lowest radial order and by radially insensitive detection, and investigate how high-dimensional maximal entanglement can be approached by tuning source parameters. Our results provide a new analytical tool for efficiently characterizing and engineering high-dimensional spatial entanglement in SPDC.
