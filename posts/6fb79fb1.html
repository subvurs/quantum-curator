Sandia and Quantinuum Introduce Logical Gate Set Tomography to Map Fault-Tolerant Qubit Noise

Source: Quantum Computing Report October 31, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article reports the first self‑consistent tomographic reconstruction of an entire universal single‑qubit logical gate set—“Logical GST”—demonstrated on a Steane‑code logical qubit in Quantinuum’s H1‑1 processor. By confirming that error correction largely reduces physical noise to simple Pauli‑stochastic channels while also exposing subtle coherent over‑rotations and non‑Markovian effects, the work provides a crucial diagnostic tool for tightening fault‑tolerance thresholds as hardware scales. The ability to map logical‑level errors directly will help both academia and industry refine control strategies and benchmark next‑generation error‑corrected processors.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

Researchers from Sandia National Laboratories and Quantinuum have introduced "Logical GST," a protocol that provides the first self-consistent, full tomographic reconstruction of a universal single-qubit logical gate set. This was implemented on a single logical qubit encoded in the [[7, 1, 3]] Steane code on the Quantinuum System Model H1-1. The study confirmed the assumption that quantum error correction converts complex physical noise into simple, uncorrelated Pauli-stochastic channels, but high-shot emulation revealed small but statistically significant logical non-Markovianity and coherent logical over-rotations.
