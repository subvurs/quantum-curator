Role of the Drive in Mediating Correlations Between Two Qubits Through a Shared Dissipative Cavity

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics August 28, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article shows that the way a cavity is driven can fundamentally reshape the quantum correlations between two qubits that interact only through a shared, lossy mode, revealing a clear pathway from pure entanglement to discord‑only states as temperature rises. By identifying a time‑modulated parametric drive as the sole protocol that actually generates measurable two‑qubit entanglement—while other drives produce robust discord—it connects directly to recent efforts in dissipative engineering and reservoir‑mediated gates, offering a practical knob for tailoring coherence versus correlation trade‑offs in superconducting or trapped‑ion platforms. The temperature‑driven crossover highlighted here underscores how thermal noise can be harnessed rather than merely suppressed, informing the design of more resilient quantum processors that exploit discord for certain tasks while preserving entanglement when needed.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

Using a numerically exact master equation, we demonstrate that two qubits, coupled solely through a shared damped, driven cavity, can become correlated. The drive influences both the amount and the type of correlation. For parametric, coherent, and resonantly modulated drives, the qubits develop quantum discord that increases with cavity temperature, while the logarithmic negativity remains numerically zero. This indicates the presence of discord without entanglement. In contrast, a time-modulated parametric drive is the only one that generates genuine two-qubit entanglement, achieving \(E_\mathcal{N} \simeq 0.15\) and concurrence \(\simeq 0.16\) at \((\eps, γ) = (0.3, 0.2)\), which rises to \(E_\mathcal{N} \simeq 0.32\) in the weak-coupling, moderate-damping region. Heating eventually destroys this entanglement around \(n_{\mathrm{th}} \simeq 0.2\), while discord continues to grow, resulting in a temperature-driven transition from entanglement to discord within a single drive. Moreover, the parametric drive offers the best protection for single-qubit coherence, unlike the coherent and modulated drives. An adiabatic-elimination model indicates that the cavity generates an effective coupling and a collective dephasing channel, both of which increase with temperature, explaining the observed discord without entanglement.
