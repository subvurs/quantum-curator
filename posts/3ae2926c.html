NSF and UKRI Commit $10 Million in Joint Funding for Molecular Quantum Information Science

Source: Quantum Computing Report September 17, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article highlights a rare transatlantic partnership that injects $10 million into molecular quantum information science, a niche where chemistry meets qubit engineering and could unlock scalable spin‑based processors. By funding eight teams to design custom molecules and model quantum state transfer, the program builds on recent advances in molecular spin qubits from both the US and UK, accelerating the move from proof‑of‑concept devices toward chemically tailored hardware. The investment signals growing policy support for bottom‑up quantum architectures, but readers should note that practical quantum computers based on these molecules are still years away as synthesis challenges and coherence times remain open questions.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

The NSF and UKRI have jointly committed $10 million to fund eight transatlantic research teams in molecular quantum information science. This initiative, with $5 million from NSF and £3.7 million from UKRI, focuses on foundational chemistry research to manipulate quantum states in complex molecular architectures. This second phase of funding aims to synthesize custom molecular systems, engineer molecular-scale spin qubits, and model quantum information transfer, directly supporting international research partnerships.
