High-Throughput GPU Decoding of Quantum Bivariate Bicycle Codes under Circuit-Level Noise

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics November 25, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article demonstrates that bivariate bicycle codes—already known for their lower qubit overhead compared to surface codes—can be decoded at microsecond‑scale speeds even under realistic circuit‑level noise, thanks to a highly optimized parallel‑window GPU engine. By achieving a 120× boost over traditional sliding‑window decoders and reducing logical failure rates by up to 36% relative to prior belief‑propagation methods, the work narrows the gap between theoretical error‑correction thresholds and the real‑time processing required for scalable quantum processors. The results suggest that commodity GPUs could become a practical backend for on‑the‑fly decoding in near‑term devices, although performance will still depend on hardware generation and the ability to map larger code families onto similar architectures.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

Bivariate bicycle (BB) codes reduce the physical-qubit overhead of quantum error correction compared to other codes such as Surface codes, but circuit-level faults create dependencies across measurement rounds that limit decoding throughput. We present a parallel-window GPU decoder that combines reusable fault patterns, compact shared-memory state, and persistent GPU workers using min-sum with Relay-BP recovery. On an RTX 5090, the proposed decoder keeps pace with one syndrome round every 1 $μ$s across all four evaluated codes, up to [[144,12,12]] with 63-144 $μ$s median commit delays. This rate has not been demonstrated in prior circuit-level GPU decoding studies and it provides $120\times$ higher decoding throughput than sequential sliding-window decoding. Checking neighboring detector rounds before accepting a decoding result reduces failure rates by up to 65%. For [[144,12,12]] at $p=0.002$, our proposed decoder achieves $7.58\times 10^{-6}$ failures per round, 36% lower than the published belief propagation (BP) with syndrome flipping (BP-SF) result.
