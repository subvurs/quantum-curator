Data informed Ansatz Selection for Variational Quantum Circuits

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics November 03, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This work shows that the architecture of variational quantum circuits can be chosen deterministically from the data itself rather than by costly trial‑and‑error searches, a step that could dramatically shrink the overhead of VQC design on near‑term hardware. By linking Fourier coefficient ranks to entanglement needs and using the growth law of the quantum Fisher information matrix to set depth, the authors provide concrete, analytically grounded rules that complement recent efforts to automate ansatz construction and reduce barren‑plateau risk. The demonstrated 78 % accuracy on a benchmark dataset without any hyperparameter sweep suggests a practical pathway for scaling VQCs to larger problems while keeping experimental resources in check, though performance will still depend on the quality of data encoding and hardware noise levels.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

Choosing a variational quantum circuit (VQC) usually means searching over ansatze and depths, and training each candidate. We give three rules that fix these choices from the data and the gate set alone, before any training. First, a circuit without entangling gates has a rank-one Fourier coefficient tensor on every subset of qubits. A singular value decomposition of a dataset's Fourier coefficients therefore indicates whether entanglement is needed, which qubits it connects, and a lower bound on the error of omitting it. Second, the rank of the quantum Fisher information matrix grows by $p_0$ per layer, after a first-layer deficit $d_0$, until it reaches a ceiling set by the dynamical Lie algebra. This law fixes the depth. Third, reading out all $2^N - 1$ Pauli-Z products from one batch of shots resolves half of the state's directions, the most any single-basis readout can. A single-qubit readout resolves an exponentially small fraction. For the Madelon dataset, the rules prescribe 7 blocks of 3 qubits at depth 3: a 239-parameter classifier with 78.5% test accuracy, found without a grid search. Ablations confirm three predicted effects: shallower circuits, smaller blocks and dropping reuploading all lower accuracy.
