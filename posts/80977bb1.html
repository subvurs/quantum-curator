Horizon Quantum Integrates Triple Alpha IDE with OQC’s Toshiko Processor for Pulse-Level Control

Source: Quantum Computing Report November 09, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article matters because it brings pulse‑level programmability—previously a niche capability—to OQC’s 32‑qubit Toshiko processor via Horizon’s Triple Alpha IDE, giving researchers direct control over timing, calibration and custom gate sequences on the Coaxmon architecture. It follows recent moves by IBM, Rigetti and others to expose low‑level hardware interfaces, signaling that fine‑grained optimization is becoming a standard tool for squeezing out higher fidelities in near‑term devices. While the integration promises faster gate tuning and more experimental flexibility, its impact will initially be limited to users of OQC’s platform and those comfortable with pulse engineering.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

Horizon Quantum Computing and Oxford Quantum Circuits have integrated Horizon's Triple Alpha IDE with OQC's 32-qubit Toshiko processor. This integration allows for pulse-level control, enabling users to execute pulse scheduling, qubit characterization, and automated gate calibration directly within the IDE. This development provides researchers with programmatic access to optimize gate fidelities and deploy custom sequences on OQC's Coaxmon architecture.
