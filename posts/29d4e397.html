Evaluation of the quantum Fisher information for large systems via an ancilla

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics October 21, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article tackles a long‑standing bottleneck by showing how an ancillary qubit can be used to extract the quantum Fisher information of many‑body states without full tomography, opening a scalable route to quantify metrological resources in large processors. By proving conditions for “perfect” evaluation and demonstrating it on GHZ circuits, the work dovetails with recent efforts to benchmark variational algorithms and quantum sensors where QFI serves as a key performance metric. If the protocol can be implemented on near‑term hardware, it could provide experimentalists with a practical tool for assessing entanglement‑enhanced precision without prohibitive measurement overheads, though its reliance on coherent ancilla control may limit immediate applicability to noisy devices.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

Quantum Fisher information (QFI) is a fundamental quantity in quantum information and quantum mechanics, and evaluating it in various physical systems has become an important research topic. However, its evaluation in large systems still faces many challenges. Hereby we propose an ancilla-based protocol for evaluating the QFI of large systems and establish the conditions under which this protocol can ``perfectly'' evaluate the QFI in theory. As a demonstration, the protocol is applied to quantum circuits to evaluate the QFI of the Greenberger-Horne-Zeilinger state.
