Quantum Coherence and Basis-Dependent Correlations in Quantum Systems

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics November 24, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article pinpoints exactly which bipartite states carry no extra coherence beyond what is already present locally, showing that block‑diagonal product structures erase basis‑dependent correlations for both Tsallis‑entropy and quantum‑Fisher‑information coherence measures. By clarifying the structural conditions under which global coherence adds nothing to local resources, it sharpens our understanding of how coherence can be harvested or conserved in multi‑qubit hardware—a key issue as experimental platforms strive to balance entanglement generation against decoherence overhead. The results also provide a useful diagnostic tool for designing quantum processors that need to isolate coherent subspaces without unintentionally introducing hidden correlations. However, the equivalence holds only under specific measurement bases and coherence definitions, so extending the insight to more general settings will be an important next step.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

Quantum coherence and quantum correlations are two fundamental features of quantum systems and are closely related in various quantum information tasks. In general, due to the existence of correlations, the amount of coherence in a bipartite state should be no less than that in the reduced states. We focus on what types of states have an equal amount of coherence in the global state and the local states. For such states, the basis-dependent correlations (the difference between the coherence of a bipartite state and its reduced state with respect to a set of standard orthogonal local bases) vanish. Based on the Tsallis relative entropy coherence measure, we prove that block-diagonal product states exhibit vanishing basis-dependent correlations. Furthermore, we establish an equivalence between the absence of basis-dependent correlations and the block-diagonal product structure in a special case. We then consider the coherence measure based on quantum Fisher information and show that block?diagonal product states again lead to vanishing basis-dependent correlations, while in the two-qubit case the vanishing condition constrains the state to a broader block-diagonal-like product structure. These results provide a structural characterization of bipartite states with vanishing basis-dependent correlations under different coherence measures.
