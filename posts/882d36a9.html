RIKEN and Quantinuum Demonstrate Heterogeneous Multi-QPU Workflows on Fugaku

Source: Quantum Computing Report November 10, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article showcases the first large‑scale heterogeneous quantum workflow that couples a world‑leading classical supercomputer with two fundamentally different QPUs—Quantinuum’s trapped‑ion processor and IBM’s superconducting chip—demonstrating that distinct hardware can be orchestrated to tackle a single scientific problem more efficiently. By cutting the energy error in a 16‑site 2D transverse‑field Ising model, the experiment proves that hybrid multi‑QPU pipelines can extract complementary strengths (high fidelity gates versus fast operation) and may become a practical route toward near‑term quantum advantage. The use of Tierkreis to manage distributed resources also signals a maturing software stack for cross‑platform quantum orchestration, an essential step as larger ecosystems of specialized processors emerge. Readers should note that the demonstration remains at modest qubit counts, so scaling the approach will require further advances in error mitigation and interconnect latency.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

Researchers successfully demonstrated a hybrid quantum-classical computing architecture integrating the Fugaku supercomputer with two distinct quantum processors: Quantinuum's trapped-ion Reimei and IBM's superconducting ibm_kobe. This setup enabled a multi-modality workflow, significantly reducing energy error in a 16-site 2D Transverse-Field Ising Model calculation by combining the strengths of both quantum systems. The Tierkreis workflow management system was adapted to orchestrate these complex computations across geographically separated quantum processing units.
