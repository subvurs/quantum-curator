Optimal fidelity over zero-capacity erasure channels

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics November 23, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article settles a long‑standing question about how well quantum information can survive through zero‑capacity erasure channels by giving an exact formula for the optimal asymptotic entanglement fidelity in the antidegradable regime. By leveraging fractional extendibility to prove a tight upper bound and then constructing permutation‑invariant codes that meet it, the authors demonstrate that even “useless” channels can preserve nontrivial quantum coherence when used with suitably tailored error‑correcting schemes. The result sharpens our theoretical limits for quantum networking over highly lossy links and informs the design of practical codes that approach these limits in future quantum repeaters.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

Antidegradable quantum channels have zero capacity for reliable quantum communication. However, they can nevertheless transmit quantum information with nontrivial fidelity. This raises the question: how well can quantum information be preserved through such channels? In this work, we exactly characterize the optimal asymptotic entanglement fidelity of quantum erasure channels throughout the antidegradable regime. Specifically, we show that it equals $γ+\frac{1-γ}{d}$ for transmission probabilities $0<γ\le \frac{1}{2}$ and logical dimension $d$. We use fractional extendibility to establish an upper bound on the entanglement fidelity achievable by arbitrary error-correcting protocols. Furthermore, we construct a family of permutation-invariant codes that asymptotically saturates this bound.
