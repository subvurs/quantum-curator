On the Origin of Beyond-Classical Advantage in the Parity-Permutation Problem

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics July 31, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article overturns the common assumption that entanglement is the essential ingredient for quantum speed‑ups by showing that, in the parity‑permutation problem, merely increasing the local Hilbert‑space dimension can already yield a beyond‑classical advantage. By proving that product‑state preparations achieve perfect success in locally quantum GPTs and that no amount of entanglement helps below a dimensional threshold, the work clarifies which physical resources truly power probabilistic gains in permutation‑based sensing tasks. The result sharpens our theoretical toolkit for designing low‑entanglement quantum protocols and sets clear limits on what can be achieved with higher‑dimensional but separable systems.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

We investigate the task of identifying the parity (odd vs even) of an unknown permutation applied to $n$ particles. Classically, using fewer than $n$ distinct labels per particle limits the success probability to random guessing, whereas quantum mechanics, exploiting entanglement in both preparation and measurement, accomplishes the task perfectly with as few as $\big\lceil \sqrt{n}\big\rceil$ levels per particle [\href{https://doi.org/10.1103/yhyv-xnwq}{PRL {\bf 135}, 260603 (2025)}]. We show that even without entangled preparation, quantum theory still offers a probabilistic advantage over classical strategies. Moreover, such product preparations yield perfect success in locally quantum theories, where elementary systems are quantum but their composition follows the minimal tensor product structure of generalized probabilistic theories (GPTs). We further identify GPT models that accomplish the task with certainty without requiring entanglement either at the preparation stage or at the measurement stage. Our central result establishes that the linear dimension of the elementary systems, rather than entanglement, is the fundamental resource governing the existence of probabilistic advantage in the permutation parity problem. In particular, below the required dimension threshold, no amount of entanglement can improve upon the random-guessing limit.
