Molecular energies from sparse quantum prolate diagonalization on a trapped-ion quantum processor

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics November 17, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article demonstrates that sparse quantum prolate diagonalization can slash the number of required circuit executions from linear to logarithmic in the autocorrelation‑function samples while preserving chemical‑accuracy, a breakthrough for near‑term quantum chemistry on noisy hardware. By leveraging compressed sensing on Quantinuum’s Helios trapped‑ion processor, the authors achieve chemically accurate energies for an embedded arginine–tryptophan pair using circuits with up to 448 two‑qubit gates, showing that sophisticated algorithmic sparsity can offset hardware limitations. If the approach scales as suggested in weakly correlated regimes, it could dramatically accelerate realistic molecular simulations on larger ion‑trap or superconducting devices, though its performance on strongly correlated systems remains an open question.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

We introduce sparse quantum prolate diagonalization (sparse QPD), which combines the original formulation of QPD [Phys. Rev. Res., 8(3), 033177, (2026)] with compressed sensing to exploit Hamiltonian spectrum sparsity for molecular systems. We show that sparse sampling reduces the sample complexity of QPD from $\mathcal{O}(N_s)$ to $\mathcal{O}(\log N_s)$, where $N_s$ is the number of sampled points of the autocorrelation function (i.e.\ distinct circuits), without increasing circuit depth at chemical accuracy. With a two-orbital active-space-embedded Hamiltonian for an interacting arginine--tryptophan residue pair in water as an example, we show that on the Quantinuum Helios trapped-ion quantum computer, sparse QPD resolves the Hamiltonian energies to chemical accuracy with circuits of up to 448 two-qubit gates. For a suitably large quantum processor, we demonstrate numerically in the weakly correlated regime that the method extends to larger systems.
