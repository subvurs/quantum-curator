Performance of Fusion-Based Quantum Computing with Imperfect Photon Indistinguishability

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics November 17, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article shows that the fault‑tolerance advantage of logical encoding in fusion‑based photonic quantum computers can evaporate when realistic photon‑distinguishability errors are included, highlighting a hidden vulnerability of otherwise promising architectures. By contrasting all‑photonic and emitter‑assisted repeat‑until‑success schemes, the authors demonstrate that the choice of resource‑state generation method can be as decisive for error thresholds as the encoding itself—a nuance that recent photonic hardware roadmaps have largely overlooked. The findings urge experimental teams to prioritize indistinguishability control and to reassess performance projections that assume idealized resource states before scaling up FBQC platforms.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

Fusion-based quantum computation (FBQC) is a promising route to fault-tolerant photonic quantum computing, connecting small entangled resource states through linear-optical fusion measurements. While logical encoding can substantially improve resilience to photon loss, whether these benefits extend to other noise remain an open question. In this work, we show that this is not generally the case when accounting for partial photon distinguishability during resource state generation and fusion measurement. We develop hardware-motivated noise models to capture these effects and investigate their impact on fault tolerance in the 6-ring fusion network. We compare unencoded and Shor(2,2)-encoded resource states prepared using all-photonic and emitter-assisted repeat-until-success (RUS) schemes. For the all-photonic construction, encoding reduces the distinguishability threshold from 0.136% to 0.08%. In contrast, the RUS construction tolerates more distinguishability, with comparable unencoded and encoded thresholds (0.256% and 0.271%, respectively). These results expose a fundamental limitation of evaluating logical encodings under idealized resource-state assumptions and demonstrate that resource-state generation can critically determine the fault tolerance of photonic architectures.
