Quantum subsets discrimination and key sifting with success probability one

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics November 06, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article tackles a long‑standing limitation of quantum cryptography by showing that error‑free discrimination between subsets of non‑orthogonal, even linearly dependent, states can be achieved with unit success probability—a feat previously thought impossible without sacrificing determinism. By turning the subset‑discrimination protocol into an alternative basis‑reconciliation step, it promises sifting efficiencies of 100 % for multi‑basis or high‑dimensional QKD schemes, potentially closing the gap between theoretical key rates and practical implementations. The result builds on recent advances in probabilistic orthogonalization and could reshape how future quantum‑secure networks handle state preparation and measurement, though experimental realization will hinge on implementing the required collective measurements with low overhead.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

Non-distinguishability of non-orthogonal quantum states is the essential feature of quantum mechanics. In particular, one cannot deterministically orthogonalize the set of quantum states, while probabilistic orthogonalization is possible, including unambiguous state discrimination and discrimination between the subsets. Here, we propose a method for error-free subsets discrimination with significantly increased success probability, which may reach one even for non-orthogonal and linearly dependent input states. This method may be applied in quantum cryptography: An alternative basis reconciliation procedure is described, which may provide unit sifting probability even for a large number of bases and for non-orthogonal states within each basis.
