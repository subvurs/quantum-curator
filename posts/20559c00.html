Full-chip constrained quantum annealing via self-calibrating spin-susceptibility control

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics November 20, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article tackles a long‑standing obstacle for quantum annealers—efficiently handling linear constraints without inflating the problem into dense penalty graphs that quickly outstrip the sparse connectivity of current superconducting chips. By exploiting single‑shot spin‑fluctuation covariances to reconstruct dual curvature, the SelfCal‑QSQN scheme eliminates extra slack qubits and penalty couplers, enabling full‑chip feasibility on D‑Wave Advantage devices and delivering performance that rivals time‑matched mixed‑integer solvers at the 256‑spin scale. The result opens a realistic pathway for applying quantum annealing to real‑world constrained optimization in finance, power grids and logistics, though further work will be needed to confirm scalability beyond today’s few‑hundred‑qubit problems.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

Discrete optimization in finance, power grids, and logistics requires minimizing Ising Hamiltonians under coupled linear constraints. However, quadratic-penalty formulations generate dense cliques that exhaust the minor-embedding capacity of sparse superconducting processors at 128-176 spins, and first-order dual subgradient schemes diverge or stall under constraint coupling. Accordingly, this study proposes a closed-loop primal-dual scheme, designated as Self-Calibrating Quantum Susceptibility Quasi-Newton (SelfCal-QSQN), in which linear constraints are encoded in longitudinal fields and dual curvature is reconstructed from single-shot spin-fluctuation covariances. This scheme requires no penalty couplers, slack qubits, or perturbation queries. Evaluated over 891 runs on D-Wave Advantage (5,612 qubits) and Advantage2 (4,560 qubits), SelfCal-QSQN achieves 83.3% full-chip exact feasibility, reduces high-constraint error by 6.1-20.1-fold over $K$-scaled first-order updates (138.4-323.3-fold over fixed-gain curvature), outperforms time-matched mixed-integer programming at 256 spins, and attains 95.0%-96.7% feasibility on non-native and inequality benchmarks. Moreover, the scheme is robust toward shot noise and freeze-out drift.
