End-to-End Quantum Algorithm for Differential Equations: Application to Simulating a Biomolecular System

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics November 19, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Summary

Quantum algorithms for differential equations have the potential for substantial algorithmic speedups over classical methods. However, they rely on certain assumptions and abstract oracle specifications that leave their practical feasibility and computational advantages for realistic applications unclear. Here we formalize the concept of an end-to-end quantum algorithm for differential equations as one that accepts classical inputs and returns classical outputs, and provide implementations for algorithmic primitives in constructing such algorithms. We present a tailored end-to-end quantum algorithm for the linearized Poisson-Boltzmann (LPB) equation, a partial differential equation (PDE) with variable coefficients widely used in simulating a biomolecular system. We explicitly implement all aspects of the algorithm, from preparing an initial state to generating a quantum solution and estimating properties of the system. In particular, we develop a new discrete representation for variable-coefficient PDEs on a grid that enables efficient implementation of the operator oracle, and build on a recent technique based on optimal wavelet preconditioning to generate a quantum solution with a cost independent of the condition number of the discretized operator. We show the algorithm can be implemented by quantum circuits of size polylogarithmic in both the number of grid points $N_g$ and the inverse of the desired accuracy $\varepsilon$, with an overall gate cost polylogarithmic in $N_g$ and quasilinear in $1/\varepsilon$. We also show the classical preprocessing to construct the circuits has polylogarithmic cost in $N_g$ and $1/\varepsilon$. Our work provides a concrete end-to-end quantum algorithm for a PDE of practical value, demonstrates an exponential improvement in scaling with $N_g$ over classical methods, and provides a foundation for assessing its potential quantum advantage in practice.
