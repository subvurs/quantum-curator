Doppler effect on dual-downlink time-bin quantum key distribution and a proposed ephemeris-assisted witness-field correction

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics November 18, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article tackles a key obstacle for space‑based time‑bin QKD—tiny Doppler‑induced phase shifts that can cripple the entangled‑photon correlations needed for secure keys. By showing that each ground station can locally reconstruct and pre‑compensate its own Doppler phase using an ephemeris‑driven witness field, the authors provide a practical feed‑forward solution that avoids real‑time quantum communication between stations. The approach dovetails with recent LEO QKD demonstrations (e.g., Micius, QUESS) by offering a scalable way to preserve high‑visibility interference without bulky hardware upgrades. If experimental noise and timing jitter remain manageable, this technique could accelerate the rollout of global entanglement‑based crypto networks.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

Time-bin entanglement is promising for satellite Quantum Key Distribution (QKD) due to its compatibility with telecom fiber networks, but its phase-sensitive measurements are vulnerable to Doppler effects induced by satellite motion. We present a spectral-temporal framework for time-bin-entangled photon pairs generated onboard a satellite via Spontaneous Parametric Down Conversion (SPDC) and distributed to two Optical Ground Stations (OGSs) over independent downlinks with distinct Doppler shifts. Each OGS is equipped with an Unbalanced Mach-Zehnder Interferometer (UMZI) used to perform Bennett-Brassard-Mermin 1992 (BBM92) entangled-photon QKD. In a simulated Low Earth Orbit (LEO) pass with matched 400 ps analyzer delays, the received time-bin separation changes by at most 8.8 fs per link, while the combined carrier phase shifts by about 38 rad, which primarily degrades phase-basis correlations. After zero-Doppler calibration, this phase separates into independent signal-and idler-link contributions, allowing each station to correct its link Doppler phase shift locally, without real-time phase exchange between the stations. We propose an ephemeris-assisted feed-forward architecture, in which an auxiliary interferometer analyzes a co-propagating classical witness field to reconstruct the local Doppler-induced phase, which a phase modulator then compensates on the quantum channel. In a noise-free model, a 1 Hz sample-and-hold correction rate keeps the phase-basis Quantum Bit Error Rate (QBER) below 2% and retains 93% of the ideal asymptotic key yield. After 18 accumulated passes, the corrected finite secret key reaches 95% of its ideal length, whereas relabeling the parity from the ephemeris without optical correction reaches only about 8%.
