Solving the MAX-$k$-CUT problem for hypergraphs with quantum computing

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics October 30, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article shows how a broad class of combinatorial optimization problems—MAX‑k‑CUT on hypergraphs—can be mapped to standard pairwise Ising models, opening the door for existing quantum algorithms like QAOA to tackle them without bespoke hardware. By demonstrating systematic performance gains across up to 15 QAOA layers on realistic hypergraph instances, the work bridges a gap between theoretical reductions and near‑term quantum simulation on platforms such as IBM’s AerSimulator. The results suggest that even today’s noisy devices can explore richer graph structures, though scaling beyond small hypergraphs will still demand error mitigation and deeper circuits.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

We investigate the MAX-$k$-CUT problem on hypergraphs by considering their representation as spin systems with Ising interactions. The corresponding cost Hamiltonian for MAX-$k$-CUT problem on hypergraphs is reduced to an Ising Hamiltonian with pairwise interactions. This reduction simplifies the solution of the MAX-$k$-CUT problem using quantum computing. As illustrative examples, a 3-uniform tight-star hypergraph and a sunflower hypergraph are considered. The corresponding Hamiltonians are constructed, and quantum protocols for the MAX-$k$-CUT problems are presented. The MAX-3-CUT and MAX-4-CUT instances are investigated for these hypergraphs by varying the number of Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm (QAOA) layers from (p=2) to (p=15) with quantum computing on IBM's Qiskit AerSimulator.
