Using a microwave link to transfer a three-level quantum state

Source: Phys.org Quantum November 18, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article demonstrates the first coherent transfer of a qutrit—a three‑level quantum system— over a superconducting microwave waveguide, showing that higher‑dimensional states can be moved reliably between distant chips. By extending beyond binary qubits, the work opens a path toward more compact encoding schemes and error‑correcting codes that exploit the extra Hilbert space, complementing recent advances in microwave photon routing and modular quantum processors. If the technique scales to larger networks, it could reduce the overhead of entangling many qubits and accelerate the construction of distributed quantum computers, though maintaining fidelity across longer links will remain a key challenge.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

Quantum technologies, devices and systems that process, store, transfer or collect information leveraging the laws of quantum mechanics, could outperform their classical counterparts on some real-world tasks. Many of these technologies encode quantum information in qubits, which can exist in either of two basic states or a superposition of them.
