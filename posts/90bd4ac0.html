Spin-adapted sample-based quantum diagonalization with randomized time evolution

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics November 13, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article shows that enforcing exact spin symmetry can turn a generic sample‑based diagonalization scheme into a dramatically more efficient quantum chemistry tool, shrinking the required subspace by two to three orders of magnitude without sacrificing accuracy. By adapting the SqDRIFT protocol—already popular for its low‑depth randomized Trotterisation—to respect S², the authors bridge a gap between early‑fault‑tolerant hardware and chemically relevant problems that demand spin‑pure states. The result is a practical pathway to obtain high‑quality excited‑state energies on modest quantum processors, though the approach still relies on sufficient sampling depth to capture the full symmetry‑closed configuration space.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

Quantum algorithms based on the classical post-processing of measurement samples have emerged as effective and robust methods to approximate low-energy wavefunctions of many-body quantum systems on present-day and early-fault-tolerant quantum devices. This paper introduces a generic framework for enforcing spin symmetry within these approaches. We tailor this framework to SqDRIFT, a sample-based quantum diagonalization variant that uses the qDRIFT randomized compilation protocol to generate the quantum samples. We discuss how three key components of SqDRIFT (the initial-state preparation, the construction of the time-evolution circuits, and the classical post-processing) can be adapted to incorporate spin symmetry. We show that spin-pure circuits alone are not sufficient, since the sampled determinants generally span a subspace that is not closed under $\hat{S}^2$, and we restore this closure by completing the sampled electronic configurations. In classical simulations of 4-aminobenzonitrile and of an $[\text{Fe}(\text{NH}_{3})_{4}]^{2+}$ model complex, and in experiments on an IBM quantum processor for the oxo-Mn(Salen) complex, this spin-adapted post-processing yields roots that are exact spin eigenfunctions at every subspace size. On hardware, it does so with diagonalized subspaces two to three orders of magnitude smaller than those of the conventional constructions for the same circuit depth.
