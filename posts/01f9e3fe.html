Tessa: Encoding Classical Data Across an Entire Quantum Processor

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics November 13, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article matters because it tackles the long‑standing data‑loading bottleneck by showing how to map thousands of classical values directly onto a quantum processor without any SWAP routing, leveraging the native coupling graph of the hardware. By extending earlier QPIXL and QCrank ideas into a checkerboard layout that pairs address and data qubits, Tessa demonstrates scalable state preparation on IBM’s Phoenix and Fez devices—an advance that could accelerate near‑term quantum‑machine‑learning and quantum‑simulation workflows. The approach is still constrained by readout noise and the degree of local connectivity, but it proves that device‑aware encoding can be realized today on real hardware.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

We present Tessa, a hardware-aware, device-wide scheme to encode classical data on quantum computers. We use it to experimentally encode and decode up to $1568$ real values using IBM's Phoenix and Fez quantum computers on a variety of data. Tessa's construction is an extension of QPIXL and QCrank. However, it checkerboards address and data qubits across a coupling graph with neighbouring data qubits sharing only a subset of address qubits. When using the device's coupling graph no SWAP gates or routing is required, but the construction works for any bipartite coupling graph. The shots required for readout are determined by the local coupling degree. As examples, we have used cat states (pixel art of cats), various pixel art, audio, MIDI, DNA sequences and protein amino acid sequences.
