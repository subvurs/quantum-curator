Essentially optimal gate teleportation

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics August 25, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article reports the first deterministic LO C C protocol that implements an arbitrary two‑qubit controlled‑phase gate with entanglement consumption essentially matching the theoretical minimum for every rotation angle, even vanishingly small ones. By breaking the Clifford‑only barrier and showing that non‑Clifford phases can be teleported with almost no ebit when φ≈0, it paves the way for far more resource‑efficient distributed quantum processors and modular hardware architectures. The result sharpens our understanding of the entanglement–communication trade‑off in gate teleportation, although experimental realization will still need to address practical issues such as high‑fidelity shared states and fast classical feed‑forward.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

Gate teleportation allows us to implement a nonlocal unitary using local operations, classical communication (LOCC), and a shared entangled state. Known deterministic teleportation protocols consume at least one full ebit and achieve optimal entanglement consumption only for Clifford gates. Here, we present a deterministic LOCC protocol for implementing the two-qubit controlled-phase gate $U_φ=\mathrm{diag}(1,1,1,e^{i φ})$ with $φ\in [0,π]$ whose entanglement consumption is close to optimal for every $φ$. In particular, vanishing rotation angles require vanishing entanglement.
