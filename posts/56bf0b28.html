Quantum Autoencoder on Real Hardware for New Physics Searches at Muon Colliders

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics November 19, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article marks one of the first demonstrations that a quantum autoencoder can be run on real‑world hardware to tackle a high‑energy physics problem, showing that a six‑qubit circuit on the Tianyan176 processor already distinguishes Standard Model tri‑photon events from anomalous quartic gauge‑coupling signatures. By delivering reconstruction‑loss based discrimination comparable to classical analyses, it highlights how near‑term quantum machine‑learning tools could become part of the data‑analysis pipeline for future muon colliders, where massive event rates will demand ultra‑efficient pattern recognition. The work also underscores current limitations—noise and qubit count still constrain scalability—but sets a clear benchmark for how quantum‑enhanced searches for new physics might evolve as hardware improves.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

We apply a quantum autoencoder to search for new physics (NP) in the tri-photon process $μ^+μ^-\toγγγ$ at muon colliders. Using a six-qubit circuit on the Tianyan176 quantum computer, we measure the reconstruction losses for Standard Model (SM) events and anomalous quartic gauge couplings (aQGCs) events. The results demonstrate that the hardware-implemented quantum autoencoder effectively discriminates NP signals from the SM background. Concurrently, we derive the expected constraints on the aQGCs coefficients and compare them with those obtained from conventional methods.
