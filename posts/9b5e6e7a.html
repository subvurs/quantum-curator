Quantum simulation of low-temperature impurity models

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics November 23, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article presents the first quantum algorithms that can prepare low‑temperature thermal states of impurity models with polynomial scaling in the inverse temperature and only polylogarithmic dependence on precision, eliminating the exponential β overhead that has limited previous Gibbs samplers. By enabling efficient ground‑state and finite‑temperature simulations of the core building blocks of dynamical mean‑field theory, the work brings quantum advantage for studying strongly correlated materials within reach. The Heisenberg‑limited estimation of static and dynamical properties further shows that practical material‑science calculations could soon benefit from near‑term quantum processors, provided the impurity Hamiltonian has a suitable spectral gap.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

Quantum impurity models, which describe a small but strongly-interacting subsystem coupled to a large noninteracting bath, are the computational core of dynamical mean field theory and play an important role in simulating strongly correlated materials. We develop quantum algorithms for efficiently simulating these models at low temperatures and with high precision. For any impurity model on $n$ fermionic modes with fixed impurity size: (1) We give a quantum algorithm that prepares the thermal state at inverse temperature $β$ to precision $δ$ in time $\mathrm{poly}(n,β,\log(1/δ))$. This algorithm is the first to achieve both polynomial scaling in the inverse temperature and polylogarithmic scaling in the precision. When the Hamiltonian has an inverse-polynomial spectral gap, our quantum algorithm prepares its ground state to precision $δ$ in time $\mathrm{poly}(n,\log(1/δ))$. (2) We further give a quantum algorithm that prepares a purification of the thermal state in time $\mathrm{poly}(n,β,\log(1/δ))$. Using this purification, we estimate static and dynamical properties with error $δ$ in time polynomial in $n$ and $β$ and linear in $1/δ$ up to polylogarithmic factors, achieving the Heisenberg scaling. Our algorithms build on quantum Gibbs samplers, which are quantum analogues of Markov chain Monte Carlo methods. By using global jump operators instead of local ones, we avoid the $\exp(β)$ overhead commonly associated with quantum Gibbs samplers, making them efficient for impurity models even at low temperatures.
