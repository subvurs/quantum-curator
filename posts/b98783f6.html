RIKEN Demonstrates High-Fidelity Simultaneous Control of Silicon Spin Qubits Above 99.9% Threshold

Source: Quantum Computing Report October 17, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article reports a milestone for silicon‑based quantum processors by demonstrating simultaneous control of five spin qubits with fidelities exceeding the 99.9 % fault‑tolerance threshold using a single microwave line and a novel calibration scheme that suppresses AC‑Stark crosstalk. By meeting the error‑correction benchmark in a compact, CMOS‑compatible platform, the work builds on recent advances from Intel and other groups that have pushed individual silicon qubit fidelities toward the same level, bringing scalable, chip‑integrated quantum hardware closer to reality. The result is promising for larger arrays, though extending the technique beyond five qubits and integrating full error‑correcting codes will be the next critical challenges.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

Researchers at RIKEN and collaborators have achieved high-fidelity simultaneous control of five silicon spin qubits, maintaining over 99.9% accuracy. They overcame crosstalk caused by AC-Stark shifts through a new calibration protocol, enabling the use of a single microwave control line. This breakthrough meets the fault-tolerant threshold for quantum error correction and paves the way for more scalable quantum computers.
