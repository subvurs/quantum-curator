Decoy-state quantum key distribution with a leaky Mach-Zehnder receiver

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics November 03, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article tackles a long‑standing gap in quantum‑key‑distribution security by rigorously proving that phase‑encoded decoy‑state BB84 remains secure even when the Mach‑Zehnder receiver suffers from beam‑splitter imbalance and leaks detailed detection information. By deriving a loss‑independent correction to the phase‑error rate, the authors extend recent “leaky‑receiver” frameworks and make them applicable to practical, imperfect hardware—a crucial step toward commercial QKD systems that can tolerate real‑world imperfections. Summary

We prove the security of phase-encoded decoy-state BB84 quantum key distribution for a Mach-Zehnder receiver with imbalanced beam splitters and two threshold detectors that leak fine-grained information about the detection patterns. For this, we derive a lower bound on Bob's single-photon contributions and combine it with the decoy-state method and a recent phase-error framework for leaky receivers. The leakage-induced correction to the phase-error rate is independent of channel loss, providing a loss-tolerant treatment of receiver leakage even in the presence of beam-splitter imbalance. Our results provide a practical route toward loss-tolerant security proofs for imperfect QKD receivers in the presence of receiver leakage.
