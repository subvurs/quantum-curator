Rapid high-purity loading of barium ion via relayed selectivity from naturally abundant source

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics November 09, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article demonstrates a UV‑free three‑step photoionization protocol that can load a single barium isotope with >99 % purity directly from a natural source, eliminating the need for costly isotopically enriched ovens and reducing background contamination. By exploiting narrow‑band resonances at 791 nm and 554 nm before ionizing with an existing 532 nm Raman beam, the authors achieve both rapid loading rates and “relayed selectivity,” a technique that could be adapted to other trapped‑ion species as labs push toward larger, mixed‑isotope quantum processors. The result speeds up qubit initialization and simplifies hardware, addressing a bottleneck that has limited scaling in ion‑trap architectures despite recent advances in high‑fidelity gates and modular trap designs.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

Trapped ions is one of the promising platforms for realizing quantum information processing and quantum simulation. Selective and rapid loading of a specific isotope lays the critical foundation for such purpose particularly when scaling up the system size. Here we demonstrate a new method for rapid isotope-selective loading of $^{137}\mathrm{Ba}^{+}$ or $^{135}\mathrm{Ba}^{+}$, two types of promising ion qubits, from naturally abundant source using a three-step, ultraviolet-free photoionization scheme. Neutral atoms are selected on the narrow 791 nm $6s^2\,{}^1S_0\rightarrow6s6p\,{}^3P_1^o$ transition, resonantly transferred at 554 nm to $6s6d\,{}^3D_1$, and ionized using an existing 532 nm Raman beam. A spectral-overlap analysis based on isotope and hyperfine shifts shows relayed selectivity that the second step can further suppress undesired-isotope excitation and no contaminant ion is detected during our entire experiment. Besides, the resonant drive of the second step excitation yields a prominent loading rate of the selected species. This strategy using multi-step excitations to establish relayed selectivity can be extended to high-purity loading of other species of ions.
