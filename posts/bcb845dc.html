Quantum Codes for Distributed Quantum Fault Sensing

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics November 17, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article shows that carefully designed quantum‑code states can dramatically boost the ability to pinpoint a faulty qubit in a large modular processor, achieving up to a 50 % improvement over naïve independent probes without any protected ancilla memory. By proving a precise criterion for optimal distributed fault sensing and quantifying how much entanglement depth actually helps, it bridges recent advances in quantum error‑correcting codes with the practical need for fast, reliable diagnostics in scalable hardware. The results suggest that modestly sized entangled probe groups—rather than full‑scale Bell pairs—can deliver near‑optimal detection while keeping overheads realistic, a key step toward fault‑tolerant modular architectures. However, the benefit caps at about 95 % of the theoretical optimum unless many qubits participate in larger entangled blocks, highlighting a trade‑off between circuit complexity and sensing performance that engineers will need to balance.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

Targeted recovery in modular quantum processors requires identifying the component affected by a fault. Independent qubits can monitor faults through repeated preparation and measurement. Bell-pair probes improve the guaranteed correct-site probability for unknown local channels, but require partners kept away from the fault. Can entanglement among exposed probes supply this advantage without protected storage? We establish a quantum-code criterion for optimal distributed fault sensing of one unknown local qubit channel with strength in a prescribed positive range. For one interrogation with zero false alarms and no background noise, suitable code states attain the optimal worst-case correct-site probability, improving on independent probes by 50% with every qubit exposed. Exact attainment is equivalent to mutually orthogonal input and single-qubit Pauli error subspaces. Pairwise entanglement gives no improvement, and entanglement depth at most four cannot exceed 95.54% of the optimum without protected ancillas. Approaching the optimum without protected ancillas also requires a number of probes proportional to the array size to participate in larger entangled factors. For a persistent fault under memoryless depolarizing background of fixed strength 0 < eta < 1, independent probes already attain the optimal logarithmic interrogation scaling at fixed success between zero and one and a positive false-alarm budget. In three 1000-site comparisons with specified readouts, an encoded-probe protocol certifies fewer interrogations than independent probes and only one more than optimally decoded ideal Bell pairs, while storing no protected qubits.
