Experimental simulation of a Hilbert-space inner-product change

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics November 16, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article marks the first experimental demonstration of a controllable change in a qubit’s Hilbert‑space inner product, moving beyond earlier PT‑symmetric simulations that kept the metric fixed and opening a new route for probing non‑Hermitian physics on quantum hardware. By implementing the IP‑change channel with both a qutrit encoding and a two‑qubit Stinespring dilation, and by establishing a rigorous fidelity threshold that excludes trivial mixtures of unitaries, the work shows how subtle metric transformations can be verified even in noisy superconducting processors. The clear benefit of readout error mitigation—raising successful instances from 14 to 24 out of 40—highlights an immediate practical lesson for any near‑term experiment that seeks to explore exotic quantum dynamics beyond standard unitary evolution.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

We simulate an effective change in a single qubit's inner product (IP) by implementing the channel $\mathcal{F}^η$ on a superconducting quantum processor. Whereas previous experiments simulated parity-time (PT)-symmetric dynamics with a fixed IP, this experiment implements the IP-change channel itself for metrics associated with PT-symmetric systems. Building on an earlier adversarial verification approach, we derive a process-fidelity threshold that rules out mixtures of qubit unitary operations with random discarding of runs. We test the channel for 40 metrics using a single qutrit and a two-qubit Stinespring dilation. The measured fidelities exceed the threshold in 24 of 40 qutrit instances after readout error mitigation, compared with 16 of 40 unmitigated two-qubit instances. Without mitigation, 14 of 40 qutrit instances pass. These results demonstrate a direct simulation of an effective Hilbert-space IP change and its verification against a specified adversarial class, while showing the importance of readout treatment in comparing the encodings.
