New theoretical model allows for sustained entanglement between diamond qubits at greater separations

Source: Phys.org Quantum November 24, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article reports a theoretical scheme that could keep nitrogen‑vacancy centers in diamond entangled over distances far beyond the nanometer scale by using continuous microwave driving while the crystal lattice stays thermalized. By achieving steady‑state, long‑range entanglement without requiring fragile photonic links, the work tackles a key bottleneck for solid‑state quantum networks and complements recent experimental advances in remote NV coupling via phonons or waveguides. If experimentally realized, the approach would broaden the architectural options for scalable quantum processors and distributed sensing, though practical implementation will still need to address microwave control noise and material decoherence.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

In a new study accepted in the journal Physical Review B, a team of physicists from the University of Delaware may have found a way to overcome limitations in maintaining the entanglement of diamond qubits. The model involves driving the qubits away from equilibrium using microwave radiation while the surrounding material is allowed to remain in equilibrium. The approach allows for both long-range and steady-state entanglement of the qubits. The paper is published on the arXiv preprint server.
