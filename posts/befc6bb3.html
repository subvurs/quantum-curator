Design and Modeling of the Charge Readout of a SiMOS Quantum Dot with a Single Electron Transistor and CryoCMOS

Source: arXiv Condensed Matter August 28, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article demonstrates a concrete step toward truly scalable silicon‑based quantum processors by marrying a single‑electron‑transistor sensor with a purpose‑built cryogenic CMOS readout ASIC (QNDR1), eliminating the bulky RF hardware that has limited integration density in current spin‑qubit experiments. By delivering amplification and digitization at millikelvin temperatures, the design leverages mature CMOS fabrication—mirroring recent Intel and IBM efforts—to enable thousands of parallel readout channels with minimal thermal load and wiring complexity. If the simulated performance translates to silicon‑MOS devices, it could accelerate the deployment of large‑scale spin‑qubit arrays for both quantum computing and high‑energy‑physics detector applications, though real‑world noise and reliability at cryogenic temperatures will still need experimental validation.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

Single electron spin qubits trapped in SiMOS quantum dots are a promising technology for scaling to thousand- or million-qubit systems due to their compatibility with mature CMOS manufacturing processes. A readout system that combines a single electron transistor with a custom cryogenic CMOS amplification and digitization chain offers key advantages by avoiding the use of bulky RF components or room-temperature interconnects. We present design techniques and simulation results for an optimized qubit-SET cryoCMOS interface, culminating in the design of the QNDR1 ASIC, the first cryogenic readout ASIC designed under the Quandarum project, which targets the development of a many-channel spin qubit based detector for use in high energy physics.
