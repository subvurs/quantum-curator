MIT Bengaluru Inaugurates Q-HUB@MAHE and Deploys QuantrolOx’s Open-Architecture VIDYAQAR Quantum System

Source: Quantum Computing Report October 17, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article marks a significant step for India’s quantum ecosystem by pairing a university‑scale research hub with QuantrolOx’s open‑architecture VIDYAQAR system, the first 25‑qubit dilution‑refrigerated platform that can be programmed via custom Python workflows. By embedding an extensible hardware stack into the new Q‑HUB@MAHE, the initiative accelerates both hands‑on training of a hundred quantum engineers and rapid benchmarking of components needed for the nation’s roadmap toward 1,000‑plus qubits. The open architecture also lowers barriers for academic groups to experiment with control electronics and error‑mitigation techniques that have so far been confined to proprietary labs. While the system’s modest qubit count limits immediate computational advantage, its flexibility positions it as a valuable testbed for scaling strategies and software–hardware co‑design in the coming years.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

Manipal Institute of Technology (MIT Bengaluru) has inaugurated the Manipal House of Quantum (Q-HUB@MAHE) in Bengaluru, India, on October 13, 2026. This facility features QuantrolOx’s VIDYAQAR open-architecture quantum system, a 25-qubit-capable dilution refrigeration platform that is part of a national roadmap to scale to over 1,000 qubits. The initiative aims to train 100 quantum engineers by December 2026 and allows researchers to test and benchmark quantum components using custom Python workflows.
