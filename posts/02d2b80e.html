D-Wave Demonstrates 3D Analog-Digital Quantum Simulation of Higher-Order Topological Insulators Using 728 Qubits

Source: Quantum Computing Report October 17, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article shows that D‑Wave’s Advantage2 processor can now run a true three‑dimensional analog‑digital simulation of higher‑order topological insulators on a scale of 728 qubits, marking the first time such bulk topological invariants have been extracted with quantum annealing hardware. By marrying analog annealing with digital control pulses, the work expands the toolbox for programmable condensed‑matter simulators and puts D‑Wave’s platform in direct competition with gate‑model approaches that are still limited to far smaller lattices. The result hints at faster exploration of exotic materials, though it remains confined to Hamiltonians that map naturally onto the annealer’s connectivity and noise profile.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

D-Wave has successfully demonstrated 3D analog-digital quantum simulation of higher-order topological insulators using up to 728 qubits on an Advantage2 superconducting quantum annealing processor. This achievement, detailed in a research study on arXiv, involved new analog-digital quantum control protocols to measure localized boundary modes and extract 3D bulk topological invariants. This breakthrough confirms the utility of analog-digital quantum annealing hardware as a programmable simulator for condensed matter physics and topological materials.
