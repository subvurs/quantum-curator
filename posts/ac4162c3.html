Correlations carried and controlled by coupled non-Markovian environments

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics November 10, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article introduces a numerically exact “local hierarchy of pure states” method that can capture both memory effects and spatial correlations in non‑Markovian environments—a capability that has been missing from most open‑system tools until now. By showing how photon hopping between cavity modes can ferry quantum information and generate entanglement across otherwise isolated spins, the work links recent advances in cavity‑QED platforms and feedback‑based control to practical routes for engineering long‑range correlations in quantum simulators and sensors. The approach also dovetails with tensor‑network techniques, suggesting a scalable way to explore many‑body dynamics where coherent environmental transport plays a decisive role.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

An environment can retain quantum information and carry it between spatially separated systems. Describing the combined influence of these processes requires a treatment of both environmental memory and spatial correlations. Here, we formulate a local hierarchy of pure states for quantum systems coupled to spatially correlated, non-Markovian environments whose modes themselves exchange excitations. We use this method to describe the reduced spin dynamics of a chain of locally coupled Rabi spin--cavity pairs motivated by cavity-QED implementations, where photon hopping in the eliminated cavity environment generates correlations and entanglement between otherwise isolated spins. Monitoring intermediate cavities and feeding their measurement records back onto the corresponding spins further enhances correlations between endpoint spins that receive no direct feedback. The delayed response when feedback starts, and continued entanglement growth after it stops, support a mechanism in which coherent environmental dynamics carry the effects of earlier feedback to the endpoint spins. Finally, tensor-network simulations of XXZ and Heisenberg chains show how environmental transport changes many-body correlation patterns. These results show how coupled environments and local feedback can control correlations between distant spins. We provide a numerically exact stochastic pure-state framework for simulating these processes, opening up avenues to explore the coherent and dissipative control of interacting non-Markovian many-body systems.
