Optimal Error corrected Hamiltonian estimation with nuisance parameters

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics November 19, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This work overturns a long‑standing assumption that nuisance parameters must be locally suppressed in error‑corrected quantum sensors, showing instead that only Markovian noise needs active correction while coherent control can simply separate unwanted drifts from the signal over the full interrogation time. By proving a necessary and sufficient condition for Heisenberg‑limited precision under realistic Hamiltonian dynamics, the authors expand the parameter regimes where quadratic scaling is achievable—an advance that could immediately inform the design of next‑generation atomic clocks, magnetometers, and distributed sensor networks. The result also clarifies why recent experiments using dynamical decoupling or tailored pulse sequences have succeeded despite residual systematic drifts, pointing to a broader, more flexible blueprint for robust quantum metrology.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

Realistic quantum sensing is often affected by both noise and unknown nuisance parameters. Under Markovian dynamics, existing error correction approaches have often sought to suppress nuisance responses alongside environmental noise. Here we show that this local suppression of nuisance parameters is unnecessary and can be overly restrictive: nuisance parameters and Markovian noise impose fundamentally different constraints and require distinct treatments. We establish a general framework for quantum sensing with parameters encoded in a general time dependent Hamiltonian, in the presence of both nuisance parameters and Markovian noise. We derive a necessary and sufficient condition for Heisenberg scaling, determine the exact optimal precision, and construct protocols that attain it. Our results show that to achieve Heisenberg scaling Markovian noise must be suppressed locally by quantum error correction, whereas nuisance parameters need not, and in general should not, be eliminated locally. Instead, optimally designed coherent control can separate their accumulated effects from the target signal over the full sensing evolution. Consequently, Heisenberg scaling can remain attainable even when the target signal is instantaneously aligned with nuisance parameter directions, in sharp contrast to alignment with Markovian noise. This distinction substantially enlarges the regime in which Heisenberg scaling is attainable and provides a general principle for designing robust quantum sensors.
