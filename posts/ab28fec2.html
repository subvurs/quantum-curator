HTM: A Hybrid Classical-Quantum Computation Model

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics October 27, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article matters because it offers the first formal framework—HTM—that unifies classical and quantum control logic into a single state‑transition model, something that has been missing despite growing interest in quantum‑accelerated workflows. By introducing standardized representations for hybrid states and a complexity metric based on entanglement and superposition, HTM directly addresses current pain points such as ad‑hoc task partitioning and hardware‑agnostic scheduling, complementing recent efforts like Qiskit Runtime and Azure Quantum’s orchestration services. If adopted, the model could streamline compiler design and system architecture across platforms, although its practical impact will hinge on how quickly near‑term quantum processors can support the required control‑sharing mechanisms.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

Classical and quantum computers have achieved collaborative computation and completed applications across numerous fields. Nevertheless, the absence of a unified hybrid computation model has led to issues such as non-standardized engineering architecture design, ambiguous task partitioning criteria and so on. To address these issues, this research proposes a hybrid classical-quantum computation model (HTM). By constructing unified state sets and transition function representations, HTM clarifies hybrid control logic and implements a control-sharing mechanism. Simultaneously, a comprehensive complexity calculation method based on entanglement and superposition was proposed, supporting computation task partitioning and resource scheduling. The HTM fills a theoretical gap in the classical-quantum hybrid computation area and implements the joint promotion and development of computing models and hardware.
