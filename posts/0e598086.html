Kerr nonlinearity and three-wave mixing in superconducting resonators hosting Al-InAs weak links

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics August 28, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article shows that a superconducting resonator built around an Al‑capped InAs nanowire weak link can be tuned so its unwanted Kerr (quartic) nonlinearity vanishes while retaining a sizable cubic term, enabling clean three‑wave mixing on demand. By demonstrating flux‑ and gate‑controlled suppression of the Kerr shift together with robust parametric conversion, the work offers a new route to low‑noise amplifiers and bosonic‑mode processors that sidesteps the trade‑offs seen in conventional SNAIL or JRM designs. The results also validate semiconductor–superconductor hybrids as a versatile hardware platform, although the underlying Andreev‑bound‑state model remains qualitative and will need further refinement for predictive circuit design.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

Nonlinear microwave resonators are a versatile tool in quantum information processing, enabling parametric amplification, continuous variable quantum computing, and engineered mode interactions. Many of these applications especially benefit from cubic nonlinearities enabling three-wave mixing; at the same time, they are limited by quartic nonlinearities giving rise to undesired Kerr effects. A recurrent challenge is therefore to engineer resonators with a finite cubic nonlinearity while suppressing quartic terms. Here, we investigate a superconducting resonator hosting two weak links fabricated from an aluminum-capped indium arsenide nanowire. We characterize the Kerr nonlinearity as a function of magnetic flux and gate bias, showing that it can be tuned to zero with either control parameter. Furthermore, we experimentally demonstrate three-wave mixing in a semiconductor-superconductor hybrid device, establishing nonzero cubic nonlinearity. An effective model based on Andreev bound states qualitatively captures the observed trends. Our results validate semiconductor-superconductor hybrid devices as a promising platform for tunable nonlinear superconducting circuits, with applications in parametric amplification, quantum control of bosonic modes, and engineering interactions between microwave modes.
