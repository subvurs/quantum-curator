Highly indistinguishable photons from a tin-vacancy spin qubit in diamond

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics July 24, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article shows that tin‑vacancy (SnV) centers in diamond can emit single photons with raw Hong‑Ou‑Mandel visibilities above 0.95 and an intrinsic indistinguishability approaching 0.999, a level of purity that rivals the best solid‑state sources while retaining long spin coherence times. By confirming that quantum frequency conversion to the telecom C‑band preserves this indistinguishability, the work directly tackles two major hurdles for scalable quantum repeaters: low‑loss fiber transmission and high‑fidelity photonic interference. The results position SnV centers as a compelling alternative to silicon‑vacancy or nitrogen‑vacancy platforms for long‑distance quantum networks, though further engineering will be needed to eliminate the remaining technical noise that limits practical deployment.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

Quantum networks promise secure communication, distributed sensing and modular quantum computing by interconnecting distant quantum nodes through photonic links. Extending such networks beyond metropolitan distances requires quantum repeaters to overcome the exponential attenuation of photons in optical fiber. Across all architectures, a key requirement is the indistinguishability of single photons, which directly impacts the fidelity of photonic operations based on two-photon interference, such as Bell-state measurements and fusion gates. Here, we demonstrate generation of highly indistinguishable single photons from a coherently excited tin-vacancy center in diamond, achieving raw Hong-Ou-Mandel interference visibilities exceeding 0.95. By separating intrinsic emitter properties from technical imperfections, we show that decoherence plays a negligible role and that the remaining limitations are predominantly technical in nature, arriving at an intrinsic indistinguishability of up to 0.999. We further show that quantum frequency conversion to the telecom C-band preserves the photon indistinguishability. In combination with the long-lived electron and nuclear spin coherence times, these results establish tin-vacancy centers in diamond as a competitive platform for long-distance quantum networks and photonic quantum information processing. We further substantiate this potential through Monte Carlo simulations of a quantum-repeater link, demonstrating that the SnV-center platform surpasses the bound set by direct transmission.
