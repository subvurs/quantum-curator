Tunable Ground-State Entanglement in Fully Connected Quantum Systems: From Logarithmic to Volume-Law Scaling

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics November 19, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article shows that by simply tweaking the strength‑scaling exponent of all‑to‑all couplings, one can continuously dial a system’s ground‑state entanglement from the familiar logarithmic bound up to a full volume law, something previously thought impossible for gapped, nondegenerate states. The result bridges theoretical work on Kac‑normalized models with the practical reality of hardware platforms such as trapped‑ion and superconducting arrays that naturally exhibit long‑range interactions, offering a new knob to engineer entanglement complexity without changing the underlying connectivity. It also warns that even modest departures from the canonical normalization can erase universal entanglement limits, a consideration that will matter for designing scalable quantum simulators and error‑corrected architectures.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

Entanglement scaling is a central probe of the complexity of quantum many-body ground states, yet once geometric locality is absent, it can depend sensitively on the structure and normalization of interactions. For fully connected Hamiltonians with the natural Kac normalization, nondegenerate gapped ground states are known to obey a logarithmic entanglement bound. Here we ask how the ground-state entanglement changes when this interaction normalization is continuously relaxed away from the Kac scale. We answer this question by constructing an explicit all-to-all bipartite Hamiltonian of $n$ qubits with Fourier pairing amplitudes and pairwise interaction strength proportional to $n^{-κ}$. For sufficiently small fixed coupling, we prove that every pure ground state has entanglement entropy $Θ(n^{2-2κ} \log n)$ for $1/2<κ\leq1$ and $Θ(n)$ at $κ=1/2$. We further show that the ground state is unique and separated from the rest of the spectrum by a system-size-independent gap for $3/4 \leq κ\leq 1$, while weak-coupling analysis and numerical calculations suggest that the gap remains open also for $1/2 \leq κ< 3/4$. Thus, varying only the interaction normalization yields a controlled hierarchy from logarithmic scaling at the natural Kac normalization $κ=1$ to increasingly strong subextensive entanglement and ultimately to a volume law, showing that even an arbitrarily weak relaxation of this normalization precludes a universal logarithmic entanglement bound.
