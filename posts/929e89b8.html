NowSecure Benchmark Finds 97% of Mobile Applications Lack Post-Quantum Cryptography Ahead of Mandated Timelines

Source: Quantum Computing Report October 24, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article spotlights a glaring “mobile gap” in the race to quantum‑resistant security, showing that almost all consumer apps still rely on classic algorithms that could be broken once scalable quantum computers arrive. The finding dovetails with recent industry pushes—such as NIST’s PQC standardization and upcoming US federal mandates—to embed post‑quantum primitives across software stacks, underscoring that mobile SDKs and third‑party libraries are the weak link many security tools overlook. For developers and enterprises, the data is a wake‑up call to audit dependencies now and begin integrating vetted PQC schemes before compliance deadlines tighten. However, the transition will be paced by the availability of performant, interoperable PQC implementations that can meet the tight latency constraints of mobile environments.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

A recent study by NowSecure, evaluating over 136,000 mobile apps, reveals that 97% lack Post-Quantum Cryptography (PQC), leaving them vulnerable to future quantum attacks. This "mobile gap" is largely due to reliance on third-party SDKs and compiled libraries that traditional security scans miss. With regulatory deadlines approaching (e.g., US federal agencies by 2030-2031), organizations urgently need to prioritize PQC migration for mobile applications.
