Sequential strategy for depolarizing strength estimation

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics November 16, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article shows that repeatedly feeding the same probe through a depolarizing channel can linearly boost the quantum Fisher information for very low noise and even let a single‑qubit probe outperform optimal two‑qubit schemes once three or more repetitions are used, highlighting a surprisingly simple route to sharper noise‑parameter estimates. By identifying an optimal finite number of channel applications in the weak‑noise regime, the work connects directly to current efforts to calibrate and mitigate errors on NISQ hardware where precise depolarizing‑strength knowledge is essential for error‑correction thresholds and quantum sensing protocols. The results suggest that sequential encoding—already easy to implement on existing platforms—could become a practical tool for faster, more accurate characterization of noisy channels without the overhead of entangled multi‑qubit probes.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

We study the advantages of applying a sequential strategy for enhancing the precision in estimating the strength $p$ of a depolarizing channel, using qudit and two-qubit probes. The sequential strategy requires that the same depolarizing channel is repeatedly applied $M$ times to the quantum probe. From the analysis of the probe's output state, both the quantum Fisher information (QFI) and its regularization RQFI, given from multiplication by $p$, are analyzed. We determine that in the limit of $p \to 0$, the QFI is {\it linearly amplified} with the number of channel repetitions. Instead, in the weak-noise regime, the QFI is maximized at a finite value of $M$. Then, the RQFI decreases more quickly with $p$ as $M$ increases. In particular, for two-qubit probes, the RQFI decreases with the depolarization strength proportionally to the amount of correlation between the qubits. The greater the number of channel repetitions, the wider the range of $p$ for which the initial state of the probe that allows for the maximization of the QFI is a separable state. We also show that a single qubit probe undergoing a sequential encoding protocol outperforms the optimal two-qubit single-round QFI for $M>3$ in the weak-noise regime.
