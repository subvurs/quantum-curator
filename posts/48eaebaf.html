IBM, RIKEN, and NVIDIA Achieve 79x Accuracy Gain in Quantum-Classical Simulations of Iron-Sulfur Clusters

Source: Quantum Computing Report November 16, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article shows that careful preparation of reference states can dramatically boost the fidelity of hybrid quantum‑classical chemistry simulations, delivering a 79× reduction in energy error for a realistic iron‑sulfur cluster on a 72‑qubit device. By marrying Sample‑Based Quantum Diagonalization with broken‑symmetry unrestricted Hartree–Fock inputs, IBM, RIKEN and NVIDIA push the frontier of near‑term quantum chemistry toward chemically accurate predictions that were previously limited to classical supercomputers. The result underscores how algorithmic advances—rather than just more qubits—are essential for tackling complex catalytic and bioinorganic systems in the next generation of quantum processors.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

Researchers from IBM Quantum, RIKEN, and NVIDIA have achieved a 79-fold improvement in energy accuracy for quantum-classical simulations of iron-sulfur clusters. This was done by initializing Sample-Based Quantum Diagonalization (SQD) with broken-symmetry unrestricted Hartree-Fock (BS-UHF) reference states, bringing ground-state energy estimates for a 72-qubit [Fe4S4(SCH3)4]2− active space within 4.3 millihartree of high-precision classical benchmarks. The study highlights the importance of reference-state preparation in SQD accuracy.
