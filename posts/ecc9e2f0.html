Rethinking Entropic Uncertainty: How measuring without a trace leads to longer keys

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics November 16, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article shows that by treating Alice and Bob as a single system and adding a virtual measurement register, the entropic uncertainty relation can be extended to incorporate any number of mutually unbiased bases, delivering provably tighter QKD key rates against coherent attacks. The technique not only resolves a long‑standing gap in multi‑basis security analysis but also offers a versatile tool for strengthening proofs across many quantum cryptographic and sensing protocols. As a result, future implementations that already employ multiple measurement settings can now extract longer secret keys without hardware changes, though the approach still relies on idealized virtual measurements that must be carefully mapped onto real‑world devices.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

In recent years, the entropic uncertainty relation (EUR) has been one of the most prominent tools to obtain tight key rates secure against general coherent attacks for a broad class of quantum key distribution (QKD) protocols. Despite its success, the fundamental question of how to incorporate the statistics of more than two mutually unbiased bases (MUBs) directly into the EUR is yet to be answered. Here, we introduce a simple yet powerful shift of perspective: Instead of the usual tripartition into the systems of Alice, Bob and Eve, we treat Alice and Bob as one joint system and introduce an additional register as the third system. This register contains the results of appropriately chosen virtual measurements that leave no trace in the real branch of the protocol, but act as a conditioning on the counterfactual phase error measurements. We show that this allows us to obtain tight key rates for QKD protocols using an arbitrary number of MUBs directly and natively from the EUR. Moreover, we demonstrate that this approach has a much broader relevance beyond the inclusion of multiple MUBs, allowing for improvements in many existing security proofs and the treatment of previously unexplored aspects for a wide range of protocols.
