South Korea Launches 614 PFLOPS Hangang Supercomputer Ahead of May 2027 IonQ Quantum Link

Source: Quantum Computing Report November 07, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article marks a significant step toward large‑scale hybrid computing, as South Korea’s new Hangang supercomputer will provide the classical horsepower needed to feed IonQ’s trapped‑ion processor once the quantum link goes live in 2027. By pairing 614 PFLOPS of HPC with a cloud‑accessible quantum accelerator, the nation is positioning itself alongside other early adopters such as the U.S. and Europe that are building integrated quantum‑classical stacks for materials science, AI, and national R&D. The move also underlines the K‑Moonshot strategy’s ambition to translate quantum breakthroughs into commercial value for SMEs, though the true impact will depend on software ecosystems and latency between the two platforms.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

South Korea has launched its sixth national supercomputer, Hangang, with a peak performance of 614 petaflops. It is designed to be the high-performance computing backbone for the country's first hybrid quantum-classical computing platform, integrating with IonQ's Tempo quantum processor by May 2027. Hangang will support general research, national strategic R&D, and small to medium enterprises, playing a key role in South Korea's K-Moonshot initiative.
