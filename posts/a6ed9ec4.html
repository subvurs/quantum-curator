Chalmers Next Labs, ConScience, and NQFF Validate Superconducting Qubit Chip Stability Across International Testbeds

Source: Quantum Computing Report October 17, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article shows that superconducting qubit chips can retain their performance even after being shipped across continents, a milestone for scaling quantum hardware collaborations and supply chains. By proving that the QiB1 processor survives the environmental shifts between Gothenburg and Singapore, the study validates the emerging “cross‑facility measurement service” model that could become a de‑facto standard for benchmarking devices worldwide. It dovetails with recent efforts to create interoperable testbeds such as the WACQT–NQFF Twin Testbed, helping industry move from isolated labs toward a more distributed, commercial quantum ecosystem. The result also reminds readers that while transport stability is now demonstrated, long‑term reliability under repeated handling and varied cryogenic setups will still need systematic monitoring.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

A joint study by Chalmers Next Labs AB, ConScience AB, and Singapore’s National Quantum Federated Foundry (NQFF) validated the stability of a superconducting quantum processor (QiB1) when transported between international research facilities. The study, conducted under the WACQT–NQFF Twin Testbed initiative, confirmed that environmental changes between Gothenburg and Singapore did not degrade the processor’s performance. This collaboration is now being commercialized as a standardized cross-facility measurement service.
