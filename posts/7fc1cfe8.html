Digital Coherent Twin-Field Quantum Key Distribution with Gaussian-Modulated Coherent States

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics November 13, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article shows that twin‑field ideas can be transplanted into continuous‑variable QKD without the costly active stabilization normally required for TF‑QKD, by leveraging Gaussian‑modulated coherent states and digital signal processing already familiar from modern coherent optical links. By introducing a new set of numerical constraints, the authors demonstrate key rates that surpass direct‑transmission CV‑QKD and even break the repeaterless bound at metropolitan‑scale distances, marking a practical step toward long‑range, hardware‑efficient quantum‑secure communications. The result bridges the gap between high‑performance TF‑QKD concepts and deployable CV platforms, though its security proof still relies on a photon‑number cutoff approximation that will need further validation for full composable security.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

Quantum key distribution (QKD) enables information-theoretically secure key sharing between two distant parties. Recently, one promising QKD protocol which can distribute a secret key over long distances called twin-field QKD (TF-QKD) has been developed. However, it requires active compensation for experimental imperfections such as phase, polarization, and frequency fluctuations with expensive devices and this makes its experimental implementation challenging. Here, we propose a long-distance continuous-variable QKD (CV-QKD) protocol which is inspired by TF-QKD. Our protocol can remove these requirements by using Gaussian-modulated coherent states and digital signal processing which is widely used in recent digital coherent optical communication and experimental implementations of CV-QKD. For security analysis, we use a numerical optimization approach with a photon-number cutoff assumption. We propose a new set of constraints for numerical optimization which can efficiently constrain a highly non-Gaussian quantum state shared by two legitimate parties in our protocol. We show that the key rate of our protocol exceeds the key rate of a direct-transmission CV-QKD protocol at long distances and it can overcome the repeaterless bound.
