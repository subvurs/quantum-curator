Fujitsu Open-Sources OpenQARP Framework to Streamline Quantum Application Development

Source: Quantum Computing Report September 16, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article is noteworthy because Fujitsu’s decision to open‑source OpenQARP under a permissive Apache license gives developers immediate access to a sizable library of more than 100 reusable components that can cut quantum‑software codebases by up to 70 %. By targeting both noisy intermediate‑scale quantum (NISQ) devices and future fault‑tolerant architectures, the framework bridges today’s experimental hardware with the longer‑term roadmap, complementing other open ecosystems such as Qiskit and Cirq. Its multi‑language support—including Python, CUDA‑Q, and a 40‑qubit simulator—means teams can prototype on classical GPUs before moving to real qubits, accelerating algorithm prototyping and cross‑platform benchmarking. The main caveat is that the true impact will depend on how quickly the community adopts the library and integrates it with existing toolchains, but the move signals growing momentum toward standardized, reusable quantum software stacks.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

Fujitsu has open-sourced OpenQARP, a quantum application development framework, under the Apache License 2.0. Available on GitHub, it offers over 100 modular software components to simplify NISQ and FTQC application workflows, potentially reducing code volume by up to 70%. The library includes Fujitsu-developed algorithms for various quantum computing tasks and supports execution across Python, NVIDIA CUDA-Q, and Fujitsu's 40-qubit simulator.
