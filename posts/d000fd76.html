High-fidelity control of long-lived data qubits via a noisy mediator

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics November 19, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article shows that high‑fidelity gates on long‑lived storage qubits no longer demand an equally coherent mediator, because a rapid reset of the noisy auxiliary qubit can actively suppress its relaxation‑induced errors. By framing the dynamics in quantum‑trajectory language, the authors derive compact fidelity formulas that can be plugged into superconducting, trapped‑ion or spin‑based platforms, echoing recent pushes toward modular architectures where “fast” ancillae mediate interactions. The result widens the hardware design space and could accelerate scaling by allowing engineers to prioritize qubit lifetime over mediator coherence without sacrificing gate performance.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

Long-lived qubits are attractive for storing quantum information, but they are often difficult to control or couple directly. A common strategy is to use mediator qubits to generate effective interactions for gate implementation. However, the mediator typically has a shorter coherence time than the data qubits, so mediator relaxation and dephasing are expected to limit the achievable gate fidelity. Here, we show that this limitation can be overcome. We present a reset-assisted control method that suppresses mediator-induced errors and enables high-fidelity single- and two-qubit gates on a quantum register of long-lived data qubits. Although mediator quantum jumps render the data-qubit evolution stochastic, repeated mediator reinitialization drives the resulting evolution paths toward the desired target operation. An analytical quantum-trajectory analysis reveals the physical mechanism of error suppression and provides compact fidelity formulas for adapting the method to different platforms. Our results relax the requirement for long mediator coherence, broadening the range of mediators that can support high-fidelity control.
