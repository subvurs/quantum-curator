SCOPE: A Physical Execution Latency-Guided Quantum Circuit Compilation Framework

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics November 16, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article introduces SCOPE, a compiler that uses an operator‑Schmidt time surrogate to steer pulse‑level optimization toward the shortest possible execution latency, directly addressing the coherence‑time bottleneck that limits near‑term quantum processors. By integrating a physics‑based lower bound on nonlocal evolution into a genetic search, SCOPE achieves up to a two‑fold reduction in critical‑path duration compared with the previous EPOC framework across a range of benchmark circuits, including a six‑qubit QFT. The results demonstrate that latency‑aware compilation can translate into tangible speedups without increasing gate count, highlighting a practical pathway for squeezing more algorithmic depth out of noisy hardware. However, the approach depends on accurate coupling models and incurs extra classical optimization overhead, so its benefits will be most pronounced when pulse‑level control is already available on the target device.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

Quantum computing requires compilers to translate algorithms represented in the quantum circuit model into unitary operations supported by physical devices. Limited coherence time makes physical execution latency an important compilation objective. Gate fusion can shorten pulses, but gate count and depth do not capture fused targets' control demands. Evaluating every candidate with quantum optimal control (QOC) would embed pulse optimization in structural search. We present Schmidt-guided Compilation for Optimized Pulse Execution (SCOPE), driven by an operator Schmidt time surrogate. The target's angular distance from product operators measures the required nonlocal change; dividing it by a coupling-limited evolution-rate bound gives a lower bound on exact implementation time. Trace-angle and waveform constraints complete block scores for critical-path evaluation during genetic search. Local QOC determines final pulse durations. Under a numerical nearest-neighbor control model, all 104 results across 13 instances from four application families have shorter QOC critical-path durations than an adapted efficient pulse generation framework with advanced synthesis for quantum circuits (EPOC). Using eight-run instance means, the equally weighted reduction is 42.50\%, with a geometric mean speedup of 2.00. In a shared six-qubit quantum Fourier transform (QFT) ablation, time-surrogate guidance reduces mean QOC duration by 22.21\% relative to gate-count guidance and 21.01\% relative to depth guidance, validating the surrogate's joint use in fitness evaluation and mutation-region selection.
