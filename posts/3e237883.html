Symmetries and model reduction in a $\cos 2\varphi$ transmon qubit

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics October 30, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article delivers the first systematic low‑energy model for the newly demonstrated cos 2φ transmon, showing how engineered symmetries can be captured in a compact Born–Oppenheimer description that makes selection rules and noise protection transparent. By isolating a heavy transmon mode from two lighter interferometer modes, the authors identify a sweet‑spot where flux‑noise dephasing is dramatically reduced while preserving the intrinsic charge‑noise immunity—a combination that has been elusive for most protected superconducting qubits. The work not only clarifies the underlying physics of multi‑mode circuits but also provides a practical design recipe for scaling symmetry‑protected hardware toward more reliable quantum processors.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

Engineered symmetries in superconducting circuits can protect quantum information; much like selection rules in atomic physics, they forbid transitions between the qubit states. Protected circuits, however, combine several loops and islands and host multiple nonlinearly coupled modes, calling for reduced low-energy models that inherit the circuit symmetries and make the protection manifest. We develop such a reduction for the $\cos 2\varphi$ transmon qubit, recently realized experimentally with a two-Cooper-pair interferometer, whose circuit hosts three coupled modes. Exploiting the separation of energy scales between the heavy transmon mode and the light internal modes of the interferometer, we derive an effective single-band Born-Oppenheimer model, including its geometric correction, and extend it to several bands. We identify the symmetries of the full and reduced Hamiltonians and derive exact selection rules. Finally, we identify a parameter regime beyond the single-band description that suppresses flux-noise dephasing without degrading charge-noise protection.
