University of Padova and ThinkQuantum Demonstrate 18-km Intermodal Free-Space QKD with Room-Temperature Detectors

Source: Quantum Computing Report August 27, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article matters because it demonstrates a fully operational 18‑km intermodal free‑space QKD link that uses inexpensive room‑temperature single‑photon detectors, removing the bulky cryogenic hardware that has long hampered practical deployments. By successfully coupling a remote transmitter on Monte Grande with an urban optical ground station, the work shows how fiber‑based and free‑space quantum channels can be integrated into hybrid networks—a capability that complements recent satellite QKD milestones such as China’s Micius program and Europe’s upcoming space‑based projects. The result points toward lower‑cost, scalable secure communication infrastructure for cities, though performance will still depend on atmospheric conditions and detector efficiency improvements.

— Mark Eatherly

Researchers from the University of Padova, Italian photonics firm ThinkQuantum s.r.l., and the National Research Council of Italy (CNR-IFN) have published a real-time field trial of an intermodal quantum key distribution (QKD) system in npj Quantum Information.
