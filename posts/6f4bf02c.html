A Koashi--Imoto Criterion for Holevo-Rate Visible Compression

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics November 24, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article settles a long‑standing question in quantum source coding by giving an exact, operational condition—via the Koashi–Imoto decomposition—for when the optimal Holevo rate can be reached without entanglement assistance, simply using shared randomness. The result clarifies that for qubit ensembles the only way to hit the Holevo limit is if the signals are all pure or mutually commuting, a restriction that directly informs the design of hardware‑efficient compression protocols and error‑tolerant quantum networking stacks. By linking visible compression to recent recovery and decoupling techniques, it also bridges foundational information theory with practical coding strategies, though the bound shows that any mixed component forces a non‑negligible rate penalty regardless of block length.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

For a finite memoryless quantum source whose encoder knows the sequence of classical labels, we determine when the Holevo rate is achievable with shared classical randomness and no shared entanglement. In the maximal Koashi--Imoto decomposition of the signal ensemble, this occurs exactly when each signal is pure on the quantum part of every sector it occupies. For qubits, this means that the signals are all pure or commute pairwise. If any occupied quantum part is mixed, every code with sufficiently small mean local error has rate bounded away from the Holevo information, uniformly in blocklength. To prove the converse, we apply Fawzi--Renner recovery to a hypothetical sequence of visible codes approaching the Holevo rate at vanishing local error, then invoke a decoupling bound of Baghali Khanian and Winter for blind compression.
