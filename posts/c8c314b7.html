ZuriQ and Rhonexum Form Multi-Year Partnership to Integrate Cryogenic Control ICs into 2D Trapped-Ion Processors

Source: Quantum Computing Report October 23, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article marks a concrete step toward solving the long‑standing I/O bottleneck that has limited trapped‑ion systems from scaling beyond a few hundred qubits. By embedding Rhonexum’s cryogenic control ICs directly on ZuriQ’s 2D ion chips, the partnership promises to shrink wiring complexity and reduce latency, echoing similar integration moves seen in superconducting platforms such as IBM’s Cryo‑CMOS effort. If the first shipment arrives on schedule in late 2026, it could enable trapped‑ion processors with thousands of qubits, opening a realistic path toward fault‑tolerant architectures; however, the true impact will depend on how well the cryogenic electronics perform under the stringent noise and thermal budgets required for high‑fidelity gates.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

ZuriQ and Rhonexum have partnered to integrate low-temperature control electronics into ZuriQ's 2D trapped-ion processors. This collaboration aims to overcome the I/O wiring bottleneck in quantum computing by using Rhonexum's cryogenic integrated circuits directly within the quantum hardware, with the first hardware shipment expected by late 2026. This will allow for the scaling of trapped-ion architectures to thousands of qubits.
