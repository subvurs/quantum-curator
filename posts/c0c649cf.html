TOYO Corporation Acquires Second On-Premises IQM System to Open Dual-QPU Testbed for Japan’s Ecosystem

Source: Quantum Computing Report September 16, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article marks a notable step for Japan’s quantum ecosystem, as TOYO Corporation will host two complementary IQM processors—a 5‑qubit Spark and a 20‑qubit Radiance—creating the country’s first dual‑QPU testbed with open access for academia and industry. By providing both a small, low‑latency device for algorithm prototyping and a larger, more powerful processor for scaling studies, the platform mirrors the multi‑node strategies emerging in Europe and the U.S., accelerating collaborative research and workforce development ahead of the 2027 operational target. The initiative also aligns with Japan’s national quantum roadmap, offering a practical venue to benchmark error mitigation techniques and software stacks before commercial deployment. However, the modest qubit counts mean that breakthroughs will likely be incremental rather than game‑changing until larger hardware arrives.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

TOYO Corporation has expanded its quantum hardware by acquiring an IQM Spark (5-qubit) in addition to its previous order of a 20-qubit IQM Radiance processor from IQM Quantum Computers. These systems, expected to be operational by early 2027, will establish a dual-QPU testbed in Japan, offering open access to universities, startups, and industrial researchers to advance quantum technology and achieve national strategic targets.
