Iceberg Quantum Validates Pinnacle 1.2 Architecture with Crevasse QLDPC Decoder to Reduce RSA-2048 Factoring Resources

Source: Quantum Computing Report October 23, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article demonstrates that Iceberg Quantum’s new Pinnacle 1.2 architecture, paired with the Crevasse QLDPC decoder, can push logical error rates below 10⁻¹² per qubit‑cycle—precisely the regime needed for a realistic RSA‑2048 factoring attack. By validating these numbers through full circuit‑level simulations, the team shows that a modest 48 k physical‑qubit device could theoretically break RSA‑2048 in under a day, a dramatic improvement over earlier estimates that required millions of qubits and months of runtime. While still a simulation‑based claim, the result highlights how advances in low‑overhead quantum error correction are rapidly narrowing the gap between theoretical cryptanalytic threats and near‑term hardware capabilities.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

Iceberg Quantum's Pinnacle 1.2 architecture, featuring the Crevasse QLDPC decoder, significantly reduces the resources needed to factor RSA-2048. They achieved logical error rates below 10^-12 per logical qubit-cycle, a critical threshold for this task, through direct circuit-level simulations. This breakthrough could factor RSA-2048 in as little as 0.98 days with 48,000 physical qubits.
