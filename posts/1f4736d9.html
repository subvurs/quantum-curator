IonQ Secures $7.5 Million Phase 2 AFRL Allocation to Expand Rome, NY Quantum Network with Free-Space Optics and Frequency Conversion

Source: Quantum Computing Report October 27, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article highlights a rare and sizable defense‑funded push to turn quantum networking from a laboratory curiosity into an operational capability, with IonQ’s $7.5 million Phase 2 grant earmarked for free‑space optics and wavelength conversion in its Rome, NY testbed. By tackling the twin challenges of fiber loss and mismatched photon frequencies, the project moves closer to interoperable quantum links that could support distributed computing or secure communications across heterogeneous hardware—a key step beyond today’s single‑node demonstrations. The effort dovetails with recent advances from government and industry on satellite‑based QKD and photonic interconnects, suggesting a near‑term pathway for scalable quantum networks while still leaving open questions about long‑term reliability in harsh environments.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

IonQ has received a $7.5 million Phase 2 allocation from the Air Force Research Laboratory (AFRL) to expand its quantum networking in Rome, New York. This phase will focus on connecting different quantum computers using both fiber optics and open-air links. Key upgrades include free-space optics and frequency conversion to overcome signal loss and wavelength incompatibilities, enabling quantum communication in challenging environments.
