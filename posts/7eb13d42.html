Time-invariant negativity and persistent bound entanglement in a cyclic two-qutrit family

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics November 03, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article reports the first observation of time‑invariant entanglement in a qutrit‑qutrit pair subjected to collective dephasing, showing that a suitably weighted component in a decoherence‑free subspace can lock the negativity at a constant non‑zero value despite ongoing noise. By delivering exact PPT/ separability boundaries, explicit product decompositions and a closed‑form realignment test, the authors give the community a rare “complete” characterization of bound‑entangled trajectories that complements recent studies on decoherence‑free subspaces and entanglement sudden death. The results suggest new routes for designing noise‑resilient high‑dimensional quantum channels, although the effect hinges on precise state preparation and may be sensitive to deviations from the ideal collective dephasing model.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

We study the entanglement dynamics of two qutrits under collective dephasing for a family of states obtained by mixing a maximally entangled state that lives in the decoherence-free subspace with three nonstationary two-term states sharing a common amplitude ratio $r$. We obtain exact PPT and separability regions along the whole trajectory, a specialization of known results on cyclic sign invariant states, and we supply explicit certificates: a finite product decomposition, three fixed positive-map witnesses, and a closed realignment formula that detects every PPT-entangled state, so that no state is left undecided. The negativity is computed in closed form. It shows that the entanglement is time-invariant, positive and constant from the initial time although the state changes, exactly when the weight of the maximally entangled state exceeds the threshold $U_r=r/(1+r+r^2)$. To our knowledge, this is the first demonstration of time-invariant entanglement for qutrit--qutrit systems under collective dephasing. For smaller weights the entanglement decays and vanishes at a finite time: the state either becomes separable (entanglement sudden death) or, for $r\neq1$ in an intermediate range, loses distillability suddenly and remains bound entangled at all later times. Every state with negative partial transpose is one-copy distillable by a fixed local projection. The same trajectories arise from the global dephasing map and from a correlated local random-unitary semigroup, although the two channels differ on product inputs. Exact rational benchmarks and embeddings of the Horodecki and Ha--Kye--Park families are provided.
