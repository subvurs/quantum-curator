Quantum Algorithm for Direct Estimation of Energy-Level Differences via Quantum Dynamics Simulation

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics November 25, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article introduces a fault‑tolerant‑ready algorithm that extracts tiny energy gaps—such as molecular force constants—directly from a pair of time‑evolution circuits, cutting the sampling cost from the full Hamiltonian norm to the much smaller norm of its derivative. By sidestepping the heavy overhead of statistical phase estimation for differences, it promises faster, lower‑qubit calculations of gradients that are essential for geometry optimisation and materials design, complementing recent advances in quantum gradient‐estimation and variational chemistry methods. The numerical results on the Fermi‑Hubbard model suggest a practical pathway to obtain accurate forces on early quantum hardware, though scaling to larger, chemically realistic systems will still depend on improvements in error rates and Hamiltonian simulation techniques.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

We present a quantum algorithm for the early fault-tolerant quantum computing (early-FTQC) era that estimates minute energy-level differences, such as energy gradients and molecular forces, at a lower sampling cost than existing phase-estimation methods. Such energy derivatives, accessed as the small energy difference induced by an infinitesimal parameter shift, are central to quantum simulation in chemistry and materials science. Although Statistical Phase Estimation (SPE) is well-suited to energy estimation in this era, applying it to an energy difference demands a sampling complexity that scales with the operator norm of the full Hamiltonian, which grows with system size. Our algorithm overcomes this by incorporating two time-evolution operators, corresponding to the Hamiltonians before and after the parameter shift, into a single quantum circuit, which is combined with a non-adaptive, statistically sampled reconstruction in the energy-difference domain. This reduces the relevant scale from the operator norm of the full Hamiltonian to that of the operator norm of the Hamiltonian derivative and the parameter shift, thereby lowering the sampling complexity. We further propose the joint estimation of energy eigenvalues and their differences, giving a holistic view of the energy landscape. Numerical simulations and resource estimations on prototypical systems, such as the Fermi-Hubbard model, demonstrate the resulting reductions in required resources without compromising precision. This positions energy-difference estimation as a practical task for early-FTQC devices, opening a route to molecular forces, response properties, and excited-state energy differences.
