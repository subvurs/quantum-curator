Podcast with Johannes Pollanen, Co-founder of EeroQ and a Professor of Physics at Michigan State University

Source: Quantum Computing Report October 17, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article spotlights EeroQ’s unconventional qubit design that traps single electrons on a superfluid‑helium film, offering potentially record‑long coherence while still being fabricated on standard semiconductor wafers. By arguing that the proven silicon chip manufacturing ecosystem is essential for scaling, Pollanen links an exotic physics platform to the practical realities of mass production—a key hurdle for any quantum hardware roadmap. The discussion places EeroQ’s approach alongside recent advances in silicon spin qubits and trapped‑ion systems, highlighting a possible hybrid path that could combine ultra‑low decoherence with industrial‑scale integration. Readers should watch how this marriage of novel qubit physics and conventional chip fabrication might reshape the race toward fault‑tolerant quantum computers.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

Johannes Pollanen, co-founder of EeroQ and a professor of physics at Michigan State University, explains how EeroQ builds qubits from single electrons floating on a film of superfluid helium, and why he thinks the semiconductor chip is the only proven way to scale a quantum computer.
