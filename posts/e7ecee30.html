Floquet spectra and parameter sensitivity of entanglement in the quantum kicked top

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics November 23, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article shows for the first time that peaks in entanglement sensitivity of a quantum‑kicked‑top processor line up with Floquet eigenphase degeneracies and short‑period revivals, providing a concrete spectral fingerprint of where small changes in control parameters produce large entanglement swings. By demonstrating that the resultant of the characteristic polynomial can map these “hot spots” even for just four qubits—and arguing that the pattern extends to higher spin values—the work offers a practical diagnostic tool for calibrating chaotic‑type quantum simulators and for tailoring gate sequences that deliberately harness or avoid such sensitivity. In the broader landscape, it bridges the long‑standing gap between quantum chaos theory and near‑term algorithmic design, suggesting new ways to predict error amplification in hardware that exploits periodically driven dynamics.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

We investigate the relationship between the Floquet spectrum and the sensitivity of the entanglement landscape to parameter and initial condition change in the quantum kicked top. We focus on the case of four qubits and on locating degeneracies in its Floquet eigenphases using the resultant of its characteristic polynomial $p$ and its derivative $\partial_λp$ and quantitatively map the sensitivity of the entanglement landscape to changes in kicking strength over the sphere of spin coherent initial states. We find that prominent sensitivity maxima coincide with either Floquet eigenphase degeneracies or kick strengths exhibiting short-period quantum revivals, with some points satisfying both conditions. These results establish a connection between the spectral structure of the Floquet operator and entanglement landscape sensitivity, demonstrating the utility of the resultant in studying the dynamics of small-$j$ quantum kicked tops. Since this structure extends to all values of $j$, the central lessons apply completely generally.
