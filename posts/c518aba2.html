Emergence of Strategic Equilibria from Transverse Field Ising Hamiltonian Dynamics

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics August 30, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article shows that the dynamics of a transverse‑field Ising Hamiltonian can itself generate the entangling operations needed for quantum games, turning a physical interaction into a tunable “quantum referee” rather than relying on ad‑hoc gate constructions. By linking game‑theoretic equilibria to experimentally controllable Hamiltonian parameters, it bridges abstract quantum‑game theory with near‑term quantum hardware such as superconducting qubits and trapped ions, where transverse fields are already used for annealing and simulation. The result is a concrete pathway to demonstrate genuine quantum advantage in strategic settings, though the practical impact will depend on how robustly the engineered entanglement can be maintained amid decoherence in real devices.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

Game theory studies strategic decision-making among rational agents, and many classical games can be mapped onto interaction models such as the Ising model. Quantum game theory extends this framework by allowing players to exploit quantum superposition and entanglement. In this work, we study quantum games using an operator-based formulation derived from the transverse-field quantum Ising model. We show that the Hamiltonian-driven dynamics naturally generate entangling operator which resolve the dilemma in the game settings. This leads to a clear quantum advantage over classical outcomes. Unlike standard quantization schemes based on fixed entangling gates, the present approach enables tunable entanglement, controlled directly by physical Hamiltonian parameters, providing a hardware-relevant perspective on quantum game design.
