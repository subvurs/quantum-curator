Alice & Bob Details QRMI and Slurm 26.11 Hierarchical Resource Integration for Cat-Qubit Systems at SC26

Source: Quantum Computing Report November 17, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article matters because it demonstrates the first seamless coupling of cat‑qubit processors to a mainstream HPC scheduler, letting quantum jobs be submitted through NVIDIA’s Slurm 26.11 just like any CPU or GPU workload via the new QRMI interface. By leveraging Slurm’s hierarchical resource model, Alice & Bob avoid bespoke middleware and make their Felis cloud API compatible with existing supercomputing ecosystems—a step that echoes recent efforts to treat quantum hardware as a first‑class resource in HPC environments. The result could dramatically simplify hybrid quantum‑classical workflows for researchers, though real‑world gains will still hinge on latency, scheduler adoption across sites, and the maturity of cat‑qubit error mitigation techniques.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

At SC26, Alice & Bob detailed how their cat-qubit quantum processors integrate with NVIDIA’s Slurm 26.11 workload manager via the Quantum Resource Management Interface (QRMI). This collaboration with STFC Hartree Centre, IBM, and Pasqal allows Alice & Bob’s Felis cloud API to connect directly to standard supercomputing schedulers. The new architecture leverages Slurm 26.11's native Hierarchical Resources (HRES) support, enabling seamless quantum hardware integration without custom software.
