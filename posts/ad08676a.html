Autonomous cooling of Kerr-cat qubits to minutes-long bit-flip times

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics November 03, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article demonstrates that Kerr‑cat qubits can be autonomously cooled via a resonant lossy mode, extending their intrinsic bit‑flip lifetime from milliseconds to minutes (up to 450 s) while preserving fast Z gates and high‑fidelity single‑shot readout. By confirming the predicted exponential scaling of flip times with photon number, the work pushes biased‑noise hardware‑efficient error correction into a regime where logical qubits could be built with dramatically lower overhead than conventional superconducting transmons. The result shows that the remaining bottleneck is now phase‑flip loss rather than bit‑flips, highlighting a clear path toward more practical fault‑tolerant quantum processors.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

Quantum error correction becomes more hardware efficient when physical noise is strongly biased towards one error channel. Cat qubits realize such a bias by encoding information in two opposite coherent states of a superconducting oscillator, so that bit flips are exponentially suppressed with photon number while phase-flip rates increase only linearly. In Kerr-cat qubits, a driven double-well potential confines these states and enables fast coherent control, but excitations can induce interwell switching, limiting bit-flip times to milliseconds. Here we show that the nonlinear interaction generating the confinement can also cool these excitations. When an intra-well transition becomes resonant with a lossy readout mode, excitations are transferred out of the qubit and dissipated, as established by spectroscopy and relaxation measurements. We observe the predicted exponential scaling of Kerr-cat bit-flip times with photon number, reaching 450 s, over five orders of magnitude beyond previous demonstrations. Phase-flip rates remain consistent with intrinsic photon loss at operating points with seconds-long bit-flip times. We further demonstrate fast Z gates without measurable degradation of bit-flip protection and single-shot readout with a bit-flip probability below $10^{-6}$ per measurement. Autonomous cooling thus brings minutes-long bit-flip protection to Kerr-cat qubits while retaining fast coherent control, without additional cooling drives or circuit elements.
