Bound Entanglement with Negative Partial Transpose Exists

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics November 25, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article settles a decades‑old question by demonstrating the first explicit example of a negative‑partial‑transpose (NPT) state that is nevertheless bound entangled, overturning the long‑standing belief that NPT automatically implies distillability. By proving that the Werner state on an 8 × 8 system cannot be distilled even with unlimited copies or separable operations, it shows that distillable entanglement is neither additive nor convex—fundamental properties assumed in many theoretical frameworks. The result forces a re‑examination of entanglement‑based benchmarks for quantum hardware, reminding developers that simple PPT tests are insufficient to guarantee usable entanglement. It also opens new avenues for exploring exotic resource states that could be harnessed—or must be avoided—in future quantum protocols.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

We show that the Werner state $ρ= (23 I - 3\,\mathrm{SWAP})/1448$ on $\mathbb{C}^8 \otimes \mathbb{C}^8$ has a negative partial transpose and is bound entangled: no LOCC protocol can distill entanglement from any number of copies of $ρ$, and even with separable operations the fidelity of a two-qubit output with a Bell state never exceeds $(1+\sqrt{2})/4 \approx 0.604$. It follows that distillable entanglement is neither additive nor convex.
