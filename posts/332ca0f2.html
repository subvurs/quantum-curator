Breakdown of Linearized Dispersion in Waveguide QED

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics November 03, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article shows that the long‑standing linearized‑dispersion approximation, which has underpinned most waveguide QED models, can fail dramatically once emitters reach strong coupling or broader photonic bandwidths, because it wipes out bound‑outside‑continuum states that dominate decoherence dynamics. By providing a clear criterion based on the number of bound states and demonstrating how giant atoms experience qualitatively new decay patterns, the work gives theorists and experimentalists a practical tool for assessing when more exact dispersion treatments are required. The result sharpens design rules for quantum interconnects and simulators that rely on waveguide‑mediated interactions, warning that oversimplified models could miss crucial loss channels in next‑generation hardware.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

Exploring interactions between quantum emitters and guided photons, waveguide quantum electrodynamics (wQED) offers a versatile platform for quantum simulation and quantum interconnect. To simplify the quantized light-matter interactions, a linearized dispersion approximation (LDA) was widely used in wQED under the conditions of weak coupling and narrow photonic bandwidth. However, a growing number of strong-coupling wQED experiments have called the validity of LDA into question. Here, by investigating giant atoms coupled to a semi-infinite waveguide, we propose a principle to judge the validity of LDA. We find that the decoherence of the atoms exhibits rich dynamical behaviors determined by different numbers of bound states in and out of the continuum (BICs and BOCs) formed in the energy spectrum of the total atom-photon system. Based on this, we show that despite the LDA can partially preserve some of BICs, it completely erases BOCs. This causes the dominant role of LDA in the atomic dynamics to break down. Providing a physical insight into the validity of LDA, our results lay a firm foundation for exploring fundamental physics and its application frontier in strong-coupling wQED platforms.
