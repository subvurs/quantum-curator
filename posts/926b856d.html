Quantum Capacity with Incoherent Codes

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics November 24, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article shows that when quantum processors are limited to incoherent (basis‑state) encoding—a realistic constraint for many near‑term hardware platforms—even infinitesimal depolarizing noise can collapse the channel’s capacity to zero, meaning no qubit can be transmitted reliably. By deriving a single‑letter Holevo‑information formula and proving that incoherent decoding can at most halve the achievable rate (a bound that is tight), the work provides the first quantitative benchmark for how much performance is sacrificed under such restrictions. The results sharpen our understanding of practical capacity limits and will help guide error‑mitigation strategies as experimentalists design devices that must balance coherence control with hardware simplicity.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

Arbitrarily weak depolarizing noise drives the quantum capacity to zero in every input basis when the encoder is restricted to incoherent operations. Even one qubit cannot then be sent reliably: if the output is replaced by the maximally mixed state with probability $\varepsilon\le1/2$, the entanglement fidelity is at most $1-\varepsilon/2$ at every block length. With unrestricted decoding, a single-letter formula gives the capacity with incoherent encoding as the maximum Holevo information over ensembles of basis states with identical environment outputs. For either incoherent or unrestricted encoding, imposing incoherent decoding reduces the capacity by at most a factor of two; this factor is optimal. We bound the fidelity of incoherent decoders and evaluate the capacities with incoherent encoding for multiqubit Pauli channels.
