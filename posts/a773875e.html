Fer expansion converges almost twice as far as Magnus for qubits

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics November 13, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article shows that the Fer series—an alternative to the widely used Magnus expansion for simulating time‑dependent qubit dynamics—has a provably larger convergence radius, roughly twice as large as the universal Magnus bound of π. By extending guaranteed convergence out to about 6.21 (≈1.98 π), the result promises more accurate and efficient pulse‑level simulations for hardware‑focused applications such as optimal control and error‑suppressed gate design, especially when dealing with strong driving fields. The proof currently applies only to a single qubit, so further work will be needed to see how the advantage scales to multi‑qubit systems.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

The Fer expansion writes the propagator of a time-dependent Hamiltonian as a product of unitary exponentials whose error decreases doubly exponentially with the number of factors, while the Magnus expansion improves by one order at a time. Its convergence, however, was guaranteed only below a radius of about $2.6058$, smaller than the Magnus radius $π$. We establish a guaranteed convergence threshold of about $6.2106$, or $1.98\,π$, for a qubit. This is almost twice the optimal universal Magnus threshold $π$; the bound on a single Fer step is sharp, while the exact convergence radius remains open.
