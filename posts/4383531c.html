Bell-pair distillation under imperfect operations

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics October 26, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article shows that the Bell‑pair distillation protocol introduced last year can still boost entanglement fidelity even when the underlying gates and measurements are noisy, a crucial step toward deploying entanglement purification on near‑term hardware. By mapping out which incoherent versus coherent error regimes preserve the advantage—and revealing how asymmetric errors can be balanced through complementary postselection—the work links theoretical guarantees to realistic quantum‑network nodes and sensing platforms. The findings suggest that modest‑quality devices could already benefit from built‑in entanglement cleaning, though the protocol’s tolerance varies sharply with error type, underscoring the need for tailored calibration in practical deployments.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

We recently proposed an entanglement distillation protocol based on local operations and classical communication that probabilistically and universally distills a maximally entangled Bell pair from multiple copies of an unknown two-qubit state [Phys. Rev. Lett. 135, 260202 (2025)]. Here, we investigate the robustness of this protocol against imperfections in its implementation. We consider several incoherent and coherent gate-error models, as well as measurement readout errors, and determine the regimes in which distillation enhances the fidelity with the target Bell state. We find that the protocol remains effective over substantial error ranges, although its robustness depends strongly on the type of error. We further analyze asymmetric errors between the two parties and identify conditions under which complementary postselection outcomes yield identical distillation dynamics, allowing the success probability to be enhanced.
