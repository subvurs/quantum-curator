BlueQubit Benchmarks Quantum Computational Advantage in Random-Circuit Sampling on IBM Nighthawk r2 Hardware

Source: Quantum Computing Report October 29, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article marks the first demonstration of quantum computational advantage on IBM’s latest Nighthawk r2 (ibm_phoenix) processor, showing that a 61‑qubit random‑circuit sampling run can achieve a fidelity that would require more than a century for today’s classical supercomputers to match. By confirming the classical hardness of the benchmark on a hardware platform distinct from Google’s Sycamore, it validates IBM’s recent architectural upgrades—lower error rates and improved connectivity—that are crucial for scaling toward practical quantum workloads. While the result is limited to a specialized sampling task, it signals that near‑term devices are reaching the performance envelope needed for more useful algorithms in the coming years.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

BlueQubit, in collaboration with EPFL and the XPRIZE Foundation, has demonstrated quantum computational advantage using IBM Quantum's 120-qubit ibm_phoenix processor. Their random-circuit sampling experiment, involving 61 active qubits, achieved a fidelity that would take over a century for classical supercomputers to replicate. This study highlights significant architectural improvements in IBM processors and validates the classical hardness of their quantum benchmarks.
