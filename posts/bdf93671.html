Detection of genuine multipartite entanglement by means of entanglement monotones

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics November 20, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article introduces a systematic way to construct genuine multipartite‑entanglement monotones that can certify “irreducible” D‑partite entanglement—states that cannot be broken down into lower‑order pieces—a capability that has been missing from most existing measures. By showing that no entropy‑based witness can satisfy both LOCC‑monotonicity and subadditivity, the authors motivate a relaxed LO‑monotone approach that nonetheless succeeds in detecting any form of genuine tripartite entanglement in three‑qubit systems, offering a powerful new benchmark for emerging quantum processors. The result dovetails with recent efforts to develop hardware‑friendly entanglement diagnostics and could help validate multi‑qubit gates and error‑correction codes once experimental protocols are built around these monotones.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

We look for pure state entanglement monotones that may serve as certificates of a strong form of genuine multipartite entanglement, that we refer to as irreducible entanglement: a $D$-partite state (with $D\geq 3$) is irreducibly $D$-partite entangled if it cannot be decomposed as a product of states that are at most $(D-1)$-partite entangled. We start by establishing a no-go result proving that no reasonable genuine multipartite entanglement entropy can simultaneously obey LOCC-monotonicity and subadditivity. After relaxing the first condition to LO-monotonicity, we provide a simple method generating genuine multipartite entanglement monotones with the desired properties. Focusing on a subfamily of these monotones, we show that they are able to detect any form of genuine tripartite entanglement in three-qubit systems.
