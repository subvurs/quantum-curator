Strong Converse Exponent of Quantum State Merging

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics August 27, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article settles a long‑standing gap by identifying the exact strong converse exponent for quantum state merging, showing that it is governed by optimized α‑z conditional Rényi entropies rather than the more familiar sandwiched versions. The result sharpens our understanding of one‑shot entanglement cost and links directly to recent advances in smoothed entropy techniques, clarifying when club‑sandwiched versus global smoothing yields different operational rates. By pinning down these exponents, the work provides a tighter benchmark for any protocol that seeks to trade entanglement and classical communication in near‑term quantum networks.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

We determine the strong converse exponent for the entanglement cost of quantum state merging, showing that it is characterized by the optimized $α$-$z$ conditional Rényi entropies with $z=α/2\in[1/2,1]$. This contrasts with the sandwiched conditional Rényi entropies that typically govern strong converse exponents in quantum information theory. As a consequence, we derive the strong converse exponent of the partially smoothed conditional min-entropy in purified distance. This exponent is governed by club-sandwiched conditional entropies, whereas global smoothing leads to a sandwiched expression.
