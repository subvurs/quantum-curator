Closing the loop on memory: accurate, shallow and transferable decoding of quantum LDPC codes

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics November 16, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article shows that the TEMPO‑BP decoder can automatically tune each variable’s memory strength during belief‑propagation, delivering logical failure rates far below those of conventional BP + OSD and Relay‑BP decoders on realistic circuit‑level noise. By achieving comparable or better performance with dramatically fewer iterations and without any code‑specific retuning, the method brings high‑rate qLDPC codes closer to practical fault‑tolerant implementations that promise lower qubit overhead. The results suggest a scalable path for hardware‑friendly decoding, though further testing on larger code families and under varied noise models will be needed before deployment in full quantum processors.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

Quantum low-density parity-check (qLDPC) codes have attracted interest for fault-tolerant quantum computing because their high-rate constructions offer a route to reduced qubit overhead. Realizing this potential requires reliable decoding. Belief propagation (BP) forms the basis of many qLDPC decoders, but can fail to converge under circuit-level noise. Memory-based variants can improve convergence, yet selecting memory strengths that work reliably across different syndromes remains challenging. Here we introduce TEMPO-BP, which adapts each variable's memory strength to the evolving decoding state through local feedback. On the gross code at a physical error rate of 0.4%, single-run TEMPO-BP achieves a per-shot logical failure rate of 0.73%, compared with 2.51% for BP+OSD+CS-10. Ensembles of 10-15 concurrent instances match or lower failure counts relative to Relay-BP-5 at all ten tested operating points, with per-instance iteration caps at least tenfold lower than its serial limit. Without retuning, the gross-code parameters yield fewer observed failures than Relay-BP-5 in its published gross-code configuration across four additional code families. Ablations support state-dependent memory control, while a loop-gain analysis characterizes specific amplification modes in the decoding dynamics.
