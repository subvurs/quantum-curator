Magic Can Superactivate Quantum Capacity

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics November 06, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article demonstrates that “magic”—the non‑stabilizer resource essential for universal quantum computation—can act as a catalyst that turns two otherwise useless channels into a conduit for genuine quantum information, extending the classic superactivation phenomenon to a new class of Clifford‑based convolutional channels. By linking magic to channel capacity through a concrete construction with mixed‑magic environments and half‑erasure auxiliaries, the work bridges abstract resource theories and practical communication models, suggesting that engineered noisy environments could be harnessed rather than merely mitigated. The result sharpens our understanding of what physical resources are truly required for quantum transmission and sets a benchmark for future protocols that aim to exploit non‑stabilizer noise as an asset rather than a liability.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

Environmental magic can enable two channels with zero quantum capacity to transmit quantum information jointly. We establish this superactivation within a family of discrete quantum convolution channels with fixed Clifford interactions. For the couplings considered, stabilizer-mixture environments induce entanglement-breaking channels. We construct a mixed magic environment whose induced channel has positive private capacity and a Choi state with positive partial transpose, hence zero quantum capacity. A half-erasure auxiliary activates this channel through the Smith--Yard mechanism. Finite-block encoding and decoding over a self-complementary convolution auxiliary then realize a higher-dimensional erasure channel that still permits activation. We also bound the activated capacity by the modified relative entropy of magic of the main environment. Replacing either environment of the convolution pair by any stabilizer mixture makes the joint quantum capacity vanish, identifying environmental magic as a necessary resource on both sides for this realization.
