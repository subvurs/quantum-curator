Quantinuum Chief Scientist Argues Physical Fidelity Trumps Gate Speed in Fault-Tolerant Scaling Race

Source: Quantum Computing Report October 31, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article spotlights a pivotal debate in the race toward fault‑tolerant quantum computers: whether ultra‑low error rates or sheer gate speed will drive scalability. By emphasizing Quantinuum’s trapped‑ion platform—whose physical fidelities already eclipse many superconducting chips—the piece underscores how higher fidelity can dramatically shrink the overhead of quantum error correction, a bottleneck that has slowed practical deployments. If Quantinuum meets its 2029 target for faster ion gates, the combination of low errors and competitive speed could reshape industry roadmaps and accelerate the arrival of commercially viable quantum services.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

Quantinuum's Chief Scientist, Harry Buhrman, argues that high physical fidelity in quantum gates is more critical than raw gate speed for achieving fault-tolerant quantum computing. He highlights Quantinuum's trapped-ion architecture as superior to superconducting platforms due to its low error rates, which significantly reduce the resources needed for quantum error correction. While trapped-ion systems currently have slower gate speeds, Quantinuum aims to overcome this limitation by 2029 to make its Apollo system commercially competitive.
