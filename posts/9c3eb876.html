When can local unitaries generate a complete orthogonal basis?

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics November 12, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article settles a long‑standing open problem by proving that any pure state of two qutrits can be extended to a full orthogonal basis using only local unitary operations, establishing universal local encodability (ULE) for the $3\times3$ case and expanding the catalog of dimensions where ULE holds. By turning a heuristic parameter count into a rigorous necessary condition, the authors also identify infinitely many multipartite systems that can never be universally locally encoded, sharpening our understanding of which hardware architectures can support simple, fully local state‑preparation schemes. The operational link to measurement discrimination—showing that non‑ULE exactly corresponds to an advantage for general over projectively simulable measurements—provides a concrete benchmark for quantum sensing and verification protocols that rely on locally generated bases.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

The question of local encodability asks: can a given, multipartite pure quantum state be completed to an orthonormal basis using only local unitary rotations? If any such state in a given system has this property, it is called universally locally encodable (ULE), a feature, which has applications in quantum information theory, engineering and math. Despite its easy formulation, the answer to this question depends nontrivially on the local dimensions of the system: While universal local encodability is known for $2\times 2$, $4\times 4$ and $8\times 8$-dimensional systems, it was so-far only conjectured to hold in $3\times 3$, and non-ULE was recently proved for systems of four and more qubits by Pauwels and Gühne. We resolve the open conjecture by proving universality for two qutrits. Furthermore, we construct (among others) universal local encodings in dimensions $d\times2$ for $d\ge2$, in dimensions $d\times3$ for $d=3,4$ and $d\ge6$, and in every bipartite system with one or two infinite-dimensional subsystems. For finite-dimensional multipartite systems, we turn a heuristic parameter count into a necessary condition for universality, which excludes universal local encodability in infinitely many cases. Finally, we give universal local encodability an operational interpretation by showing that non-ULE is equivalent to whether general measurements yield an advantage over projectively simulable ones in discrimination tasks where unknown, random local unitary rotations have to be detected.
