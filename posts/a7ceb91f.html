Bound magic in multi-qubit systems

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics November 25, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This paper proves that even in standard qubit hardware there exist non‑stabilizer “bound‑magic’’ states from which no pure magic can be distilled, closing a long‑standing gap that was previously only known for odd‑dimensional systems. By formulating a PPT‑like criterion that is stable under tensor products and stabilizer operations, the authors give a concrete way to identify these useless resources and explicitly construct examples for three or more qubits. The result sharpens our understanding of magic‑state distillation limits, warning designers of fault‑tolerant architectures that not all noisy non‑stabilizer states can be salvaged and motivating new protocols to avoid such bound regions.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

Magic captures nonstabilizerness, a resource required for quantum computational speedups. We prove the existence of multi-qubit nonstabilizer states from which no pure magic can be distilled, even with arbitrarily many copies and rounds of stabilizer operations. Such bound magic states have previously been only known to exist in odd Hilbert-space dimensions, where positivity of discrete Wigner functions can be used analogously to the positive partial transpose (PPT) criterion for bound entanglement. We introduce a PPT analogue for magic in multi-qubit systems that is closed under tensor products and stabilizer operations and reduces to the stabilizer polytope for one and two qubits. This enables the proof of multi-qubit bound magic and the explicit construction of bound magic states for $n \geq 3$ qubits.
