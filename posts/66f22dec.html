Resource efficient Sample Based Quantum Simulation through Fragmentation and Virtual Partitioning of QPU

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics October 30, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article demonstrates a practical route to scaling quantum‑chemical simulations on today’s noisy devices by fragmenting the problem and virtually partitioning the QPU, cutting two‑qubit depth by up to half and slashing runtime by more than 80 % while still reaching chemical accuracy. By coupling a “half‑qubit” ansatz with an HCI expansion, the authors show that only a vanishing fraction of the determinant space needs to be sampled, enabling active spaces as large as 104 spin orbitals on 52 qubits—far beyond what prior sample‑based methods could handle. The work directly tackles the twin bottlenecks of circuit depth and classical post‑processing in quantum‑centric supercomputing, suggesting that near‑term hardware can now address chemically relevant systems such as drug‑target complexes.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

Present-day sample-based quantum algorithms within quantum-centric supercomputing workflows require substantial classical post-processing, large circuit depths to produce chemically relevant samples, and long QPU times to execute the many circuits needed for molecules of chemically relevant size. We introduce FMO-QVM-HCI-HSQD, a resource-efficient sample-based framework that breaks a large problem into fragments, runs the fragments in parallel on virtual regions of the QPU, and reduces circuit depth through a half-qubit ansatz whose samples seed an HCI expansion. We demonstrate the algorithm on ibm_marrakesh and ibm_boston for the water trimer and the carmofur_Mpro complex with up to 52 qubits, benchmarking against FMO-CCSD(T), FMO-CASCI and FMO-HCI. For the water trimer (14 spin orbitals for monomers, 28 for dimers), our method reduces the two-qubit depth by 39% compared to FMO-QVM-SQD and the QPU time by 82% compared to sequential FMO-SQD, and accesses 3% of the determinant subspace versus 40% for FMO-SQD, while achieving more accurate energies. For the carmofur_Mpro complex (16 and 32 spin orbitals), it reduces the two-qubit depth by 47% and the QPU time by 20% compared to FMO-QVM-SQD, and accesses 0.007% of the determinant subspace versus 40%, while remaining within chemical accuracy. Extending the carmofur_Mpro active space in the 6-31G basis to 52 and 104 spin orbitals, sampled on 26 and 52 qubits, the total energy deviates from FMO-HCI by only 1.22 mHa. These results show that combining fragmentation, virtual QPU partitioning and half-qubit sampling makes sample-based quantum simulation of chemically relevant systems feasible on current hardware.
