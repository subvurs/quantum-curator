Programmable multipartite entanglement analogues

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics November 24, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article demonstrates a reconfigurable metasurface that can program the number and type of coherently linked degrees of freedom in a classical electromagnetic field, achieving Bell‑like and GHZ‑like states up to five parties—the highest multipartite order yet realized in optics without genuine quantum entanglement. By making multipartiteness itself tunable across polarization, frequency, orbital angular momentum, wavevector direction and envelope modes, the work provides a versatile testbed for probing the structure of complex quantum correlations using lossless, room‑temperature light. It bridges recent advances in programmable photonic metasurfaces with the need for scalable platforms to benchmark multipartite protocols, offering a low‑cost sandbox for algorithm development while reminding readers that the underlying physics remains classical rather than nonlocal.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

Quantum entanglement underpins the quantum information science. Its multipartite forms distribute coherence across several subsystems, but controlling their composition and order is increasingly difficult as more subsystems participate. Classical nonseparable waves provide accessible analogues of these structures, although existing implementations largely fix the participating degrees of freedom through their generating architectures. Here we overcome this constraint by making multipartiteness itself programmable in electromagnetic fields. A spatiotemporal metasurface reconfigures the identity and number of coherently linked state spaces independently across polarization, frequency, orbital angular momentum, wavevector direction, and orthogonal envelope modes. We realize complete Bell-like and Greenberger-Horne-Zeilinger-like families from bipartite to five-partite order and exchange constituents at fixed order. To our knowledge, the five-partite state has the highest multipartite order experimentally realized in classical electromagnetic fields. These results establish the distribution of coherence across physical state spaces as a programmable property of classical waves and provide a platform for studying selected structures of multipartite quantum states without invoking quantum entanglement or nonlocality.
