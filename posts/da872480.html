BMO Demonstrates Quantum Utility in Game-Theoretic Machine Learning Feature Selection on IBM Nighthawk r2

Source: Quantum Computing Report November 20, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article shows a concrete quantum‑utility case by using game‑theoretic machine‑learning on IBM’s Nighthawk r2 to trim feature sets with far fewer samples than classical pipelines, a step toward practical cost savings for data‑intensive finance workloads. It builds on the recent wave of industry pilots that pair variational algorithms with real‑hardware, echoing similar demonstrations from JPMorgan and Volkswagen but adds a novel game‑theory layer that directly targets predictive relevance. If the approach scales to larger portfolios, banks could accelerate model development while reducing classical compute budgets, though broader validation on diverse datasets will be needed before it becomes a production staple.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

BMO Financial Group, in collaboration with DUTH and IBM, has demonstrated a quantum machine learning feature-selection workflow on IBM quantum processors. They applied quantum game theory to identify high-value predictive variables in financial datasets, achieving comparable quality to classical methods with significantly fewer algorithmic samples. This milestone builds on BMO's earlier entry into the IBM Quantum Network and the launch of their AI & Quantum Institute.
