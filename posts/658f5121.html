An entanglement band structure for general multiqubit states

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics October 21, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article extends the classic Majorana representation into a full “entanglement band structure,” showing that the reduced state of any qubit in an arbitrary multiqubit wavefunction sweeps out eigenvalue bands on the Bloch sphere and even exhibits non‑Hermitian exceptional points without any gain or loss. By mapping entanglement invariants to a Riemann‑surface‑like topology, the work provides a new geometric language for classifying multipartite entanglement that could streamline benchmarking of noisy intermediate‑scale quantum (NISQ) devices where state tomography is costly. The five‑qubit result—pinning down an entanglement class up to only finitely many possibilities—suggests a practical route toward compact, hardware‑agnostic descriptors for complex quantum states, though experimental extraction of the band data remains an open challenge.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

Majorana drew a symmetric multiqubit state as points on the Bloch sphere, antipodal to the zeros of its overlap with spin coherent states. Projecting only one qubit of a general state leaves the others in a state that varies with direction. Its entanglement invariants are eigenvalues of a non-Hermitian matrix and form bands over the sphere. Exceptional points appear, without gain or loss, where the entanglement family of the rest changes. Circling one exchanges two bands, so the bands form one Riemann surface. At five qubits it fixes the entanglement class up to finitely many choices.
