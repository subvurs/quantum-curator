New York Deploys $360 Million for Stony Brook and Regional Hubs Supported by $1.37B in Federal Foundry Commitments

Source: Quantum Computing Report October 25, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article highlights a rare convergence of state‑level capital and federal foundry commitments that could accelerate the U.S. quantum supply chain by anchoring hardware development at Stony Brook while simultaneously seeding regional commercialization hubs. By creating open‑access testbeds and startup incubators, New York aims to translate advances in superconducting qubits and photonic chips into marketable services for finance, pharma, and defense—addressing the “valley of death” that has slowed many quantum ventures. The $360 million infusion builds on recent federal initiatives such as the National Quantum Initiative Act, positioning the state as a model for coordinated public‑private ecosystems. However, success will depend on how quickly the hubs can attract industry partners and scale prototypes beyond laboratory demonstrations.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

New York State is investing $360 million in quantum technology, with $300 million for a Quantum Research and Innovation Hub at SUNY Stony Brook and $60 million for regional commercialization hubs. This initiative, supported by $1.37 billion in federal funding, aims to connect the state's hardware and semiconductor assets with enterprise demand in various sectors. The investment will establish open-access test facilities, foster startup incubation, and link regional hardware clusters with commercial end-users.
