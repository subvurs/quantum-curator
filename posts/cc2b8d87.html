Variable-length MDI QKD under source imperfections and local randomization over horizontal links

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics November 17, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article pushes measurement‑device‑independent QKD toward real‑world deployment by rigorously handling source flaws and finite‑size effects with a variable‑length security proof based on the marginal‑constrained entropy accumulation theorem. By showing that secret keys can be distilled from as few as 30 000 pulses—even over asymmetric, 80‑km horizontal links—the work bridges the gap between laboratory demonstrations and the longer distances required for commercial satellite constellations. Summary

Quantum key distribution (QKD) provides security guarantees that hold despite device imperfections and finite-size effects. We address these challenges in measurement-device-independent (MDI) QKD, combining its intrinsic untrusted detection with an imperfection model for Alice's and Bob's sources via fidelity bounds. We establish variable-length general security under the Marginal-constrained Entropy Accumulation Theorem (MEAT), estimating the relevant Rényi entropies through conic optimization. We evaluate our model under a horizontal channel link, reporting secret keys for blocks as small as n~3x10^4 rounds under atmosphere-free conditions, and show that compensating for unequal channel attenuation through appropriately chosen transmitted amplitudes improves performance in asymmetric configurations, enabling communication distances beyond 80 km for commercial satellite systems.
