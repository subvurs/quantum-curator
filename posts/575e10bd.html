Chip-Based Discrete-Modulated Continuous-Variable Quantum Key Distribution with Uncertainty-Principle-Based Composable Security

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics November 13, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article demonstrates that high‑order discrete‑modulated continuous‑variable QKD can be secured with a tight, uncertainty‑principle‑based composable analysis, eliminating the heavy numerical overhead that has hampered practical finite‑size proofs. By streamlining the security evaluation to essentially one‑dimensional searches—and even an analytical bound—the authors enable real‑time key generation at 17 Mbps on a discrete‑component platform and over half a megabit per second on a silicon‑photonic receiver across 50 km of fiber, a clear milestone for integrated telecom‑compatible quantum cryptography. The work bridges the gap between laboratory‑grade CV‑QKD demonstrations and deployable, chip‑scale secure communication systems, though further optimisation will be needed to bring silicon‑based rates up to parity with bulk optics.

Summary

Continuous-variable quantum key distribution offers a promising platform for scalable quantum networks through its compatibility with standard telecommunications technology and photonic integration. High-order discrete modulation facilitates practical implementation while approaching the key-rate performance of ideal Gaussian modulation, but its finite-size composable security analysis entails substantial computational overhead. Here, we develop an uncertainty-principle-based security framework that enables finite-size composable key-rate evaluation against collective attacks for constellations up to 256-QAM. By introducing a heterodyne-measured ancillary mode into the entanglement-based source description, we incorporate additional source correlations into the security analysis that allow the Heisenberg uncertainty principle alone to provide a tight bound for the unknown correlations in a covariance matrix characterizing the whole system. Matrix symmetrization further reduces the key-rate optimization to a one-dimensional search, while subsequent mode reduction yields an analytical lower bound requiring no numerical search. Experimental demonstrations using 256-QAM on discrete-component and silicon-photonic-receiver platforms achieve finite-size composable secret key rates of 17.436 and 0.519 Mbps, respectively, over a 50 km fiber link. This approach facilitates the practical deployment of high-speed, real-time and compact quantum key distribution systems. Beyond quantum key distribution, the principle of tightening uncertainty-constrained bounds via source-mode expansion offers a paradigm for exploring the information-theoretic properties of complex non-Gaussian systems.
