Efficient Iterative Symmetrization algorithm for quantum computing and hadron applications

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics November 13, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article introduces a recursive (anti)symmetrization routine that reduces the gate count for adding or removing particles from O(j³)‑type scaling to O(j log²j + j n₍qb₎), bringing symmetrized state preparation into reach of near‑term devices with limited coherence times. By requiring only a modest number of clean ancilla qubits, the method dovetails with emerging dynamic‑memory architectures and complements recent advances in efficient Dicke‑state generation for quantum chemistry and lattice gauge simulations. Its demonstration on hadron‑physics benchmarks shows that realistic few‑body particle‑number–changing problems could be tackled on upcoming superconducting or trapped‑ion processors, although scaling to truly many‑particle systems will still demand further hardware improvements.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

Efficient algorithms for the (anti)symmetrization of quantum registers are necessary for upcoming few- and many-body physics applications, from atomic to particle-physics scales. We propose a recursive algorithm (anti)symmetrizing the last particle added to memory, each time one is created or destroyed, respect to the rest of already (anti)symmetrized ones. Given a generalized Dicke state (GDS) of $(j-1)$ quantum registers, each assigned to a particle, the algorithm produces another GDS of $j$ registers (of $n_{qb}$ qubits each) using a mere $\mathcal{O}(j\log_2^2 j + j n_{qb})$ $\mathtt{CNOT}$ and single-qubit gates, and $3j+\lceil\log_2j\rceil+\mathcal{O}(\max(n_{qb},\log_2 j))$ clean auxiliary qubits. The (anti)symmetrizing operator constructed for this purpose depends on the quantum numbers of the additional particle being introduced or ``created''. The algorithm improves upon the construction presented in Appendix A of \cite{Galvez-Viruet:2024hry} by almost two powers of $j$ and is on par with the most efficient methods to date, while ideally suited for dynamical memory management with variable particle number. We exemplify its applicability with a small selection of problems from hadron physics which are expected to be addressable with upcoming quantum computers.
