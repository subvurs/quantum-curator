Mathematical foundations of second quantization: an introduction through spin-boson models

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics October 30, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article delivers a rare, mathematically rigorous bridge between the abstract machinery of second quantization and the concrete spin‑boson models that underlie many superconducting qubit architectures, showing exactly how ladder operators, Fock spaces and canonical commutation relations give rise to well‑posed Hamiltonians. By proving self‑adjointness, providing explicit spectra for Jaynes‑Cummings and Rabi‑type systems, and even delivering a quantified rotating‑wave approximation error bound, it equips hardware designers with trustworthy analytical tools for modeling decoherence and engineered dissipation. In an era where quantum processors are increasingly judged by their noise mitigation strategies, such solid foundations help translate theoretical insights into more reliable device simulations and error‑corrected control protocols.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

These lecture notes originate from a one-semester course on the mathematical foundations of second quantization, first given at the Department of Physics of the Friedrich-Alexander-Universität Erlangen-Nürnberg in the Winter Semester 2025/26 to a mixed audience of physics and mathematics students. Their guiding thread is a class of physical models: spin-boson models, describing a two-level system coupled to a boson field. After summarizing the necessary background on unbounded operators, up to the Kato-Rellich and KLMN theorems, and developing direct sums and tensor products of Hilbert spaces and of unbounded operators, we treat single-mode boson fields, constructing the ladder and number operators and characterizing their domains, before moving to Fock spaces, second quantization, bosonic and fermionic annihilation and creation operators, and the canonical (anti)commutation relations. Throughout, spin-boson models provide both the motivation and the test bench for the formalism: we rigorously establish their self-adjointness, solve the pure dephasing and Jaynes-Cummings models explicitly, deriving their spectra and the qubit channels they induce, show how their multimode counterparts display irreversible decoherence and decay, and, in an appendix, prove the rotating-wave approximation for the quantum Rabi model with an explicit error bound. Finally, we use the KLMN theorem to construct spin-boson models with non-normalizable form factors, and briefly discuss stronger ultraviolet divergences and the need for renormalization. Exercises with full solutions are included.
