Rare-earth ions could enable telecom-ready control of interacting qubits

Source: Phys.org Quantum July 29, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article highlights how rare‑earth ion dopants can serve as both long‑lived quantum memories and optical interfaces that operate directly at telecom wavelengths, a combination that has been elusive for solid‑state qubits. By demonstrating coherent interactions between such ions while preserving low‑loss fiber compatibility, the work bridges recent progress in rare‑earth ensemble quantum repeaters with the need for scalable, room‑temperature control hardware. If these results translate to larger arrays, they could simplify the photonic interconnect layer of future quantum processors and networks, though integration challenges and error rates still require careful engineering.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

Quantum technologies are devices and systems that exploit the laws of quantum mechanics and could perform tasks that are difficult or impossible to tackle using their classical counterparts. These technologies process and store information using qubits (i.e., quantum bits), which can exist in a superposition of multiple states simultaneously.
