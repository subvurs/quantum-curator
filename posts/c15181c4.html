Differentiable Digital Twins for Optimization, Calibration, and Characterization of Neutral-Atom- and Ion-based Quantum Computers

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics November 06, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article matters because it delivers the first open‑source “digital twin” that can be differentiated end‑to‑end, letting researchers automatically compute gradients of gate performance for neutral‑atom and trapped‑ion processors. By marrying high‑fidelity physical modeling with quantum optimal control and automatic differentiation, the toolset bridges a gap between hardware characterization and software optimization that has limited fidelity gains in recent experiments on Rydberg arrays and ion chains. The ability to rapidly calibrate and improve gates could accelerate scaling efforts, though its impact will depend on how easily experimental groups can integrate the framework with their existing control stacks.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

Quantum technologies based on individually controlled atoms are among the most promising platforms for scalable quantum computing, owing to their favorable coherence properties and controllability. Nonetheless, achieving reliable and high-fidelity gate operations remains challenging due to the sensitivity of these systems to noise and hardware imperfections. In this work, we introduce a comprehensive software toolset designed to model atomic quantum systems with high precision and simulate qubit gate dynamics. By integrating quantum optimal control and benchmarking techniques with automatic differentiation for efficient and accurate gradient-based optimization, the toolset enables significant improvements in gate performance. We demonstrate its capabilities through detailed case studies involving both neutral atoms and trapped ions, highlighting its effectiveness in enhancing quantum gate fidelities, and providing valuable insights for experimental implementation.
