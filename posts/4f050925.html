Mixed-State Symmetry-Protected Topology and Strong-to-Weak Spontaneous Symmetry Breaking in a Superconducting Qubit Array

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics August 25, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article demonstrates the first experimental observation of mixed‑state symmetry‑protected topological (SPT) order and its transformation under measurement and controlled dephasing in a five‑qubit superconducting array, showing that distinct symmetry sectors can survive decoherence while others are selectively suppressed. By conditioning on measurement outcomes and using nonlinear fidelity correlators, the team reveals a finite‑size signature of strong‑to‑weak spontaneous symmetry breaking—a phenomenon that bridges recent advances in measurement‑based quantum computing and topological error protection. Although limited to a small register, the work establishes superconducting circuits as a versatile platform for probing hidden order and could inform future designs of robust quantum memories or sensors that exploit mixed‑state topology.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

We experimentally investigate how symmetry-protected topological order in a one-dimensional cluster state is transformed by measurement and decoherence in a five-qubit superconducting array. We first characterize the state's nonlocal string order and show that controlled dephasing selectively suppresses one symmetry sector while leaving the other robust, consistent with average symmetry-protected topological order. We then measure one sublattice in a tunable basis and show that the remaining qubits are driven between a long-range-entangled GHZ state and a paramagnetic state. When the measurement record is discarded, the conventional long-range correlator vanishes while a nonlinear fidelity correlator remains finite, providing a finite-size signature of strong-to-weak spontaneous symmetry breaking. These experiments demonstrate how conditioning, averaging, and decoherence reveal distinct manifestations of order encoded in the same underlying cluster state, and establish a superconducting-circuit setting for probing mixed-state symmetry and topology.
