Entanglement detection from moments of the realigned state: application to continuous-variable quantum systems

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics November 12, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article introduces a practical entanglement‑witness that sidesteps full continuous‑variable tomography by measuring second‑order moments of the realigned density operator, an approach that can be implemented with a linear interferometer acting on multiple copies of the state. It builds directly on the recent multicopy techniques used to access partial‑transpose moments (Phys. Rev. Research 8, 033064 2026) and shows that even modest experimental resources suffice to certify entanglement in both Gaussian and highly non‑Gaussian states such as lossy NOON states. By providing a scalable, experimentally friendly tool, the work could accelerate verification of quantum‑optical platforms where full state reconstruction is prohibitive.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

Certifying entanglement is a central problem in quantum information sciences, especially for continuous-variable quantum systems where full state tomography is experimentally demanding. We introduce a family of separability criteria based on the singular moments and weak moments of the operator obtained by applying the realignment map to the density operator. In practice, we find that the criterion based on the second-order moments of the realigned operator is already sufficient to witness the entanglement of a wide range of quantum optical states. The realigned moments can be readily accessed using an optical scheme consisting of a linear interferometer that couples several identical copies of the state, similarly to the multicopy technique used to measure the partial-transpose moments of a state [Phys. Rev. Research 8, 033064 (2026)]. We illustrate the detection capability of the second-order realigned-moment method for both Gaussian and non-Gaussian states, including lossy NOON states.
