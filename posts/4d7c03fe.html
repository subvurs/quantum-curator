Implementation of a 1D Gaussian-modulated CV-QKD under finite-size parameter estimation

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics November 12, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article reports the first fully integrated experimental demonstration of a one‑dimensional Gaussian‑modulated CV‑QKD system using independent lasers and a local‑local oscillator, achieving a privacy‑amplified key rate of 1.421 Mbit/s (asymptotic) and 0.364 Mbit/s when finite‑size effects are included over 12 km of standard fiber. By simplifying the modulation to a single quadrature while still delivering megabit‑per‑second rates, it addresses long‑standing hardware complexity concerns and brings continuous‑variable QKD closer to practical telecom deployment. The results also highlight that finite‑size parameter estimation remains a bottleneck, underscoring the need for larger data blocks or more efficient reconciliation to reach commercial‑grade performance.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

Continuous-variable quantum key distribution (CV-QKD) is a promising technology for quantum-secure communications, offering high secret-key rates and straightforward integration with coherent optical communication systems. Conventional Gaussian-modulated CV-QKD encodes information in both optical-field quadratures, increasing requirements for state preparation, modulation, and calibration, while potentially introducing additional preparation imperfections. Unidimensional (1D) Gaussian modulation offers a simpler alternative by encoding information in only one quadrature, while the conjugate quadrature constrains the protocol's security. However, an integrated experimental 1D implementation combining independent-laser coherent reception with practical reconciliation, finite-size parameter estimation, and privacy amplification remains challenging. Here, we experimentally demonstrate a 1D Gaussian-modulated CV-QKD system over 12~km of standard single-mode fiber using a local local oscillator (LLO) architecture with independent lasers and an integrated offline classical post-processing chain. The implementation includes coherent detection, digital signal processing, information reconciliation, parameter estimation, physicality-constrained security analysis, and privacy amplification. The 1D system achieves a privacy-amplified rate of $1.421$~Mbit/s under the asymptotic security treatment and $0.364$~Mbit/s under the finite-size scenario. These results establish an experimental baseline for the performance--complexity trade-off associated with unidimensional Gaussian modulation and provide an experimental basis for developing lower-complexity CV-QKD transmitters.
