Secure Information Flow in Quantum Programs

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics November 10, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article delivers the first rigorous semantic framework for reasoning about confidentiality in quantum software, extending classical noninterference concepts to handle superposition and entanglement while proving its equivalence to an attacker‑centric simulation view. By introducing “self‑conjugation,” a clever workaround to the no‑cloning obstacle, the authors enable quantum Hoare‑logic proofs of information‑flow security for protocols such as the quantum one‑time pad, error‑correcting codes and anonymous entanglement generation. The work lays essential groundwork for future toolchains that can certify secure quantum programs on cloud platforms, even though practical verification tools are still in early development.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

Information-flow security aims to prevent the leakage of confidential information through program execution. This problem becomes fundamentally subtler for quantum programs, because their states may involve superposition and entanglement, and their executions may involve intricate interactions between classical and quantum variables. This paper develops semantic foundations and verification methods for secure information flow in quantum programs. We formulate two notions of information-flow security. The first is a noninterference-based definition that naturally generalizes its classical counterpart. The second is a simulation-based definition that explicitly models an attacker, providing a clear operational interpretation of security. We prove that these two notions are equivalent. To verify the information-flow security of quantum programs, we develop a proof framework based on quantum Hoare logic. A key challenge is that the standard self-composition technique, which expresses information-flow security in Hoare logic by composing a program with a renamed copy of itself, fails in the quantum setting due to an obstruction analogous to the no-cloning theorem. To overcome this challenge, we introduce self-conjugation, a novel technique that leverages the deferred measurement principle and properties of unitary operations. We demonstrate our framework on examples drawn from the quantum one-time pad, quantum error correction, and anonymous entanglement generation.
