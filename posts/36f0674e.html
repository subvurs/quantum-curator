A Highly Nondegenerate Photon-Pair Source for Detector-Matched Quantum Key Distribution

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics October 30, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article demonstrates a clever way to cut the dominant hardware expense in entanglement‑based QKD by generating highly nondegenerate photon pairs so that only the telecom‑wavelength photon needs an expensive cryogenic detector while the visible partner can be caught with a cheap, high‑efficiency Si‑SPAD. By showing that even an unoptimised source already narrows the cost per secret bit to within 25 % of a conventional dual‑SNSPD link—and that realistic improvements could slash total expense by up to 70 %—the work points toward more economically scalable quantum‑secure networks. The result dovetails with current industry pushes for integrated, low‑cost photonic sources and highlights that the remaining challenge is boosting collection efficiency and brightness before large‑scale deployment.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

In a commercial entanglement-distribution system over telecom fibre, the single-photon detectors are the largest part of the hardware cost. In entanglement-based quantum key distribution (QKD), both photons of each pair must be detected. With two telecom photons, each arm needs either an InGaAs single-photon avalanche diode (SPAD) of low efficiency or a superconducting nanowire single-photon detector (SNSPD) with its own cryostat. We study a highly nondegenerate photon-pair source that needs a telecom detector on one arm only. Two periodically poled KTiOPO$_4$ (ppKTP) crystals pumped at 405~nm emit pairs at 548 and 1550~nm. A Si-SPAD with 82\% efficiency detects the 548~nm photon at the source node, and only the 1550~nm photon travels through fibre. A first, unoptimised module in a polarisation-correlated configuration generates about $1.5\times10^6$ pairs/s per mW of pump power. It collects 13\% of the pairs on the 548~nm arm and 23\% on the 1550~nm arm. In an asymptotic BBM92 model, we compare its links with generic $2\times1550$~nm links that use a bright published source at the same pump power. At 50~km, our link with one SNSPD costs about 25\% more per secret bit than the generic link with one SNSPD. With optimistic target values of 52\% collection and twice the brightness, it costs about 40\% as much as the generic link with two SNSPDs. Our link without a cryostat then costs about 70\% less than the generic link with one SNSPD. If every link runs at its own best pump power, the two-SNSPD link is again the cheapest. The source can also serve clock synchronisation and detector calibration, which need only time-correlated pairs. All key rates assume the entangled configuration of the source, which is under development.
