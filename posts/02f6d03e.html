Security Analysis of Ekert-type QKD Protocol Coming Under Cloning Attacks

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics October 27, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article sharpens our understanding of how entanglement‑based QKD—specifically Ekert’s protocol—holds up when an eavesdropper employs sophisticated asymmetric cloning attacks on one or both photons, a scenario that mirrors realistic side‑channel threats in emerging quantum networks. By mapping out which two‑qubit states still yield a positive Devetak–Winter key rate under such attacks, the work bridges theoretical security proofs with practical hardware constraints and informs the design of more robust entanglement sources for future deployments. The findings also echo recent efforts in device‑independent QKD to certify secrecy despite imperfect devices, though they remind readers that not all entangled states are equally resilient, so careful state engineering remains essential.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

We analyze the security of an Ekert type bipartite key distribution protocol coming under cloning attacks by an untrusted observer. Precisely, an eavesdropper is assumed to intercept and apply an asymmetric, phase-variant cloner on one or both qubits which are transmitted over public channel from an authenticated source to the trusted parties. We partially characterize two-qubit states from the perspective of designing a QKD protocol that can generate a distillable secret key among the trusted users even under such cloning attacks. We rely upon Devetak Winter framework for computing key rate so as to identify states, which when used in the protocol, secret-key can be distilled. Utility of an entangled state, for designing a successful QKD protocol, changes from single-qubit to double-qubits cloning attacks. Interestingly, we can distinguish some useful families of two-qubit entangled states when only one of the transmitted qubits is cloned by a malicious third party.
