Max-Cut QAOA on Non-Hardware-Native Graphs via MinLA Surrogates and Configuration Recovery

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics November 13, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article tackles a key bottleneck for near‑term QAOA implementations by showing how to prune long‑range edges in non‑native MAX‑CUT graphs and then recover lost objective value with lightweight classical post‑processing. By leveraging a Minimum Linear Arrangement ordering to keep only short‑distance two‑qubit gates, the authors dramatically cut SWAP overhead on devices like IBM Kingston while still achieving competitive cuts after signed‑residue or bipartite‑block recovery. The workflow dovetails with recent hardware‑aware compilation and hybrid quantum–classical strategies, offering a practical path to scale QAOA to larger, irregular graphs without demanding deeper circuits. Readers should note that the improvement comes from a clever trade‑off—some quantum information is discarded but efficiently restored classically, making it especially relevant for today’s noisy intermediate‑scale quantum processors.

Summary

For non-hardware-native \textsc{MAX-CUT} instances, the Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm (QAOA) can incur additional two-qubit depth overhead due to SWAP-based routing of interactions between non-adjacent qubits. We propose a hybrid quantum--classical workflow that reduces this depth by selectively removing interactions and subsequently recovers solution quality through classical optimization of the original \textsc{MAX-CUT} objective. We employ a Minimum Linear Arrangement (MinLA)-based vertex ordering to preferentially retain short-range interactions, thereby constructing a two-qubit depth-reduced surrogate QAOA ansatz. To compensate for the information lost through interaction removal, we develop classical \textit{recovery} strategies that refine QPU-sampled bitstrings directly on the original \textsc{MAX-CUT} objective. We develop two recovery strategies: signed-residue-based local search and bipartite-block optimization, where each conditional block problem is solved exactly using an $s$--$t$ minimum cut. Recovery is performed independently on each sampled configuration, ensuring monotonic improvement in cut value without additional QPU executions. We evaluate the workflow on \texttt{ibm\_kingston} for 120 distinct non-hardware-native instances comprising $6$-regular and Erdős--Rényi graphs with 40, 60 and 100 vertices. We verify that the surrogates substantially reduce transpiled two-qubit depth while staying within the prescribed two-qubit depth, and with recovery yield \textit{near-optimal} cuts on the original graphs. These results demonstrate the potential of hardware-aware interaction selection and classical recovery to extend QAOA to graph instances whose full cost Hamiltonians exceed practical circuit-depth budgets.
