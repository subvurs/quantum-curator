Engineered Learning Problems on Noisy Quantum Processors

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics October 30, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article marks one of the few empirical demonstrations that a quantum‑enhanced machine‑learning model—specifically a projected quantum kernel—can outpace all tested classical classifiers on an engineered classification task using up to 45 noisy qubits, and it does so reproducibly across multiple superconducting processors. By showing how error‑mitigation techniques not only improve raw hardware fidelity but also raise the difficulty of the corresponding classical problem, the work highlights a concrete pathway for quantum advantage in near‑term QML despite the artificial nature of the benchmark. The authors caution that state‑of‑the‑art tensor‑network simulations still close much of the gap, underscoring that scaling to realistic data sets will be the next critical hurdle.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

Despite quantum machine learning being an active topic of research, demonstrations of algorithms run on quantum hardware beating classical methods remain rare. In this work, we demonstrate a significant performance gap using a projected quantum kernel for $n=15,30$ and $45$ qubits over classical methods on an engineered classification problem, and show reproducibility of the results over different quantum processor units. We also demonstrate how error mitigation can widen this gap, both by improving hardware performance but also by increasing the classical difficulty, with different techniques contributing unequally to these phenomena. This work provides strong evidence that superior empirical performance of quantum hardware over all classical techniques is an achievable goal in quantum machine learning, albeit for an artificial problem. However, as with any application relying on observables, beating state-of-the-art simulation methods remains the primary challenge, as our matrix product state results here show.
