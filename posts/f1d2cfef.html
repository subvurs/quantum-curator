IDEMIA and KT Deploy Quantum-Resistant SIMs Supporting Dual NIST and KpqC Algorithms for South Korean Military Networks

Source: Quantum Computing Report October 27, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article highlights the first large‑scale rollout of quantum‑resistant SIM cards that embed both NIST‑standardized and Korea‑specific post‑quantum algorithms, turning a ubiquitous mobile component into a hardware root of trust for military communications. By offloading heavyweight key‑exchange operations to the SIM, the deployment demonstrates a practical path toward crypto‑agility that can be mirrored in civilian networks as nations scramble to harden infrastructure before functional quantum computers arrive. It also underscores South Korea’s proactive stance in the global post‑quantum migration race, though the dual‑algorithm approach will need careful interoperability testing to avoid performance bottlenecks and ensure seamless transition when NIST standards are finalized.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

IDEMIA Secure Transactions and KT Corporation are deploying quantum-resistant SIMs in South Korea's military networks, supporting both NIST and Korean Post-Quantum Cryptography (KpqC) algorithms. This initiative, part of South Korea's 2026 Post-Quantum Cryptography Pilot Conversion Project, aims to enhance crypto-agility and secure military communications against future quantum threats. The SIMs act as a hardware root of trust, offloading intensive cryptographic handshakes from host devices and ensuring operational resilience across various military applications.
