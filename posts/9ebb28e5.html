Quantum control of solid state qubits via Reinforcement Learning

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics October 15, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article demonstrates that model‑free reinforcement learning can reliably discover high‑fidelity pulse sequences for indirect control of NV nuclear spins even when the surrounding spin bath is modeled with realistic many‑body noise, a regime where traditional analytic or gradient‑based methods struggle. By benchmarking RL against other black‑box optimizers on sparse, noisy reward signals, the authors show that AI‑driven control can match or exceed state‑of‑the‑art performance without any prior knowledge of the device Hamiltonian—a crucial step toward autonomous calibration of NISQ hardware. The work broadens the recent wave of RL applications from superconducting circuits to solid‑state spin platforms, suggesting a path toward scalable, noise‑robust quantum gate synthesis for emerging quantum sensors and processors.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

The use of reinforcement learning (RL) in the design of quantum control strategies for pulse level control of NISQ era quantum devices is attracting growing interest. Model-free RL, not requiring preexisting knowledge of the physical system, acts as a black-box optimization tool well suited for noisy devices. In this work, we provide a simulation-based analysis of the performance of model-free RL for state preparation and qubit gate design in a challenging setup: indirect control of NV nuclear spins in a spin-bath. In particular, the spin-bath is modeled via the generalized cluster correlation expansion accounting for magnetic impurities and intra-bath interactions. We analyze the performance of the RL agents by performing simulations with minimally observable environments and noisy, sparse rewards as introduced in (Phys. Rev. X 12, 011059), and compare them to other state-of-the-art black-box optimization tools.
