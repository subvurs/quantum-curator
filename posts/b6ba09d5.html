Quantum resources constrain function approximation

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics November 23, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article pinpoints exactly which quantum resources—coherence, imaginarity, entanglement and magic—are needed to faithfully approximate a given function, moving beyond the generic claim that quantum models are universal approximators. By deriving tight bounds for one‑ and two‑qubit circuits and showing how Clifford‑only training can recover uniform approximation with only polynomial depth overhead, it connects directly to current efforts to design hardware‑efficient quantum machine‑learning algorithms for noisy intermediate‑scale devices. The work clarifies when exotic resources such as non‑Clifford “magic” actually boost accuracy, giving practitioners a concrete guide for matching circuit resources to the structure of their target functions.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

Universal function approximation is central to machine learning. Quantum learning models are universal as well, but this does not specify the resources needed to approximate a given function. We study how coherence, imaginarity, entanglement and magic constrain function approximation for a fixed encoding and readout, using the cumulative resource power of the trainable gates. In a one-qubit model with phase encoding, coherence bounds the oscillation of the model function, while imaginarity bounds its odd part. In a two-qubit model, entanglement bounds the distance from product functions. These bounds hold at any encoding depth. For magic, the phase encoding itself supplies non-Clifford operations. On domains bounded away from zero, Clifford trainable gates suffice for uniform approximation. Replacing unrestricted trainable gates by Clifford gates increases the encoding depth at most polynomially in the original depth, the inverse error and the inverse distance of the domain from zero. Magic in the trainable gates can nevertheless improve accuracy at fixed depth. These results show how the resources of a quantum model must match the structure of the target function.
