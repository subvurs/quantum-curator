A receiver-side approach to confirm phase randomization in BB84 time-bin QKD with weak coherent pulses

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics November 19, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article tackles a subtle but critical loophole in time‑bin BB84 QKD by giving the receiver a way to verify that the sender’s weak coherent pulses are truly phase‑randomized, something previously only checked at the source. By embedding the test directly into the detection stage without hardware changes, it dovetails with recent moves toward plug‑and‑play and field‑ready quantum networks where continuous monitoring of security assumptions is essential. The approach strengthens practical defenses against sophisticated coherent attacks, though its effectiveness will still depend on detector performance and integration into existing protocol stacks.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

In quantum cryptography using time-bin BB84 protocols, the relative phase of symbol pulses has to be random to avoid vulnerability to coherent attacks. Typically this phase randomization is checked on the sender side. Here, we propose a protocol for monitoring and confirming phase randomization independently on the receiver side during operation. The proposed method can be implemented on the single-photon level without the need for any physical adaptation of the receiver. This can provide an additional tool for monitoring against possible eavesdropping attempts that affect the phase randomization.
