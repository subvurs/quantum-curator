Classiq Collaborations with Shionogi, KDDI Research, and SoftBank Earn Awards in Japan’s NEDO Quantum Challenge

Source: Quantum Computing Report November 18, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article shows how Classiq’s Qmod platform is moving beyond abstract circuit design into real‑world workloads, delivering concrete resource savings for drug discovery, cryptography and logistics—areas that have long been touted as quantum “killer apps.” By proving that a smaller qubit register can still capture molecular detail, simulating an avalanche effect for hash functions, and trimming drone delivery routes, the collaborations illustrate the growing maturity of algorithm‑hardware co‑design pipelines that bridge academic theory and industry needs. The results also underscore Japan’s strategic push through NEDO to accelerate quantum adoption, though the demonstrations remain simulations rather than runs on fault‑tolerant hardware, so further scaling will be required before commercial impact is realized.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

Classiq announced that three joint research initiatives with Shionogi, KDDI Research, and SoftBank received awards in Japan’s NEDO Quantum Computing Challenge. These projects utilized Classiq’s Qmod and evaluated hardware execution and fault-tolerant resource requirements across pharmaceutical, cryptographic, and logistics workloads. The collaborations demonstrated significant achievements, such as reducing qubit register size for molecular modeling, achieving a simulated avalanche effect for cryptographic hash functions, and shortening drone delivery distances.
