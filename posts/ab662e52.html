Robust Geometric Gates for QCCD Architecture

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics November 04, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article demonstrates that non‑cyclic geometric single‑qubit gates can dramatically suppress the shuttling‑induced noise that has long limited QCCD‑based trapped‑ion processors, delivering gate fidelities well above those of conventional dynamical pulses even when ions are moved repeatedly. By validating the approach on a planar chip trap with up to 33 qubits, the work shows that robust geometric control scales alongside the ion‑shuttle architecture, bringing truly plug‑and‑play quantum primitives within reach for large‑scale ion‑based computers. The result bridges a critical gap between high‑fidelity stationary gates and the flexible connectivity required for fault‑tolerant QCCD systems, although further integration with two‑qubit operations will be needed to complete a full error‑corrected stack.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

The trapped-ion quantum charge-coupled device (QCCD) is a promising route toward scalable quantum computing. However, the frequency offset during shuttling operations introduce significant decoherence and control errors, severely degrading gate fidelity on transported ions. Here, using a room-temperature planar chip trap, we demonstrate a robust and universal single-qubit gate set in the QCCD architecture. Based on a recently proposed path-optimizable noncyclic geometric scheme, we show that dynamic noise is strongly suppressed even when multiple shuttling operations are interleaved with gate execution, achieving substantially higher fidelities than conventional dynamical gates. This robustness is further validated in a multi-ion system containing up to 33 qubits. With inherent robustness and adaptability to varying shuttle distances and ion numbers, our work offers a plug-and-play solution for building robust quantum primitives for the QCCD platform.
