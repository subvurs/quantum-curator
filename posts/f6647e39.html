IBM Connects Two 120-Qubit Processors With 99% State-Transfer Fidelity

Source: The Quantum Insider October 27, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article shows IBM’s first successful modular link between two 120‑qubit superconducting chips, achieving an average state‑transfer fidelity of 99%, which is close to the error‑correction thresholds needed for fault‑tolerant operation. By demonstrating high‑fidelity quantum communication over four cryogenic cables, the work builds on earlier interposer and photonic‑link experiments and points toward a scalable architecture where many smaller processors can be stitched together rather than fabricating ever larger monolithic chips. The result is a practical step toward distributed quantum computers, though further engineering will be required to manage wiring complexity and maintain performance as the number of modules grows.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

Insider Brief IBM researchers connected two quantum processors through four superconducting cables, demonstrating a way to build larger quantum computers from separate chips. The links transferred quantum states with an average fidelity of 99% and supported operations between computing elements on different processors, according to a study posted on the preprint server arXiv.
