500 Hz atom-atom entanglement distribution over metropolitan-scale fibers

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics November 23, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article demonstrates a semi‑deterministic entanglement link that pushes the distribution rate past half a kilohertz across a 20 km fiber span—an order of magnitude faster than any prior single‑ or two‑photon scheme and achieved without active phase stabilization. By combining two 15×15 quantum‑memory arrays, the researchers show how multiplexing can simultaneously deliver high efficiency and robustness, directly addressing the bottleneck that has limited practical quantum repeaters. The result sets a new speed benchmark for metropolitan‑scale quantum networks and brings scalable, fault‑tolerant architectures one step closer to real‑world deployment, though extending the technique to longer distances and integrating it with error‑corrected nodes will be the next challenge.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

Quantum networks and repeaters are among the most promising routes to scale up quantum information systems. Nevertheless, various aspects still require significant improvement to achieve practical realization. Multiplexing can enhance the performance in many fundamental steps, thus emerging as a critical strategy for the realization of quantum repeaters. Currently, a major bottleneck in realizing functional quantum repeaters is heralded entanglement distribution over longdistance elementary links, as neither of the two dominant protocols-single-photon nor two-photon interference-can simultaneously achieve high efficiency and strong robustness. Here, we address this challenge by experimentally realizing a method that unifies the efficiency and robustness. By harnessing two 15 * 15 quantum memory arrays, we generate remote matter-matter entanglement at a rate of 546 Hz over 20 km of fiber without active phase stabilization. This rate exceeds that of two-photon and single-photon schemes by factors of 75,932 and 23, respectively, establishing a new benchmark for entangling speed over metropolitan-scale distances and enabling semi-deterministic entanglement distribution. Inherently compatible with multiplexed quantum repeater architectures, our scheme offers a promising approach towards the realization of future high-performance distributed quantum systems.
