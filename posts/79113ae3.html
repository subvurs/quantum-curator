Sequential vs parallel Strategies for Sharing Quantum Advantage in Communication Tasks

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics November 09, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article pinpoints a sharp boundary between sequential and parallel ways of distributing quantum advantage, showing that while two observers can always mimic a parallel protocol with a sequential one, adding a third observer creates statistics that only true parallel sharing can achieve. By proving the impossibility of parallel sharing for standard 3‑bit random access codes yet demonstrating it for parity‑oblivious RACs, the work clarifies which communication tasks are fundamentally limited by measurement disturbance—a key insight for designing multi‑user quantum networks and distributed sensing platforms. The results dovetail with recent advances in quantum repeaters and clone‑based protocols, suggesting that careful task selection will be essential to harness sustainable quantum resources across many parties.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

Information can be extracted from a quantum system in a sustainable manner, allowing the system to be reused for further information extraction through subsequent interactions. We investigate the possibility of consensus between two experimentally feasible methods, namely, parallel and sequential sharing, that enable multiple observers to extract information encoded in a quantum system. We show that any statistics generated by a parallel sharing strategy can be reproduced by a sequential strategy when only two observers are involved. However, when more than two observers participate in sharing quantum correlations, certain statistics achievable through parallel strategies cannot be reproduced by sequential strategies. Moreover, we demonstrate that sharing quantum advantage through a parallel strategy is impossible for a class of communication tasks involving two decoding measurements, thereby establishing an arbitrarily large gap between the sustainability of quantum advantage under sequential and parallel strategies. This naturally raises the question of whether this impossibility extends to communication tasks involving more than two decoding measurements. We show that, while parallel sharing of quantum advantage is impossible in a 3-bit random access code (RAC), it becomes possible in a 3-bit parity-oblivious RAC, where at most two observers can simultaneously obtain a quantum advantage by performing measurements on two imperfect clones of the initially prepared quantum system. Our findings have potential applications in the development of quantum communication networks and distributed quantum information processing.
