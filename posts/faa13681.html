Quantum Zeno Effect with a Superposed Measurement Schedule in Relational Time

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics November 19, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article demonstrates a concrete way to put the timing of measurements themselves into a quantum superposition, turning the classic Quantum Zeno effect into a probe of relational time that can be realized on today’s superconducting‑qubit platforms. By showing that interference between two distinct measurement schedules depends only on their relative offset—and that a single schedule can even reverse the usual Zeno freezing— the work opens a new experimental handle for testing foundational ideas while also suggesting novel sensing schemes where timing coherence boosts resolution. The authors back up the proposal with realistic gate counts and error budgets, making it a timely bridge between abstract Page‑Wootters concepts and near‑term hardware demonstrations.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

Motivated by the Page-Wootters construction in which time is a quantum clock reading and the global system state is stationary, we propose a quantum circuit that explores the observable consequences of treating measurement timing as a quantum degree of freedom. We place two Zeno measurement schedules of a driven qubit in a coherent superposition using a schedule qubit. We show that, for a fixed drive strength, the interference visibility between the two schedules depends only on the time offset between them, not on the number of intermediate measurements taken. Then we show that, for one schedule-readout outcome, the Zeno trend reverses, predicting a result that complements the standard Zeno probability. We propose a circuit implementation with physical memory qubits and show that measuring the memory register's joint $X$-basis parity distinguishes a coherent schedule from a classical or dephased one. We give the gate count and an error budget for a superconducting-qubit implementation. The Page-Wootters picture motivates this protocol and provides an equivalent stationary representation, although the predictions follow from the standard circuit description without requiring a relational-time interpretation. This is consistent with the Page-Wootters framework recovering standard quantum dynamics through correlations between a clock and the rest of the system.
