Long-distance quantum secure direct communication with Gottesman-Kitaev-Preskill qubits

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics November 09, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article shows for the first time how Gottesman‑Kitaev‑Preskill (GKP) error correction can be harnessed to build a quantum‑secure direct‑communication link that survives more than 1 000 km of fiber, thanks to third‑generation repeater techniques and only modest 23 dB squeezing. By coupling the lattice‑based GKP code with an optimization framework for repeater placement, the work bridges the gap between theoretical error‑corrected channels and practical network design, offering a concrete pathway toward large‑scale quantum‑secure messaging. The results push QSDC from laboratory demos toward real‑world cryptographic infrastructure, although achieving the required squeezing and low‑loss repeaters remains an experimental challenge.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

The Gottesman-Kitaev-Preskill (GKP) code possesses a lattice periodic structure that enables the effective correction of small displacement errors induced by noise in phase space, which is indispensable for quantum computation. The GKP code was also used to construct a long-distance entanglement channel in quantum repeaters and distribute random keys in quantum key distribution (QKD). Using GKP encoding, we propose a quantum secure direct communication (QSDC) protocol that not only transmits the secret message directly, but also helps protect the safety of communication personnel. The exponential attenuation loss of photons can be overcome by the third-generation quantum repeater. Numerical simulation results demonstrate that secure transmission distance exceeding 1000 km is achievable with a 23 dB squeezing level. Furthermore, we establish a cost-based optimization framework to characterize the trade-off among secure communication distance, secrecy capacity, and repeater resources, enabling optimized repeater deployment and providing a systematic design strategy for scalable quantum repeater architectures. The results demonstrate the potential of combining GKP error correction for long-distance QSDC and quantum networks.
