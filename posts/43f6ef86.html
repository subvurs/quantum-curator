QuEra Computing and Lawrence Berkeley National Lab Observe Prethermal Dynamical Phase Transitions on 180 Qubits in Nature Study

Source: Quantum Computing Report November 21, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article shows that a neutral‑atom processor can reliably explore non‑equilibrium many‑body physics on a scale (180 qubits) previously only accessible to classical supercomputers, confirming that prethermal dynamical phase transitions are not just theoretical curiosities but experimentally observable phenomena. By combining the Aquila hardware with state‑of‑the‑art simulations, the collaboration bridges the gap between quantum simulation and traditional computational methods, echoing recent advances from trapped‑ion and superconducting platforms in probing Floquet dynamics. Summary

Researchers observed prethermal dynamical phase transitions on up to 180 qubits using QuEra's Aquila quantum processor. The study, published in Nature, details three phenomena: Floquet-like prethermal steady states, resonant melting, and a first-order dynamical phase transition in 2D. This work, a collaboration between QuEra, Lawrence Berkeley National Lab, Harvard, and Boston University, utilized both quantum hardware and classical supercomputer simulations.
