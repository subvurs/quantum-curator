Success Probability of Block-Encoding Methods

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics November 16, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article spotlights a hidden cost in many block‑encoding algorithms—namely the probability of successful postselection—and shows how it can dominate the shot budget for realistic Hamiltonians such as the Hubbard model. By deriving concrete bounds for LCU, qubitization and QSVT and demonstrating that favorable Chebyshev degrees or tight polynomial normalizations can dramatically improve success rates, the work gives practitioners a practical knob to tune when designing quantum‑simulation pipelines. Including these probabilities in resource estimates will make future claims about algorithmic speedups far more credible, especially as hardware moves toward the tens‑of‑qubits regime where every shot counts.

— Mark Eatherly

Summary

Block-encoding methods apply nonunitary functions $f(\widetilde H)$ of a normalized Hermitian operator on a quantum computer, but only upon a successful postselection, which introduces a shot overhead rarely included in resource estimates. We analyze the success probability $p^{(f)}_{\mathrm{success}}=\sum_n w_n|f(\tilde E_n)|^2$ of three constructions, namely linear combination of unitaries (LCU), qubitization and quantum singular value transformation (QSVT), where $w_n$ are the weights of the input state on the eigenstates of $\widetilde H$. Using the one-dimensional Hubbard model with up to 14 sites (35 qubits), we show that for LCU, the success probability is bounded by the squared ratio between the spectral norm and the $1$-norm of the Pauli decomposition, and that for a Hartree--Fock input it plateaus with system size while the ground-state overlap keeps decreasing. For qubitization, it oscillates with the Chebyshev degree, and favorable degrees can be identified from a few dominant spectral components or predicted from shallow-degree data. For QSVT, the polynomial normalization constant directly sets the success probability, and a tight certified bound balances it against phase-finding robustness. An application to quantum Krylov methods shows a nonuniform shot overhead across Krylov moments. The success probability should therefore be part of the resource estimation of block-encoding-based algorithms.
