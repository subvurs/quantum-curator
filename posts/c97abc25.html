Resilience of Entangling Gates to Spatiotemporally Correlated Noise

Source: arXiv Quantum Physics November 23, 2026 Curated by Mark Eatherly

Curator's Take

AI Commentary

This article shows that the choice of two‑qubit entangling gate can dramatically affect a processor’s tolerance to the spatially and temporally correlated noise that becomes dominant as devices scale up, identifying CZ and √iSWAP as intrinsically more robust than SWAP in semiconductor spin qubits. By deriving a general filter‑function formalism that maps measured noise spectra onto gate‑specific error budgets, the work provides a practical design rule for hardware engineers seeking to optimise error rates without additional control overhead. — Mark Eatherly

Summary

As quantum processors scale, spatially and temporally correlated noise is emerging as a limiting factor in the fidelity of multiqubit gates. Here we show that this makes the choice of entangling operation a first-order design decision: for two-qubit gates in semiconductor spin qubits, the CZ and $\sqrt{\mathrm{iSWAP}}$ gates exhibit a pronounced resilience to correlated noise relative to the SWAP gate, even though correlated fluctuations of the entangling interaction can degrade gate performance to a level comparable to that of local noise. We trace this hierarchy to the redistribution of noise-sensitive channels away from zero frequency and show it to hold for noise spectra dominated by low-frequency weight, including $1/f$ and Lorentzian. These results follow from a general expression, derived here, for the average gate infidelity under arbitrary spatiotemporal noise correlations, valid for generic multiqubit systems and system-environment coupling structures. The infidelity takes the form of a spectral overlap between the noise correlation matrix and a gate-dependent filter function, in which the coherent dynamics of the gate itself shapes the spectral window through which correlated fluctuations enter. The framework converts measured noise correlations into quantitative error budgets and provides a concrete design principle for selecting entangling operations in scalable quantum architectures.
