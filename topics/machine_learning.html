Distributed Quantum-Centric Supercomputing with Multi-Layered Generative Learning for Intermolecular Interactions

Quantum-centric supercomputing (QCSC) approaches, such as sample-based quantum diagonalization (SQD), combine quantum processors with high-performance computers (HPC), offering a promising route toward solving challenging electronic structure problems. However, implementation of QCSC approaches to l...

This article shows how a distributed quantum‑centric supercomputing framework can break large electronic‑structure problems into smaller fragments that fit on near‑term quantum processors, dramatically cutting qubit counts, circuit depth and sampling cost while still capturing inter‑fragment correlations through a clever two‑step aggregation scheme. By pairing the physics‑informed generative SQD solver with an additional generative model to identify the most important sectors of Hilbert space, the authors achieve up to a 95 % reduction in diagonalization size for water and formamide dimers—an advance that dovetails with recent fragment‑based VQE and quantum embedding efforts. If the approach scales to bigger molecules, it could make high‑accuracy quantum chemistry simulations feasible on today’s hybrid HPC‑quantum platforms, though the overall workflow still relies on substantial classical post‑processing and careful error mitigation.
